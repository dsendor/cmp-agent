**Review outline:**

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Question addressed.** The paper tackles a central open question in moiré transport: are the diverse ρ ∼ T^α behaviours of twisted bilayer graphene (TBG) governed by electron–phonon or electron–electron scattering? This includes the near-Planckian linear-in-T regime of magic-angle TBG (MATBG) and the T² regime at larger angles. The competing literature includes phonon-limited models (Das Sarma/Wu, Polshyn et al.), umklapp (Ishizuka–Levitov) and strange-metal/quantum-critical scenarios (Cao, Jaoui).
- **Experimental idea.** Continuous-wave 0.14 THz excitation raises the electron temperature T_e while the lattice is held at T_L = 2 K. Δρ_THz is read out by dual-modulation lock-in detection. The aim is to separate T_e- from T_L-controlled contributions. The concept is elegant and in principle well suited to graphene systems with weak electron–phonon coupling.
- **Key claims.**
  - (i) Giant positive photoresistance, up to 70% in MATBG, persists where ρ ∝ T.
  - (ii) Positive Δρ_THz occurs for all twist angles away from charge neutrality (CNP), ν = ±4 and ν ≈ 2.
  - (iii) Robust T² resistivity at 1.58° persists at ν ≈ ±0.1 and ν ≈ ±3.8, outside the kinematic window for umklapp.
  - (iv) This is attributed to normal inter-pocket e–e collisions enabled by trigonal warping and broken Galilean invariance, quantified by Eq. (1).
- **Novelty relative to prior work.**
  - Hot-electron photoresistance in TBG has been reported before (Hubmann et al., 2D Mater. 2022; PRM 2022). The new elements are:
    - extension to the magic angle and to several twist angles;
    - an explicit use of the method to argue against phonon-limited linear-T resistivity;
    - the umklapp-free T² analysis.
  - Connecting TBG to the "T² without umklapp" puzzle in SrTiO₃, Bi₂O₂Se and HgTe is timely and broadly interesting.
- **Potential impact.** If the central inference holds quantitatively, it would strongly constrain phonon-based theories of MATBG transport. However, the step from "Δρ_THz > 0" to "e–e interactions dominate the conductivity" is currently not established with the rigour that such a claim requires (see Section 3).

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Clear, conceptually clean experiment.** The data show large, reproducible effects across several devices: 2°, 1.58°, MATBG1 (1.05°) and MATBG2 (1.04°).
- **Physically sensible sign structure.** Negative Δρ_THz at the CNP, at ν = ±4 and near ν ≈ 2 is consistent with thermally activated or insulating behaviour. Positive Δρ_THz appears in the metallic regions. This makes Δρ_THz a useful "metallicity" diagnostic.
- **Expected T_L dependence.** Δρ_THz decays with increasing T_L (Fig. 2e), consistent with faster electron cooling and with the hot-electron picture.
- **Clean umklapp-threshold analysis.** The kinematic analysis uses Fermi-surface geometry within the mini-Brillouin zone (Fig. 3d–f) and Hall density. It convincingly shows that ν ≈ ±0.1 lies outside the intervalley-umklapp window, while still showing an enhanced T² prefactor inside the window.
- **High device quality.** Metallic behaviour down to ~10¹¹ cm⁻² in the 1.58° device gives access to a regime rarely studied.
- **Microscopic, testable theory.** Eq. (1)–(6) offer a microscopic proposal whose order of magnitude (A ~ 0.005–0.5 Ω/K²) brackets the measured A ~ 0.1 Ω/K².
- **Broad relevance.** The work links twistronics with the general problem of current relaxation in non-Galilean-invariant Fermi liquids, which interests a broad readership.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**3.1 The central inference "positive Δρ_THz ⇒ electron–electron interactions dominate the resistivity" is not logically established.**
- **Δρ_THz > 0 only shows that ρ depends on T_e, not that e–e collisions relax current.** Several T_e-only mechanisms that do not involve current-relaxing e–e scattering would also give positive photoresistance:
  - **Temperature-dependent screening of charged impurities.** This gives a linear-in-T correction in 2D and is a well-known source of ρ ∝ T at low density (Das Sarma–Hwang). It is especially relevant for reduced v_F, small T_F and graphite-gate-limited screening.
  - **Thermal smearing of an energy-dependent impurity scattering time or density of states.** This matters near van Hove singularities (vHs) and band edges, where T_e/T_F is not small. At ν ≈ 0.1 at 1.58°, T_F/2 is reached within the measured range (Fig. 3b).
  - **Hydrodynamic or viscous corrections.** These change the effective resistance of a finite-width channel with T_e (cf. the authors' own Ref. 50).
  - **Requested analysis.** The manuscript must estimate each of these quantitatively for the relevant ν and θ before attributing Δρ_THz to e–e current relaxation.
- **Electron–phonon scattering can itself depend on T_e in the relevant regime.**
  - In TBG the Bloch–Grüneisen temperature T_BG = 2ħv_s k_F/k_B is only a few kelvin at the densities studied. With T_L = 2 K ≲ T_BG and T_e up to ~17 K, spontaneous phonon emission by hot electrons need not be negligible.
  - The enhanced e–ph coupling invoked by phonon theories of MATBG (∝ 1/v_F², umklapp-assisted, "gauge phonons") would amplify this.
  - Hot-phonon bottlenecks in hBN-encapsulated stacks under CW driving could raise the effective phonon temperature above the cryostat T_L.
  - The statement that this contribution is "negligible" is supported only by the monolayer graphene (MLG) control.
- **The MLG control is not a valid null test for TBG.** MLG differs in several ways that would independently suppress Δρ_THz:
  - much smaller e–ph coupling and dρ/dT at 2–17 K;
  - a different absorbed power, because impedance matching to the antenna depends on sheet resistance;
  - different cooling channels;
  - a different T_F.
  - No T_e calibration is shown for MLG at B = 0. A vanishing Δρ_THz there may mean little heating rather than absent e–ph sensitivity.
  - The argument also cuts the other way: the stronger e–ph coupling in TBG, which is the crux of the phonon scenario, is exactly what the MLG control cannot test.

**3.2 Lack of quantitative, independent electron thermometry and of a quantitative decomposition of dρ/dT.**
- **T_e calibration is indirect and potentially circular.**
  - T_e ("up to 15 K above the lattice") is estimated partly by comparing Δρ_THz with equilibrium ρ(T). That comparison presupposes ρ = ρ(T_e) only, which is the very hypothesis under test.
  - The second method, Shubnikov–de Haas (SdH) damping, is performed at finite B. There, absorption and cooling differ (cyclotron and Bernstein resonances), possibly in a different device or at different ν; the main text does not show this.
  - Without an independent T_e (e.g., Johnson-noise thermometry), the fraction of the equilibrium slope attributable to T_e versus T_L cannot be extracted.
- **No quantitative test in the linear-T regime.**
  - The decisive quantity is r(ν) = (∂ρ/∂T_e)|_{T_L} / (dρ/dT)_eq. Near-unity r would support the claim; r ≪ 1 would leave room for phonons.
  - The manuscript shows only that Δρ_THz is "positive" at ν = 1.7. Simple arithmetic gives dρ/dT = 92 Ω/K × ΔT_e ≈ 15 K ≈ 1.4 kΩ on ρ ≈ 6 kΩ, i.e. ≈ 23%.
  - This is not obviously consistent with the headline 70%, which is shown only in the Supplementary Information. The densities, temperatures and devices at which 70% is reached must be specified and reconciled.
- **Lattice and bolometric heating and the power dependence are not addressed.**
  - At 44.4 Hz modulation, slow heating of the chip or substrate would follow the modulation. No modulation-frequency dependence, on-chip lattice thermometry or absorbed-power estimate is given.
  - The sublinear saturation of Δρ_THz(P) (Fig. 2d) is not modelled. A cooling-power law P_abs ∝ T_e^δ − T_L^δ would both test the hot-electron picture and yield the e–ph coupling constant. That constant could then be checked for consistency with a phonon contribution to ρ(T).
  - Spatial nonuniformity of heating also matters, because coupling occurs through antenna-connected source/drain contacts. Hot spots near contacts and contact-resistance photoresponse are not discussed, and the probe geometry for Δρ_THz (two- versus four-terminal) is unclear.

**3.3 The theoretical explanation of umklapp-free T² resistivity is incomplete and only weakly tested.**
- **Conceptual issue: is the dc limit of the optical conductivity the dc resistivity?**
  - Eq. (1) is obtained by taking ω → 0 of the optical-conductivity result of Sharma, Principi & Maslov.
  - In a clean system, normal collisions conserve total momentum. Unless the current is fully decoupled from momentum, a Drude weight survives and the dc conductivity is infinite even without Galilean invariance.
  - Hence a finite e–e contribution to dc ρ generally requires disorder and violates Matthiessen's rule (cf. Pal, Yudson & Maslov, Ref. 5). The resulting T² prefactor and its validity conditions (τ_ee versus τ_imp ordering) must be derived explicitly.
  - Without this, the order-of-magnitude agreement is not meaningful.
- **Weak quantitative discrimination.**
  - Λ spans two orders of magnitude (0.01–1) depending on the screening model. The "best correspondence Λ ≈ 0.2–0.3" is effectively a fit parameter.
  - No dependence on ν, θ or gate distance is predicted and compared. Yet five or more devices (θ = 1.6–3°) and a range of ν are available.
  - The warping amplitude ∝ k_F a is small exactly where the anomaly is claimed (ν ≈ 0.1). The nearly circular pockets in Fig. 3e raise the question of whether warping is large enough there.
  - The paper should show A(ν) from experiment against Eq. (1) with a warping amplitude extracted from a continuum-model band structure at 1.58°.
- **Data-analysis concerns for the T² claim.**
  - Fig. 3b spans 10–100 K, reaching ~T_F/2, where the low-T expansion in Eq. (1) is not valid. Non-degeneracy and thermally excited minority carriers (exp(−E_F/k_BT) ≈ 0.1 at 100 K for E_F ≈ 20 meV) undercut the statement that the system "remains unipolar at all measured T".
  - Near ν = 3.8, thermal activation across the ν = 4 superlattice gap could produce electron–hole friction or drag. The gap size is not given.
  - Exponents extracted from log(ρ − ρ₀) depend sensitively on the choice of ρ₀. A local-exponent plot d ln(ρ − ρ₀)/d ln T versus T and ν is needed.
  - The umklapp window is quoted as "−3.5 < ν < 0.5 in a hole-side band", which is internally inconsistent (presumably −3.5 < ν < −0.5). The thresholds k_F > g/4√3 and k_F > g/4 assume isotropic pockets despite the warping invoked later.
  - The text implies that Fig. 3b,c and the photoresistance data come from the same 1.58° device, but Δρ_THz for that device at ν ≈ 0.1 is not shown.

**3.4 Overreach of conclusions relative to evidence, and presentation deficiencies.**
- **The magic-angle conclusion is not backed by a mechanism.**
  - The paper states that the linear-T resistivity "often misattributed to e–ph scattering" is interaction-limited and claims to "unify" magic-angle and higher-angle transport.
  - However, it offers no mechanism for linear-in-T e–e resistivity. The inter-pocket theory gives T², and umklapp also gives T² in a Fermi liquid.
  - Whether the linear-T regime is a non-Fermi liquid, a renormalized Fermi liquid with T ≳ T_F, or T-dependent screening is left open. The "unification" is therefore rhetorical.
- **Novelty relative to prior hot-electron studies of TBG is understated.**
  - Hubmann et al. (Refs. 36–37) already used THz/IR photoresistance and photoconductance in TBG near the magic angle, including saturation with intensity.
  - The "general framework" claim also needs positioning relative to established hot-electron thermometry in graphene (noise thermometry, photothermoelectric methods).
- **Presentation and reproducibility.**
  - Figure references in the main text are mismatched: "Fig. 1a shows transport data" should be Fig. 1c, and "Fig. 1b" for THz illumination should be Fig. 1d.
  - The central 70% result and all T_e calibrations are relegated to the Supplementary Information.
  - There is no device table (θ and its inhomogeneity, mobility, residual ρ₀, gate dielectric thickness, channel geometry).
  - Typographical issues ("previosuly", "Galilean invariance in Dirac bands via normal intervalley scattering" versus "interpocket") and loose terminology (intervalley versus inter-pocket versus inter-layer) obscure which collision process is meant.
  - The Methods give the screening-model integrals but omit the band-structure input for Δv_TW.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Establish independent electron thermometry and a quantitative T_e/T_L decomposition.**
   - Measure T_e directly, for example by Johnson-noise thermometry at B = 0, or by SdH damping in the same device at the same ν, extrapolated to B → 0.
   - Plot Δρ_THz(T_e) at T_L = 2 K overlaid on ρ_eq(T) − ρ_eq(2 K).
   - Report the ratio r(ν, θ) = (∂ρ/∂T_e)_{T_L} / (dρ/dT)_eq across the linear-T and T² regimes, including MATBG at ν ≈ 1.7.
   - Repeat at several T_L to map ρ(T_e, T_L) and extract ∂ρ/∂T_L separately.
   - Reconcile explicitly the 70% figure with ΔT_e ≈ 15 K and the measured slopes.
2. **Exclude alternative T_e-dependent and heating mechanisms.**
   - Compute the T_e-dependence expected from temperature-dependent impurity screening and energy-dependent scattering using the measured ρ₀, n and v_F.
   - Estimate hot-electron e–ph (spontaneous emission) contributions with TBG-specific coupling and T_BG.
   - Fit the power dependence with a cooling law to extract the e–ph coupling, and test self-consistency: is this coupling compatible with a phonon-limited 92 Ω/K slope?
   - Rule out lattice or substrate heating via modulation-frequency dependence, an on-chip lattice thermometer, or comparison with a non-absorbing reference.
   - Clarify the four-terminal configuration and exclude contact photoresponse.
   - Replace or supplement the MLG control with a T_e-calibrated control of comparable sheet resistance, ideally a large-angle TBG (> 5°) or a Bernal bilayer.
3. **Put the inter-pocket theory on a firmer and testable footing.**
   - Derive the dc resistivity within a Boltzmann framework that includes impurity scattering and states the validity regime (τ_ee versus τ_imp, T ≪ T_F).
   - Compute the trigonal-warping amplitude at 1.58° (and the other angles) from a continuum model.
   - Fix the screening using the actual gate and hBN thicknesses of each device, so that Λ is no longer a free parameter.
   - Compare the predicted A(ν, θ) with the measured prefactors across all 1.6–3° devices.
   - Present local-exponent analyses, restrict T² fits to T ≪ T_F, and quantify minority-carrier and gap-activation contributions near ν = ±0.1 and ±3.8.
   - Include Δρ_THz data for the 1.58° device at these fillings so that the hot-electron and T² arguments refer to the same sample.
4. **Calibrate claims and improve presentation.**
   - Temper the statements about MATBG linear-T resistivity to what the data quantitatively show, or supply a mechanism (or at least discuss candidates) for interaction-limited linear-T resistivity.
   - Articulate clearly the advance over Hubmann et al. and Jaoui et al.
   - Move the 70% data and the T_e calibration into the main figures.
   - Add a device table (θ, homogeneity, mobility, dimensions, dielectric thickness).
   - Correct the figure-reference errors, the umklapp-window typo and inconsistent terminology.
   - Specify the absorbed power and polarization and antenna coupling, so that other groups can reproduce the method as a "general framework".
