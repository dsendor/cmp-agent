**A. Must address**

**1. Positive photoresistance does not by itself show current-relaxing e–e scattering.** The paper's central inference is "Δρ_THz > 0 at fixed T_L ⇒ electron–electron interactions limit the conductivity." A positive Δρ_THz only shows that ρ depends on T_e. Several T_e-dependent mechanisms that involve no e–e momentum or current relaxation would also give Δρ_THz ≠ 0, and none is discussed:

- **Temperature-dependent screening of charged impurities** (Das Sarma–Hwang type). In dilute 2D systems this gives a substantial, often linear-in-T correction controlled by T_e/T_F. It is especially relevant given the reduced v_F, small T_F and graphite-gate-limited screening.
- **Sommerfeld-type thermal smearing of an energy-dependent σ(E), impurity scattering time or density of states.** This includes the T-induced chemical-potential shift at fixed n. It is significant near van Hove singularities and flat-band edges even for purely elastic scattering. Here T_e/T_F is not small: at ν ≈ 0.1 at 1.58°, T_F/2 is reached within the measured range (Fig. 3b).
- **T_e-driven isospin reordering in MATBG near ν ≈ 1.7–2** (Pomeranchuk effect and cascade transitions; Saito et al., Rozen et al.). This changes ρ through entropy rather than scattering. The sign change of Δρ_THz near ν ≈ 2 shows that correlated-state physics is active in these devices.
- **Hydrodynamic, viscous or Gurzhi corrections** in finite-width clean channels (cf. the authors' own Ref. 50). These can give Δρ of either sign.

The authors should estimate each contribution quantitatively for the relevant ν and θ, using the measured ρ₀, n and v_F. For example, they could compare with Das Sarma-type ρ(T/T_F) expressions and do a convolution analysis of the measured low-T ρ(n). Only then can Δρ_THz be attributed to e–e current relaxation.

**2. Hot-electron e–ph scattering in TBG is not excluded, and the monolayer control cannot test it.** The claim that phonon contributions are "negligible" rests only on the vanishing Δρ_THz in doped monolayer graphene at B = 0. In TBG the Bloch–Grüneisen temperature T_BG = 2ħv_s k_F/k_B is only a few K to ~20 K at the relevant densities (e.g. ~20 K at ν = 0.1 for 1.58°). With T_L = 2 K and T_e up to ~17 K, the system is in the regime T_L < T_BG < T_e. There, spontaneous phonon emission by hot electrons gives a momentum-relaxation rate that depends explicitly on T_e, not only on T_L. Several features of TBG would amplify this:

- the enhanced e–ph coupling central to phonon theories of MATBG (∝ 1/v_F², umklapp-assisted, gauge phonons and phasons);
- the fast umklapp-assisted e–ph cooling the authors themselves cite (Ref. 34);
- possible hot-phonon bottlenecks in hBN-encapsulated stacks under CW driving.

MLG is not a valid null test, for several reasons:

- Its v_F is ~3–10× larger, and its T_BG, coupling, cooling channels and T_F differ.
- At 2–17 K its ρ(T) is disorder-dominated and nearly flat, so a small Δρ_THz follows from small dρ/dT regardless of mechanism.
- Its absorbed power differs, because impedance matching to the antenna depends on sheet resistance.
- No T_e calibration is shown for MLG at B = 0 (Fig. 2f shows SdHO only for MLG at finite field). A vanishing response may simply mean little heating.

The stronger e–ph coupling of TBG, which is the crux of the phonon scenario, is exactly what MLG cannot test. The authors should provide a TBG-specific calculation of ρ_eph(T_e, T_L = 2 K) with realistic coupling (including umklapp-assisted and gauge/phason channels), sound velocities and k_F. They should also replace or supplement the MLG control with a T_e-calibrated control of comparable sheet resistance and comparable, known phonon-dominated dρ/dT, e.g. large-angle TBG (> 5°) or Bernal bilayer.

**3. Electron thermometry is circular, and there is no quantitative T_e/T_L decomposition.** The abstract promises a "direct separation" of e–e and e–ph contributions, but the data are qualitative.

- **Circular calibration.** T_e ("up to 15 K above the lattice") is inferred partly by matching Δρ_THz to equilibrium ρ(T). That presupposes ρ = ρ(T_e) only, which is the hypothesis under test.
- **Unclear independent check.** The second method, SdH damping, is in the SI and done at finite B, where absorption and cooling differ (cyclotron and Bernstein resonances). It is unclear whether it used the same TBG devices, fillings and power range.
- **No n-resolved T_e map.** Absorbed power varies with σ(n), and cooling varies with n and θ. Without such a map, the sign-resolved maps (Figs. 2a, b, e) cannot be converted into ∂ρ/∂T_e.
- **Decisive ratio not reported.** The key quantity is r(ν, θ) = (∂ρ/∂T_e)|_{T_L} / (dρ/dT)_eq. Near-unity r would support the claim; r ≪ 1 leaves room for phonons. The paper only states that Δρ_THz is "positive" at ν = 1.7 in MATBG.
- **The 70% headline is unreconciled.** At ν = 1.7, dρ/dT = 92 Ω/K × ΔT_e ≈ 15 K gives ≈ 1.4 kΩ on ρ ≈ 6 kΩ, i.e. ≈ 23%. This is not obviously consistent with the 70% shown only in the SI. A large Δρ/ρ is also not the same as showing that 100% of dρ/dT is electronic.

The authors should:

- measure T_e independently on the TBG devices at the same fillings and powers, e.g. Johnson-noise thermometry at B = 0, SdHO or weak-localization damping extrapolated to B → 0, or, in MATBG, the superconducting transition or critical current as an internal thermometer;
- plot Δρ_THz(T_e) at T_L = 2 K against ρ_eq(T) − ρ_eq(2 K), or Δρ_THz/ΔT_e against dρ/dT, for each filling;
- report r(ν, θ) across the linear-T and T² regimes;
- specify the densities, temperatures and devices at which 70% is reached and reconcile it with ΔT_e and the measured slopes.

**4. The "cold lattice" premise is asserted, not demonstrated.** CW illumination deposits power not only in the TBG but also in the graphite back gate, the Ti/Au contacts, the bow-tie/triangular antenna and the hBN. Graphite and metal absorb THz efficiently. At 44.4 Hz modulation, slow heating of the local phonon bath, chip or substrate would follow the modulation, and a rise in T_L would mimic an e–ph-driven Δρ. P_max and the absorbed power are not given in absolute units, and no energy-balance estimate is provided. The authors should:

- report the absolute incident and estimated absorbed power;
- give an energy balance (absorbed power, e–ph cooling power, T_L rise through the hBN/graphite stack);
- show the modulation-frequency dependence or a time-resolved check;
- use an on-chip lattice thermometer, or show that T_L-sensitive quantities do not shift under illumination, or compare with a non-absorbing reference.

**5. The linear-in-T conclusion is extrapolated beyond the temperature window actually probed.** Photoresistance is measured essentially only at T_L ≈ 2 K, and Δρ_THz vanishes by T_L ~ 40–60 K (Fig. 2e). The linear-in-T regime and the phonon theories (Refs. 10, 17–25) concern T up to ~50 K and above. In those theories, the linear-T phonon resistivity emerges above an equipartition/BG scale. If that scale is comparable to the probed T_e range, the experiment tests a regime where the phonon contribution is expected to be small anyway. The data therefore cannot support the statement that linear-T resistivity "often misattributed to e–ph scattering" is interaction-dominated over its full range. The authors should:

- repeat the measurement at several T_L (e.g. 5, 10, 20, 30 K) with small ΔT_e, mapping ρ(T_e, T_L) and extracting ∂ρ/∂T_L separately;
- restrict the abstract and conclusions to the temperature range actually probed.

**6. The THz diagnostic is not applied to the key device and fillings for the T² claim.** Fig. 3 contains only equilibrium ρ(T). No Δρ_THz is shown for the 1.58° device at ν ≈ ±0.1 or ≈ 3.8, which are precisely the fillings where the anomalous T² is claimed to be electronic. The link "T² below the umklapp threshold ⇒ T_e-governed e–e process" is the main bridge between Figs. 2 and 3, yet it is asserted rather than demonstrated. The inset of Fig. 2a even shows negative Δρ near the CNP of the 2° device, which raises the question of the sign at ν = 0.1. The text also implies that Fig. 3b, c and the photoresistance data come from the same device, which should be clarified. The authors should measure Δρ_THz, with its power and T_L dependence, at these fillings in the 1.58° device and the other high-angle devices, and show that the inferred T_e dependence reproduces the T² coefficient.

**7. The dc limit of the e–e resistivity is not derived, so the theory–experiment agreement is not yet meaningful.** Eq. (1) takes ω → 0 of the Sharma–Principi–Maslov optical-conductivity result. In a clean system, normal collisions conserve total momentum. Unless the current is fully decoupled from momentum, a Drude weight survives and dc conductivity is infinite even without Galilean invariance, because the current component parallel to momentum is protected. A finite e–e contribution to dc ρ therefore generally requires disorder and violates Matthiessen's rule (cf. Pal, Yudson & Maslov, Ref. 5). The result changes between the τ_ee ≫ τ_imp and τ_ee ≪ τ_imp regimes. The authors should:

- derive the dc resistivity within a Boltzmann framework including impurity scattering;
- state the validity regime (τ_ee vs τ_imp, T ≪ T_F) and give estimates of τ_ee and τ_imp;
- explain how the predicted "saturation" enters the observed temperature window.

**B. Should address**

**8. The T² exponent extraction is fragile and extends outside the Fermi-liquid regime.** Fig. 3b, c cover only about one decade (≈ 10–100 K) after subtracting a chosen ρ₀, and the apparent exponent is highly sensitive to that choice. The fits reach T ≈ T_F/2 (marked in the figure itself; at ν = 0.1, n ~ 1.5×10¹¹ cm⁻², E_F ~ 15–20 meV, T_F ~ 150–230 K), where the low-T expansion behind Eq. (1) is not valid. Two other functional forms can mimic T² over exactly this window:

- a crossover from Bloch–Grüneisen e–ph behaviour (T⁴ or higher below T_BG ≈ 10–20 K) to equipartition (∝ T);
- thermally smeared umklapp just below threshold, which gives activated contributions ~exp(−Δ/T) with Δ ~ 20 meV.

The authors should:

- show local exponents d ln(ρ − ρ₀)/d ln T versus T and ν, and dρ/d(T²) versus T;
- report fitted exponents with uncertainties and their sensitivity to ρ₀;
- restrict T² fits to T ≪ T_F;
- compare against activated-umklapp and BG-crossover fits.

**9. Minority carriers, remote bands and inhomogeneity are dismissed too quickly.** The statement that "the electron system remains unipolar at all measured T" needs quantitative support.

- **Near the CNP.** At ν = 0.1 and T ~ 50–100 K (k_BT ≈ 4–9 meV vs E_F ~ 20 meV), thermally excited minority carriers in a Dirac spectrum (~exp(−E_F/k_BT) ≈ 0.1) are not negligible. They could produce electron–hole friction or drag (Refs. 41, 42). Charge puddles of ~10¹⁰–10¹¹ cm⁻² are comparable to n.
- **Near ν = 3.8.** The system is ~0.2 carriers per moiré cell from the ν = 4 band insulator, whose superlattice gap at 1.58° is likely tens of meV but is not given. Thermal activation into the remote band, i.e. the second miniband or γ-point pockets, creates a genuinely two-band (Baber-type) and electron–hole-friction situation. These are exactly the mechanisms the paper claims are inoperative.

The authors should quantify minority-carrier and remote-band populations versus T, e.g. from n_H(T), the ν = 4 activation gap, and the residual disorder density δn from the CNP width. They should then show that friction, drag and Baber contributions are negligible.

**10. The umklapp kinematic window rests on idealized Fermi surfaces and contains an error.** The hole-side window is quoted as "−3.5 < ν < 0.5." This extends to positive ν and is internally inconsistent; presumably it should be −3.5 < ν < −0.5, roughly mirroring the electron side 0.45 < ν < 3.45. The thresholds k_F > g/(4√3) and k_F > g/4 assume circular pockets. That conflicts with the strong trigonal warping visible in Fig. 3e, f and invoked later for the mechanism. In addition, the Hall density (Fig. 3d) used as a proxy for k_F departs from n near van Hove singularities, and the band model used to convert thresholds into ν windows is not stated. The authors should correct the window. They should recompute the thresholds from realistic, trigonally warped continuum-model Fermi contours reconciled with the Hall data, including thermal smearing of the threshold.

**11. Eq. (1) is only weakly constrained, and its discriminating predictions are not tested.** Λ spans two orders of magnitude (0.01–1), so A spans 0.005–0.5 Ω/K². The "best correspondence" Λ ≈ 0.2–0.3 is effectively a fit parameter. Agreement with the measured A ~ 0.1 Ω/K² therefore does not distinguish this mechanism from umklapp tails or two-phonon processes of comparable magnitude. Since ρ_ee ∝ a²Λ/v_F⁴, the prefactor also depends on the fourth power of a poorly known v_F. Eq. (1) makes sharper predictions:

- a weak n-dependence of A, since (k_F a)²/n is n-independent apart from Λ(n);
- a steep twist-angle dependence A ∝ v_F⁻⁴;
- a very small value in MLG, where (k_F a)² with the atomic a is tiny.

None is compared with data, despite five or more devices spanning θ = 1.6–3° and a range of ν. The warping amplitude ∝ k_F a is small exactly where the anomaly is claimed (ν ≈ 0.1), and the nearly circular pockets in Fig. 3e raise the question of whether warping is large enough there. The Methods give the screening integrals but omit the band-structure input for Δv_TW. The authors should:

- compute the warping amplitude at 1.58° and the other angles from a continuum model;
- fix the screening with each device's actual gate and hBN thicknesses so that Λ is no longer free;
- compare predicted and measured A(ν, θ) across all devices;
- move the A(ν) map, with its enhancement inside the umklapp windows, from the SI to the main text, and add A(θ).

**12. Eq. (1) is applied outside its regime of validity.** Several assumptions are questionable at the fillings used:

- **Warping expansion.** The derivation expands in k_F a for a weakly trigonally warped Dirac–Fermi liquid. At ν = 0.1 with a ≈ 9 nm, k_F a ≈ 0.6, so the perturbative treatment (Eq. 4) is marginal.
- **Wrong starting point at ν = 3.8.** The relevant pockets sit near γ in the flat band (Fig. 3f) and are not Dirac-like, yet ν = 3.8 is used as evidence for the same mechanism.
- **Strong coupling.** With reduced v_F at 1.58°, α_e = e²/(4πε₀ħv_F) is ~5–10 without dielectric screening. This makes a Born/second-order collision integral questionable and the prefactor uncontrolled. It is unclear whether hBN screening is included in α_e, which changes the estimate by ~ε².
- **Quantum geometry.** The model neglects quantum geometry and Wannier form factors. The final remark attributing the weak T-dependence above |ν| = 4 to flat-band quantum geometry or Wannier localization sits uneasily with Eq. (1) and undercuts its specificity.

The authors should identify where k_F a ≪ 1 holds and state how α_e is screened. They should give an explicit expression and numbers for A at ν = 0.1, discuss strong-coupling corrections, and either justify the Dirac model at ν = 3.8 or calculate the γ-point pockets separately.

**13. The physical picture of inter-pocket current relaxation is unclear.** The text alternates between "intervalley," "inter-pocket," "inter-layer" and "valley and layer inequivalence," and also writes "normal intervalley scattering" where "interpocket" seems meant. This obscures which collision process is meant. It is not explained why the time-reversal-related K/K′ pockets, whose warping is mirror-related, give a finite current-relaxing contribution in TBG. Nor is it explained how the moiré κ/κ′ pockets map onto the original K/K′ valleys. The authors should define the process precisely, use consistent terminology, and explain the role of the time-reversal partner pockets.

**14. The MATBG "unification" claim overreaches the evidence.** The paper states that linear-in-T resistivity in MATBG is interaction-limited and claims to "unify" magic-angle and higher-angle transport. However, it offers no mechanism for e–e-limited linear-in-T resistivity: the inter-pocket theory gives T², and umklapp also gives T² in a Fermi liquid. It is left open whether the linear-T regime is a non-Fermi liquid, a renormalized Fermi liquid with T ≳ T_F, or T-dependent screening. More fundamentally, photoresistance can at most separate T_e- from T_L-controlled contributions. It cannot discriminate among the competing electronic scenarios (Planckian/strange metal, umklapp Fermi liquid, heavy fermion, isospin-fluctuation scattering), so "resolving" the debate is partial. The authors should reframe the claim as ruling out or bounding phonons, or as "T_e-dominated." Alternatively, they should supply or at least discuss a candidate mechanism. They should also discuss explicitly how the results bear on the phonon theories (Refs. 17–25) and on Jaoui et al. (Ref. 11).

**15. The power dependence is not modelled.** The sublinear saturation of Δρ_THz toward P_max (Fig. 2d) is unexplained. Candidate origins include nonlinear cooling (supercollision or umklapp-assisted), absorption bleaching, or genuine saturation of ρ_e(T_e). Fitting a cooling law P_abs ∝ T_e^δ − T_L^δ would test the hot-electron picture and yield an e–ph coupling constant. That constant could then be checked for self-consistency with a phonon-limited 92 Ω/K slope. The authors should perform this analysis.

**16. Non-bolometric contributions and spatially inhomogeneous heating are not addressed.** Coupling occurs through antenna-connected source/drain contacts, which produce strongly non-uniform near fields concentrated near the contacts. Contact cooling over the cooling length also makes T_e non-uniform along the channel. The measured quantity is thus a weighted average over an inhomogeneous T_e profile, which complicates quantitative comparison with ρ(T). Several contributions are not quantitatively excluded:

- hot spots and contact or Schottky-like photoresponse;
- rectification and photogalvanic currents at the contacts;
- plasmonic effects;
- dynamic (ac) heating with ωτ not ≪ 1;
- bias-heating cross terms.

The probe geometry for Δρ_THz (two- vs four-terminal) is also unclear. The authors should:

- clarify the four-terminal configuration;
- show THz frequency and polarization dependence, and the dependence on antenna coupling;
- check linearity of V_dm in I and its sign reversal with I, and linear-response I–V at fixed P;
- present data from a contact-free region if available;
- discuss or simulate the spatial T_e profile.

**17. Novelty relative to prior hot-electron studies is not clearly articulated.** Hubmann et al. (Refs. 36, 37; 2D Mater. 2022, PRM 2022) already reported THz/IR photoresistance and photoconductance in TBG near the magic angle, including intensity saturation and bolometric interpretations. The authors' own prior THz photoconductivity work (Refs. 49, 50) is also relevant. The "general framework" claim needs positioning relative to established hot-electron thermometry in graphene, such as noise thermometry and photothermoelectric methods. The authors should state clearly that the advance lies in using the photoresponse as a scattering-mechanism diagnostic across twist angles and in the umklapp-free T² analysis, and should position the work relative to Hubmann et al. and Jaoui et al.

**C. Minor**

**18. Move key data into the main text and define the 70% figure.** The central 70% result and all T_e calibrations are relegated to the SI. The normalization behind the 70% figure is undefined. The authors should move these data into the main figures and state the normalization explicitly.

**19. Add a device table.** There is no list of devices giving twist angle and angle homogeneity, mobility, residual ρ₀, gate dielectric and hBN thickness, channel dimensions and contact geometry. There are also no device statistics (how many devices of each angle show each behaviour). The authors should add this information.

**20. Explain Fig. 2e and the negative response near ν ≈ 2.** The colour scale and sign structure in Fig. 2e should be explained, as should the origin of the negative Δρ_THz near ν ≈ 2.

**21. Correct figure references and typos.** In the main text, "Fig. 1a shows transport data" should be Fig. 1c, and "Fig. 1b" for THz illumination should be Fig. 1d. "Previosuly" should be corrected, and the missing word "antenna" in the Methods restored. Specifying the polarization and antenna coupling, together with the absolute powers requested in item 4, would let other groups reproduce the method as a "general framework."
