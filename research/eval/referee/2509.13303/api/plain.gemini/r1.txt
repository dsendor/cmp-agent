Here is a comprehensive peer-review-style evaluation of the manuscript **"Mixed Triplet-Singlet Order Parameter in Decoupled Superconducting 1H Monolayers of Transition-Metal Dichalcogenides"** by Almoalem et al.

---

### **1. Overview and Summary**
In this manuscript, the authors investigate the superconducting and electronic properties of the misfit layered compound $(\text{SnS})_{1.15}(\text{TaS}_2)$, which consists of alternating layers of semiconducting $\text{SnS}$ and metallic $1\text{H-TaS}_2$. Using a powerful combination of electrical transport, spin- and angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES), and millikelvin scanning tunneling microscopy/spectroscopy (STM/STS), the authors demonstrate that the $\text{SnS}$ layers effectively decouple the $1\text{H-TaS}_2$ layers into electronically isolated 2D monolayers. 

The central experimental finding is the observation of a fully gapped, two-gap superconducting spectrum ($\Delta_1 = 490\ \mu\text{eV}$, $\Delta_2 = 300\ \mu\text{eV}$) with an enhanced $T_c \simeq 3.1\text{ K}$ in the decoupled $1\text{H-TaS}_2$ layers, alongside strong Ising spin-valley locking. To explain why this multi-gap feature appears in decoupled $1\text{H}$ layers (as well as exfoliated monolayers and $4\text{Hb-TaS}_2$) but is absent in bulk $2\text{H-TaS}_2$, the authors develop a tight-binding BCS model. They show that local inversion-symmetry breaking, combined with finite-range attractive interactions and on-site Coulomb repulsion, induces strong singlet-triplet ($s+f$-wave) mixing, which manifests as a two-gap structure that is suppressed when interlayer hopping ($t_\perp$) is restored. Finally, the authors report a superconducting gap and vortices on the $\text{SnS}$ termination, which they attribute to intrinsic superconductivity induced by internal chemical pressure rather than a proximity effect.

---

### **2. Strengths and Significance**
* **Resolving a Key Puzzle in TMDCs:** Recent observations of unconventional superconductivity in $4\text{Hb-TaS}_2$ have raised the question of whether the exotic pairing is intrinsic to isolated $1\text{H-TaS}_2$ layers or driven by proximity to the strongly correlated (potentially spin-liquid) $1\text{T-TaS}_2$ layers. By replacing the $1\text{T}$ layer with a non-magnetic $\text{SnS}$ buffer layer, this work cleanly isolates the $1\text{H}$ physics, showing that the multi-gap structure is intrinsic to the decoupled $1\text{H}$ monolayer—and notably lacks the gapless residual density of states (DOS) seen in $4\text{Hb-TaS}_2$.
* **Multi-Modal Experimental Tour de Force:** Combining crystal growth, HRTEM, low-$T$ high-field transport, spin-ARPES, and sub-Kelvin STM/S in a single study provides a very complete and self-consistent picture of the material's electronic structure.
* **Intuitive Theoretical Framework:** The theoretical model successfully bridges the gap between monolayer and bulk limits. It provides a natural, symmetry-grounded explanation (via $A_{1g}$ and $A_{2u}$ mixing) for why the two-gap structure is inversely correlated with interlayer coupling $t_\perp$.

---

### **3. Major Critiques and Scientific Questions**

1. **The "Intrinsic" vs. "Proximity" Superconductivity in the SnS Layer:**
   The authors claim that the superconducting gap ($\Delta = 440\ \mu\text{eV}$) and vortices observed on the $\text{SnS}$ layer represent an *intrinsic, independent* superconducting state stabilized by internal structural pressure, rather than proximity-induced superconductivity from $1\text{H-TaS}_2$. This claim is difficult to reconcile with the data in **Supplementary Figure 4**, which shows that the gaps in *both* the $\text{SnS}$ and $1\text{H-TaS}_2$ layers vanish at the exact same out-of-plane critical field ($B \parallel c \approx 3\text{ kG}$ at $0.5\text{ K}$). 
   * If $\text{SnS}$ were an independent superconductor, it would be an extraordinary coincidence for it to share the exact same $H_{c2\perp}$ (and nearly identical gap magnitude) as the $\text{TaS}_2$ layer. 
   * While the authors cite Ref. [78] to argue that Fermi surface mismatch should suppress a proximity gap, the strong moiré potential (clearly visible in the STM FFTs of both layers) can relax momentum-conservation constraints and facilitate interlayer Cooper pair tunneling. The authors should tone down the claim of an "independent" superconducting state in $\text{SnS}$ or provide stronger evidence (e.g., showing different $T_c$ or $H_{c2}$ values between the layers).

2. **Alternative Explanation for the Two-Gap Structure (Multiband $s$-wave):**
   $1\text{H-TaS}_2$ has multiple Fermi surface pockets (around $\Gamma$ and $K/K'$). A conventional multi-band $s$-wave scenario (different isotropic/anisotropic $A_{1g}$ gaps on the $\Gamma$ vs. $K/K'$ pockets, i.e., mixing of $1$ and $e(\mathbf{k})$) could also produce a two-gap tunneling spectrum without invoking triplet ($A_{2u}$) pairing. 
   * The authors briefly address this at the very end of the Supplementary Material (Page 20, Section VII.C.3), noting that $1$ and $e(\mathbf{k})$ mixing would not depend strongly on interlayer coupling $t_\perp$. Because this is a crucial distinction that rules out a much simpler conventional explanation, this argument should be moved into the main text (Section III or IV) and expanded upon.

3. **Parameter Fine-Tuning in the Theoretical Model:**
   In the tight-binding model, observing a clear two-gap structure requires setting the on-site attraction $V_0 = 0.25t$ to be smaller than the nearest- and next-nearest-neighbor attractions ($V_1 = V_2 = 0.3t$). How sensitive is the two-gap feature to the exact ratio of $V_0 / V_1$? A brief comment or plot showing the phase space of $\varepsilon_{A_{2u}}/\varepsilon_{A_{1g}}$ as a function of $V_0/V_1$ would strengthen the theoretical section.

### **4. Minor Issues, Typos, and Inconsistencies**
The manuscript and supplementary material contain several cross-referencing errors, typos, and numerical inconsistencies that should be corrected before publication:

* **Inconsistent Coherence Length ($\xi$):** On Page 4 (column 2, paragraph 2), the vortex core fit in the $\text{SnS}$ layer is stated to yield $\xi_{\text{vortex}} \simeq 35 \pm 5\text{ nm}$. However, in the Supplementary Material (Page 15, Section V), the exact same fit is stated to yield $\xi = 45 \pm 10\text{ nm}$. 
* **Inconsistent $T_c$ Values:** The bulk superconducting transition temperature is reported as $3.1\text{ K}$ (Abstract and Page 2), $3.05\text{ K}$ (Page 2 text and Fig. 2a inset), and $3.14\text{ K}$ (Fig. 2a caption). While close, the authors should standardize how $T_c$ is defined (e.g., onset vs. 50% drop).
* **Contradictory Statement in Discussion (Page 7):** In column 1, bottom paragraph, the text reads: *"In the limit of strongly coupled layers, like the 2H system, the triplet component is small... In contrast, in the **strongly coupled** case, the f-wave component becomes comparable..."* The second instance must be corrected to **"weakly coupled"** (or **"decoupled"**).
* **Missing Figure Panel:** On Page 2 (column 2, paragraph 3) and Page 12 (Section III), the text refers to **"Fig. 2c"**, which does not exist (Figure 2 only has panels a and b). It should refer to **Fig. 2b**.
* **Incorrect Figure Cross-References:**
  * *Fig. 1 Caption (Page 3):* "(e) Spectra taken at the same location as (c)." $\rightarrow$ Should be **(d)**, as (c) is X-ray diffraction.
  * *Fig. 4 Caption (Page 5):* "...obtained on the $1\text{H-TaS}_2$ layer shown in Fig. 1c." $\rightarrow$ Should be **Fig. 1d**. "...obtained on the $\text{SnS}$ layer shown in Fig. 1e." $\rightarrow$ Should be **Fig. 1f**.
  * *Fig. 5 Caption (Page 6):* "(d) The derivative of the DOS shown in panel (b)..." $\rightarrow$ Should be **panel (c)**.
* **Typos in Figures and Equations:**
  * *Fig. 5a (Page 6):* "signlet" $\rightarrow$ **"singlet"**.
  * *Page 4 (column 1, last paragraph):* "above and bellow" $\rightarrow$ **"below"**.
  * *Supplementary Eq. (1) (Page 11):* In the Bloch-Grüneisen integral, the denominator is written as $(e^x - 1)(1 - e^{-1})$. It should be $(e^x - 1)(1 - e^{-x})$.
  * *Supplementary Eq. (9) (Page 18):* The equation for the antisymmetric gap $\Delta_j^{(a)}(\mathbf{k})$ has the symmetric interaction potential $V_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{p};j\ell}^{(s)}$ on the right-hand side instead of $V_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{p};j\ell}^{(a)}$.
  * *Supplementary Fig. 6 (Page 16):* The y-axis label for photon energy is corrupted ("$h8\text{ [eV]}$" instead of "$h\nu\text{ [eV]}$"), and it is referred to in the text on Page 17 as "Supplementary Figures 6,7,8" when it should be "7, 8, 9".

---

### **5. Conclusion**
This is a high-quality, timely, and well-executed study that significantly advances our understanding of unconventional superconductivity in two-dimensional TMDCs. It convincingly demonstrates that misfit compounds serve as an exceptional bulk platform for studying isolated monolayer physics. Once the authors address the questions regarding the proximity effect in the $\text{SnS}$ layer and clean up the typographical/referencing errors, this paper will be a strong contribution suitable for a high-impact journal in condensed matter physics (such as *Physical Review Letters*, *Nature Physics*, or *Nature Communications*).
