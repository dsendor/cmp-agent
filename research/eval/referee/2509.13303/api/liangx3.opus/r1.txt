**A. Must address**

1. **The theory models the wrong stacking geometry.** The model couples two mutually inverted 1H layers (2H geometry), with the SOC term λ o_k σz τz opposite in the two layers. In that geometry, interlayer hopping t⊥ restores global inversion and so suppresses singlet–triplet mixing. The manuscript itself states that in (SnS)1.15(TaS2) all 1H layers have the same orientation, so the crystal lacks inversion for any t⊥. Finite t⊥ would therefore not restore inversion here. The proposed control parameter ("decoupling → mixing") is not the relevant one for this material, and the key "t⊥ switches off the two-gap feature" mechanism is neither tested by, nor directly relevant to, this compound. Only the t⊥ = 0 limit is actually used. It is also unclear whether the model is consistent with the exfoliated few-layer data of Ref. 35. The authors should:
   - redo the calculation for same-orientation 1H layers coupled through a SnS spacer or SnS-derived band, including the half-unit-cell shift, the incommensurate moiré, possible TaS2–SnS hybridisation and charge transfer;
   - show explicitly whether interlayer coupling still suppresses mixing in this geometry;
   - explain why decoupling should matter at all when global inversion is already broken.

2. **Conventional multiband or anisotropic s-wave pairing is not excluded, and no experiment discriminates the scenarios.** Several conventional mechanisms would also give two DOS features:
   - The 1H Fermi surface has distinct Γ and K/K′ pockets with different orbital character and electron–phonon coupling.
   - The SM (Conclusions; Sec. VII.C.3) concedes that an A1g mixture of "1" and e(k) gives different gaps on Γ and K/K′. This is the standard multiband explanation used for NbSe2.
   - The CDW visible in topography (Q ≈ 0.354 Q_Bragg) is not discussed, although Fermi-surface reconstruction can also produce multiple gaps.
   - The measured ratios 2Δ1/k_BT_c ≈ 3.7 and 2Δ2/k_BT_c ≈ 2.3 are ordinary multiband values. Nothing in the spectrum points specifically to triplet admixture.

   The alternative is dismissed in one sentence without calculation. The only discriminant offered is t⊥-dependence, which is not varied here (see 6). The predicted K/K′ assignment of Δ2, the key theoretical prediction, is therefore untested.

   The authors should add discriminating experiments:
   - temperature-dependent STS from 0.3 K to T_c, tracking Δ1(T) and Δ2(T) separately and comparing mixed-parity with weakly coupled two-band (Table I) predictions;
   - field-dependent STS, in-plane and out-of-plane, resolving which gap fills first and exploiting the distinct Zeeman/Ising response of a triplet component (Supp. Fig. 4 shows only total suppression, and the small-gap feature is not tracked);
   - Bogoliubov QPI, or high-resolution ARPES, to assign Δ1 and Δ2 to the Γ, K/K′ or inner/outer spin-split pockets;
   - checks for gap modulation at the CDW wavevector, and sensitivity to non-magnetic disorder or controlled impurities (the triplet component violates Anderson's theorem, so the small gap should be more fragile);
   - bulk probes such as specific heat C(T, H), penetration depth λ(T) or thermal conductivity, to show the gap structure is not surface-specific.

   On the theory side, they should explicitly compute the 1 + e(k) and multiband alternatives and show whether they can reproduce the spectrum at t⊥ = 0. They should also state observables that separate the scenarios, for example:
   - in-plane H_c2 beyond the Ising limit;
   - field-angle dependence;
   - impurity sensitivity;
   - gap-anisotropy patterns between spin-split bands;
   - a QPI signature of s + f mixing.

3. **The two-gap fit is not compared like-for-like and its parameters are not reported.** The evidence for two gaps is a fit of one 0.3 K spectrum to a sum of two isotropic BCS DOS (Eq. 5), compared against a single-gap fit (SM Fig. 2). Several problems follow:
   - The single-gap fit needs T_eff ≈ 900 mK at a base temperature of 270–300 mK. Its poor quality is therefore partly an artifact of forcing thermal broadening to mimic the in-gap DOS shape, and it was evidently not constrained at the same T_eff as the two-gap fit.
   - For the two-gap fit, the weights N1/N2, T_eff, Dynes broadening, tip condition and lock-in modulation amplitude (not given for Fig. 4) are not reported.
   - The lock-in frequency is inconsistent: 870 Hz in the captions versus 907 Hz in Methods.
   - A model with more parameters is expected to fit better.
   - Without a common, independently calibrated energy resolution, the ~300 µeV "kink" (Fig. 4c; a subtle shoulder in d²I/dV²) cannot be separated from an anisotropic single gap, a distribution of gaps over the Γ and K pockets, Dynes broadening or lock-in smearing. The two claimed gaps differ by only ~190 µeV.

   The authors should:
   - report all fit parameters;
   - calibrate T_eff and resolution independently, using a superconducting tip or a reference Al sample;
   - compare single-gap, anisotropic-gap, Dynes-broadened, McMillan-bilayer and two-gap models with identical T_eff and Γ and matched parameter counts;
   - judge the models statistically (reduced χ², AIC/BIC).

4. **A proximity or hybridisation (McMillan-type) origin of the second gap is not considered.** Each 1H layer is sandwiched between SnS layers that, by the authors' own data, are metallic (finite DOS at E_F) and gapped at 440 µeV. A strongly coupled S/N or S/S′ multilayer generically produces two spectral features in both layers: an intrinsic gap plus an induced gap or minigap. The SnS gap lies between Δ2 and Δ1, which is arguably more consistent with interlayer gap mixing than with independent condensates. If SnS is metallic and superconducting, the 1H layers are not electronically isolated in the superconducting channel. This directly contradicts the "decoupled monolayer" narrative. The authors should analyse this scenario quantitatively, using a McMillan or Usadel treatment of an atomically thin metallic layer between superconductors. They should show whether TaS2–SnS hybridisation or proximity can generate the 300 µeV feature on the 1H termination.

5. **The ARPES evidence for decoupling is qualitative and partly non-discriminating.** The specific weaknesses are:
   - **Spin polarisation does not test decoupling.** Out-of-plane polarisation at K/K′ is allowed in a same-orientation, inversion-broken stack regardless of interlayer coupling. Hidden spin polarisation appears even in centrosymmetric 2H compounds (Refs. 39, 40).
   - **The polarisation scale is unclear.** The axis "P [%]" spans ±0.5, so it is either a mislabelled fraction or a negligible polarisation. No error bars or sign reversal between K and K′ are shown.
   - **The k_z test is weak.** It uses EDCs at a single momentum near M, at a feature at E_B ≈ −0.35 eV rather than Fermi-level states (Supp. Fig. S6). No dispersion is extracted, no fit is made and no upper bound on t⊥ is given. The inner potential is taken from another compound.
   - **The quoted k_z values look inconsistent** with Eq. (5) and V0 = 9 eV. The 25 eV → 2.96 Å⁻¹ conversion appears to neglect the work function, and 49–59 eV is said to span 1.5 BZ.
   - **The quantity the model needs is never checked.** The model requires t⊥ ≪ 0.8λ at the Fermi-level K pockets, and this is not tested directly.

   The authors should:
   - provide full k_z-dependent Fermi-surface maps at the Γ and K pockets, with explicit k_z calibration (c-axis repeat, work function);
   - derive an explicit upper bound on t⊥ relative to λ;
   - show spin-ARPES at both K and K′ with error bars and correct units;
   - state that spin polarisation alone does not prove decoupling.

6. **t⊥ is never varied within the study, and the cross-material correlation is not a controlled test.** The central discriminating argument is that multi-gap features appear in decoupled systems and vanish when t⊥ is finite. This study probes only t⊥ ≈ 0. The supporting correlation (two gaps in monolayer, 4Hb and misfit; one gap in 2H) compares materials from different groups that differ in T_c, doping/filling, CDW state, disorder and substrate. The 4Hb case relies on reading residual subgap DOS as a "destroyed" second gap, which is itself contested. The authors should compare against a controlled reference under identical STS conditions: 2H-TaS2, a misfit or intercalated variant with a different spacer thickness, or both. This would vary t⊥ within one material family. Otherwise the t⊥-dependence should be presented as an untested prediction.

**B. Should address**

7. **SnS→TaS2 charge transfer and the CDW are ignored.** Charge transfer from the MS layer to TX2 is ubiquitous and well documented in sulfide misfits. It changes band filling, the CDW (absent in resistivity yet present in STM), the DOS at E_F and the pairing scale. It could by itself explain the T_c enhancement relative to 2H-TaS2 (0.8 K → 3.05 K) and the modified gap structure, independently of t⊥. Citing the T_c as evidence of decoupling is therefore unconvincing. The authors should:
   - extract Fermi-surface volumes (Luttinger count) from ARPES and compare them with 2H and monolayer TaS2 to quantify charge transfer;
   - discuss its impact on T_c, the CDW and the gaps;
   - reconcile the CDW seen in STM with its absence in transport.

8. **Transport indicators look like a coupled, anisotropic 3D superconductor.** Several observations are at least as consistent with interlayer Josephson or proximity coupling as with 2D isolation:
   - The H_c2 anisotropy ratio of 19 is moderate, and the claimed "strong transport anisotropy" rests only on this ratio; ρ_c is not measured.
   - The in-plane H_c2 exceeds the Pauli limit by only ~1.95×, whereas gated or exfoliated Ising monolayers exceed it by ~4–8× or more. Bulk 2H-NbSe2 already exceeds it modestly.
   - There is a single sharp resistive transition.
   - STM finds the same critical field (~3 kG at ~0.5 K) on both terminations.

   The authors should measure ρ_c/ρ_ab, fine-angle H_c2 near θ = 90°, and BKT-type I–V characteristics. They should explain how isolated layers would give identical H_c2 on SnS and TaS2.

9. **The two-band H_c2 analysis is physically inappropriate and internally inconsistent.** The specific problems are:
   - The dirty two-band (Gurevich-type) formula is an orbital-limit model. Applying it to the in-plane field of an Ising, Pauli-protected 2D superconductor is inappropriate.
   - The fitted couplings differ between field orientations (λ11 = 0.88 vs 1.37; λ12 = 0.35 vs 0.49; D1 = 11.01 vs 0.67), but coupling constants are intrinsic material properties.
   - The fits converge to λ11 = λ22, i.e., symmetric bands, which undercuts their use as evidence for two distinct gaps.
   - λ12/λ11 ≈ 0.4 is not "mostly decoupled".
   - Calling D1 and D2 "electron and hole" diffusivities is unexplained.
   - Positive H_c2(T) curvature and near-T_c √(1−T/T_c)-like in-plane behaviour are generic to 2D/Ising superconductors, finite-thickness effects and dimensional crossover (Klemm–Luther–Beasley). They are not evidence for two gaps.

   The authors should refit with shared, orientation-independent coupling constants. They should either restrict the two-band fit to the out-of-plane orbital limit or use a model with Ising SOC and Pauli limiting for H ∥ ab.

10. **The theory is qualitative, not shown to be robust, and quantitatively mismatched with experiment.** The mismatches are:
    - The computed small/large gap ratio on the K pockets is ~0.1, versus Δ2/Δ1 ≈ 0.6 measured.
    - Model gap scales are an order of magnitude or more too large: ~0.015–0.02 t ≈ 4–5 meV per the SM, and main-text Fig. 5c peaks at eV/t ≈ 0.07–0.08 with t = 0.25 eV, i.e., ~15–20 meV, up to ~40× experiment.
    - Energy axes differ between Fig. 5 (eV/t up to 0.15) and Supp. Figs. 10–12 (up to 0.04).
    - The Fig. 5e inset shows ε_A2u/ε_A1g ≲ 0.3, contradicting the claim that the weights are "comparable".

    The robustness and justification of the model are also not demonstrated:
    - Only one interaction set (V0 = 0.25t, V1 = V2 = 0.3t) is shown, chosen ad hoc, with no phase diagram establishing how generic the two-gap regime is.
    - The claim that on-site Coulomb repulsion enhances the triplet component is asserted, but the model only reduces the on-site attraction V0 and includes no repulsive U.
    - The model is single-orbital, uses λ = 0.3 eV > t = 0.25 eV, and treats or omits the Γ pocket, all without DFT/ARPES justification.
    - It uses a coarse 40×40 k-mesh and Γ = 1 meV Dynes broadening. That broadening is comparable to or larger than the experimental gaps, which raises doubts about resolving the features.

    The authors should:
    - use DFT/Wannier- or ARPES-constrained bands, including the Γ pocket, realistic Ising splitting and realistic t⊥;
    - provide phase diagrams in (V0, V1, V2, λ, t⊥);
    - include the on-site repulsion explicitly;
    - tune interactions so gaps are ~0.3–0.5 meV, with a k-mesh fine enough to resolve them;
    - compare computed dI/dV, Δ_i(T) and H_c2∥ab directly with the data, including Δ2/Δ1.

11. **The model omits symmetry-lowering ingredients of the misfit.** The incommensurate rectangular SnS layer breaks C3 and σh. This permits Rashba SOC and in-plane triplet components that the model excludes by assumption (Ref. 70; S_z conservation). The CDW and the moiré potential are also omitted. The authors should include Rashba SOC from σh breaking and, if possible, the CDW and moiré potential. They should then assess the effect of these terms on the claimed s/f mixing.

12. **The claim of intrinsic, independent superconductivity in SnS is unsupported and contradicted by the authors' own data.** The observations point to one shared condensate, as expected when tunnelling through a thin, partially metallic SnS sheet into TaS2, or for a strongly proximitised or hybridised layer:
    - The SnS gap (440 µeV) is close to Δ1.
    - The STM critical field is the same on both terminations (3 kG, SM Fig. 4).
    - Coherence lengths are comparable.
    - Transport shows a single bulk transition.
    - The SnS sheet is ~2 atomic layers thick (≪ ξ ≈ 30 nm) and is sandwiched between superconducting 1H layers on both sides.

    Independent superconductors would generally have different T_c and H_c2. The counterarguments are weak:
    - Vortices, including CdGM-like core states, and matching H_c2 also occur in proximitised layers.
    - The comparison with the 1T termination of 4Hb-TaS2 is not apt, since 1T-TaS2 is a correlated Mott/CDW insulator with different tunnelling and proximity physics.
    - The "absence of a second gap" is model-dependent and could reflect momentum-selective tunnelling or matrix elements.
    - Ref. 78 concerns a semiconductor nanowire with Fermi-surface mismatch. It depends on interface transparency, which is not estimated, and does not apply to an atomically thin layer.
    - The "internal pressure" hypothesis would require >40 GPa-equivalent compression, with no strain, lattice-parameter or DFT support.

    The authors should:
    - measure the T dependence and local T_c of the SnS gap versus the 1H gaps;
    - measure STS on SnS terraces of different thickness, across 1H/SnS step edges and over buried steps, to look for a proximity length dependence;
    - measure tip-height and setpoint dependence, to exclude tunnelling into the underlying TaS2;
    - characterise SnS-derived states at E_F and charge transfer by DFT/ARPES;
    - discuss proximity in the strong-hybridisation limit.

    Otherwise they should present the SnS gap as an induced or shared condensate and frame "intrinsic SnS superconductivity" and "internal pressure" as hypotheses.

13. **The MBE-monolayer single-gap result contradicts the main conclusion and is not discussed.** MBE-grown monolayer 1H-TaS2 shows a single, f-wave-like gap. This directly challenges the conclusion that multigap superconductivity is intrinsic to isolated 1H layers. The authors should discuss and reconcile this result.

14. **Novelty is overstated relative to prior work, and the title presents an interpretation as a demonstration.** Much of the work has close precedents:
    - The two-gap spectrum and its magnitudes (≈0.25/0.46 meV) closely reproduce the exfoliated-monolayer results of Ref. 35 (Simon et al., Nat. Commun. 2024). That work shares theory co-authors, uses a similar singlet–triplet interpretation, and already linked multi-gap features to layer number.
    - Ising superconductivity and decoupling in misfit TMDs are established ((PbSe)/(LaSe)–NbSe2, Ref. 54; Devarakonda et al., Science 2020).
    - (SnS)1.15TaS2 has already been studied by STM/ARPES (Li et al., Adv. Mater. 2024).
    - ARPES decoupling in TaS2 heterostructures was shown in Ref. 41.
    - Singlet–triplet mixing in non-centrosymmetric 1H layers is known (Refs. 69, 75).

    The genuinely new elements are low-temperature STS on the misfit 1H surface, the SnS-termination gap and the t⊥-dependent calculation. The authors should cite and compare this prior work more fully. They should retitle or rephrase so that the "mixed triplet–singlet order parameter" is presented as a proposed interpretation consistent with the data, not a demonstrated order parameter.

**C. Minor**

15. **Errors and unclear statements in the theory formalism.** The authors should correct the following:
    - **Equations.**
      - Eq. (9) defines Δ^(a) with V^(s) instead of V^(a).
      - The self-consistency Eqs. (11)–(12) lack the tanh/temperature factor and the momentum sum.
    - **Symmetry and terminology.**
      - Irreps are labelled A1g/A2u of "D3", but the monolayer point group is D3h, where g/u labels are not meaningful.
      - The plotted ε_A2u/ε_A1g ratio is not defined operationally in the SM.
      - "Spin-degenerate bands of a single 1H layer" contradicts Ising SOC.
      - "Imbalance of gap strength on K and K′" is unclear. Time-reversal symmetry fixes |Δ| at K↑ = K′↓, so the splitting should be between inner and outer spin-split pockets.
      - The Discussion says the f-wave component becomes comparable "in the strongly coupled case" where "weakly coupled/decoupled" is meant.
    - **t⊥ values and figure labels.**
      - The main text uses t⊥/λ = 2 and a merger at 0.8λ ≈ 0.24 eV (Fig. 5e), while the SM uses 0.1 and 0.2 eV (≈ 0.33λ and 0.67λ).
      - SM figures label t⊥ as "t = 0".
      - The Fig. 5 caption refers to "panel (b)" for the DOS.

16. **Problems in the resistivity fit.**
    - The fitted residual resistivity (145 µΩ cm) contradicts the ~30–35 µΩ cm plotted in Fig. 2a.
    - The saturation resistivity (23 mΩ cm) is ~60× the 300 K value, so it cannot cause visible saturation.
    - The prefactor C = 3.7 ± 5 mΩ cm has an error bar larger than its value and is unconstrained.
    - The Bloch–Grüneisen integrand contains (1 − e⁻¹) instead of (1 − e⁻ˣ).

    These should be corrected.

17. **Inconsistent numerical values, broken cross-references and typos.**
    - T_c is quoted as 3.05, 3.1 and 3.14 K.
    - ξ_vortex is 35 ± 5 nm in the main text and 45 ± 10 nm in the SM. The quoted ~30 nm from H_c2 does not match the ≈24 nm implied by H_c2 = 0.59 T.
    - The STM critical field of 3 kG at ~0.5 K is described as in "excellent agreement" with transport, yet Fig. 2b shows H_c2∥c ≈ 0.5–0.6 T at ~0.4–0.5 K.
    - Figure references point to non-existent panels ("Fig. 2c"; Fig. 4 captions and text cite "Fig. 1c/1e" for STM data).
    - The SM section on magnetic-field dependence is empty.
    - There are typos ("supposed", "below", "signature", "constituent").

    Together these reduce confidence in the analysis and should be unified and fixed.
