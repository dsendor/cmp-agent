Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Question addressed.** The manuscript asks whether the unconventional, multi-gap superconductivity reported in few-layer and heterostructured TaS2 is intrinsic to an isolated 1H layer or is set by its environment. It does so using a bulk misfit crystal, (SnS)1.15(TaS2), in which SnS spacers are argued to decouple the 1H layers electronically.
- **Combination of probes.** The work brings together:
  - transport (Tc ≈ 3.05 K, anisotropy ≈ 19, in-plane Hc2 ≈ 1.95× the Pauli limit);
  - spin- and photon-energy-resolved ARPES (spin-valley locking, no resolvable kz dispersion along M–L);
  - 300 mK STM/STS (a two-gap spectrum on 1H terminations with Δ1 ≈ 490 µeV and Δ2 ≈ 300 µeV; a single gap of ≈ 440 µeV with vortices on SnS terminations).
- **Theoretical proposal.** A minimal model attributes the two-gap feature to singlet (A1g) / triplet (A2u, f-wave) mixing. The model combines local inversion breaking, Ising SOC, and finite-range attraction with a suppressed on-site term, and it predicts that the mixing is quenched by interlayer hopping t⊥.
- **Potential appeal.**
  - Using bulk misfit crystals as a macroscopic, air-stable "monolayer" platform could unify the conflicting gap reports for bulk 2H, exfoliated monolayer, MBE monolayer, and 4Hb-TaS2.
  - The claim of intrinsic superconductivity in a nominally semiconducting SnS layer would be surprising if substantiated.
- **Novelty is more limited than presented.**
  - Ising superconductivity and decoupling in misfit TMDs (e.g., (PbSe)/(LaSe)–NbSe2, Ref. 54) are established.
  - Two gaps of essentially identical magnitude (0.25/0.46 meV) were already reported in exfoliated monolayer TaS2 (Ref. 35, with overlapping authors).
  - Singlet–triplet mixing in non-centrosymmetric 1H layers is a known concept (Refs. 69, 75).
  - The main new elements are therefore:
    - observing the same two-gap spectrum in a bulk misfit crystal;
    - the t⊥-dependence argument;
    - the SnS-gap claim.
  - Each of these requires stronger support for a top-tier venue.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Well-characterized crystals and multiple probes.**
  - Sharp (00l) XRD, HRTEM, and RRR ≈ 12 support crystal quality.
  - Spatially uniform gaps (150 spectra over 45 nm) and a hard, fully gapped STS spectrum without residual zero-bias DOS contrast with 4Hb-TaS2.
- **Quantitative consistency with independent work.**
  - The two gap values match exfoliated monolayer TaS2 (Ref. 35).
  - Tc ≈ 3 K approaches the monolayer value.
  - Together these strengthen the notion that a "monolayer-like" superconducting state is realized in bulk.
- **Direct spectroscopic evidence for 2D, spin-valley-locked electronic structure.** Spin-resolved EDC/MDC show opposite out-of-plane polarization at K/K′, and the M–L EDCs are kz-independent. This complements the Ising-type Hc2 enhancement.
- **A symmetry-based, falsifiable theoretical scenario.** The proposal ties a spectroscopic observable (a two-gap DOS) to a structural control parameter (t⊥). It offers a common explanation across TaS2 polytypes and could guide experiments on other 1H-TMD misfits.
- **Interesting secondary observation.** A superconducting gap with vortices imaged on the SnS termination may, if confirmed as intrinsic, open a route to stacked, distinct superconductors within one crystal.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**(i) The two-gap identification and its link to singlet–triplet mixing are not established beyond phenomenological fitting.**

- **The fitting comparison is not on equal footing.**
  - The single-gap fit (SM Fig. 2) uses an isotropic BCS form with an unusually high effective temperature (900 mK). Its poorness is partly an artifact of forcing thermal broadening to mimic the in-gap DOS shape.
  - No comparison is made with equally parameterized alternatives: Dynes broadening, an anisotropic single gap Δ(φ) on K pockets, or Γ- versus K-pocket gaps with an isotropic s-wave order parameter.
  - The two-gap fit parameters (N1/N2 weights, Teff, broadening) and the lock-in modulation amplitude used for the gap spectra are not reported.
  - The lock-in frequency is inconsistent: 870 Hz in the captions versus 907 Hz in Methods.
  - Without these details, the significance of the subtle "kink" at ~300 µeV (Fig. 4c) cannot be judged.
- **No microscopic evidence connects the gaps to specific momenta or to triplet content.**
  - The authors' own theory (SM Conclusions) admits that an A1g mixture of "1" and e(k) produces different gaps on Γ and K/K′ pockets. This is the conventional multiband explanation already invoked for NbSe2.
  - The only discriminant offered is the t⊥-dependence, and t⊥ is not varied within this study.
  - Experiments that would actually distinguish the scenarios are absent:
    - quasiparticle interference to assign gaps to pockets;
    - temperature dependence of Δ1 and Δ2, which should differ for a mixed-parity state versus two weakly coupled s-wave bands;
    - sensitivity to non-magnetic disorder, where the triplet component violates Anderson's theorem;
    - field-dependent filling of the small versus large gap.
- **The Hc2 analysis does not independently support two gaps.**
  - The dirty two-band (Gurevich-type) formula is an orbital-limit model. Applying it to the in-plane field of an Ising, Pauli-protected 2D superconductor is physically inappropriate. The near-Tc √(1−T/Tc)-like behavior of in-plane Hc2 is generic to 2D/Ising superconductors.
  - The fit returns different "BCS coupling constants" λ11 and λ12 for the two field orientations (0.88/0.35 versus 1.37/0.49). Intrinsic couplings cannot depend on field direction, so the fits are not mutually consistent.
  - The fits converge to λ11 = λ22, i.e., symmetric bands, which undercuts their use as evidence for two distinct gaps.

**(ii) The decoupling premise and its mapping onto the 2H-bilayer theory are questionable.**

- **The structure studied is not the structure modeled.**
  - The authors state that in (SnS)1.15(TaS2) all 1H layers have the same orientation, so bulk inversion is globally broken.
  - The model instead couples two mutually inverted layers (2H geometry), where t⊥ restores global inversion and thereby suppresses mixing.
  - With same-orientation stacking, finite t⊥ would not restore inversion, so the proposed mechanism ("decoupling → mixing") is not the relevant control parameter for this material.
  - The theory should be redone for the actual stacking, including the half-unit-cell shift and the incommensurate moiré.
- **The decoupling evidence is indirect.**
  - Spin polarization at K/K′ would appear in a same-orientation stack even with appreciable interlayer coupling, so it does not by itself demonstrate decoupling.
  - The kz-independence is shown only for EDCs at a single M-point region, without a Fermi-surface kz map or an estimate of an upper bound on t⊥.
  - The claimed polarization scale ("P [%]" of ±0.5) is either a mislabeled fraction or an extremely small polarization; this should be clarified.
- **Charge transfer from SnS to TaS2 is not addressed.**
  - Charge transfer is a ubiquitous and dominant effect in misfit compounds. It changes band filling, the CDW (absent in resistivity yet present in STM), and the density of states at EF.
  - Each of these could explain the Tc enhancement (0.8 K → 3 K) and modified gap structure independently of t⊥.
  - Fermi-surface volumes from ARPES and a comparison with pristine 2H/monolayer TaS2 are needed.
- **The cross-material correlation is not a controlled test.** The supporting correlation (two gaps in monolayer, 4Hb, and misfit; one gap in 2H) compares materials that differ in doping, CDW state, disorder, and substrate. The 4Hb case relies on interpreting residual subgap DOS as a "destroyed" second gap, which is itself contested.

**(iii) The claim of intrinsic superconductivity in the SnS layer is not supported and is contradicted by the authors' own data.**

- **The data point toward a common superconducting condensate.** All of the following are what one expects when tunneling through a thin (~2 atomic-layer), partially metallic SnS sheet into the underlying TaS2, or for a strongly proximitized layer:
  - the gap magnitude (440 µeV) is close to the larger 1H gap;
  - the critical field is the same in both layers (3 kG, SM Fig. 4);
  - the coherence lengths are comparable;
  - there is a single bulk transition.
- **The counterarguments are weak.**
  - Comparison with the 1T termination of 4Hb-TaS2 is not apt, since 1T-TaS2 is a correlated Mott/CDW insulator with very different tunneling and proximity physics.
  - The "absence of a second gap" may simply reflect momentum-selective tunneling or a different matrix element.
  - Ref. 78's Fermi-surface-mismatch argument assumes specific interface parameters not established here.
  - The "internal pressure" hypothesis would need >40 GPa-equivalent compression, for which no structural or DFT support is offered.
- **Vortex imaging does not settle the question.**
  - A proximitized layer, or a layer through which one tunnels into a superconductor, also displays vortices.
  - The coherence-length values are internally inconsistent: 35 ± 5 nm in the main text, 45 ± 10 nm in the SM, and ~30 nm from Hc2.
  - The stated "excellent agreement" of the 3 kG STM critical field with transport is inconsistent with Fig. 2b, which shows Hc2∥c ≈ 0.5–0.6 T at ~0.4–0.5 K.

**(iv) The theory is qualitative, insufficiently controlled, and contains internal inconsistencies; presentation errors are pervasive.**

- **The quantitative mismatch with experiment is large.**
  - The computed small/large gap ratio on the K pockets is ~0.1 (SM), versus ~0.6 experimentally.
  - The computed gap scale (~0.02 t ≈ 5 meV) is ~10× larger than measured.
  - The inset of Fig. 5e shows εA2u/εA1g ≤ 0.3. This contradicts the narrative that the weights are "comparable".
- **Robustness and key claims are not demonstrated.**
  - The interaction parameters (V0 = 0.25t, V1 = V2 = 0.3t) are chosen ad hoc, with no phase diagram showing how generic the two-gap regime is.
  - The asserted role of on-site Coulomb repulsion in enhancing the triplet component is stated but not shown.
  - The model is single-orbital, uses a 40×40 k-mesh, and applies Γ = 1 meV Dynes broadening. That broadening is comparable to or larger than the experimental gaps, which raises questions about resolving the features.
- **There are errors in the theory and figures.**
  - In Eq. (9), Δ(a) is written with V(s) instead of V(a).
  - The t⊥ values are inconsistent: the main text uses t⊥/λ = 2 and a merger at 0.8λ, while the SM uses 0.1 and 0.2 eV (≈ 0.33λ and 0.67λ). SM figures are labeled "t = 0" for t⊥.
  - The Fig. 5 caption refers to "panel (b)" for the DOS.
  - The Discussion says the f-wave component is large in the "strongly coupled case" when "weakly coupled" is meant.
- **The transport analysis and figure references are inconsistent.**
  - The fitted residual resistivity (145 µΩcm) contradicts the plotted ~30 µΩcm.
  - The saturation resistivity (23 mΩcm) is ~60× the 300 K value, so it cannot cause visible saturation.
  - The prefactor C = 3.7 ± 5 mΩcm is unconstrained.
  - The Bloch–Grüneisen integrand contains a typo, (1−e⁻¹) instead of (1−e⁻ˣ).
  - Tc is quoted variously as 3.05, 3.1, and 3.14 K.
  - There are references to non-existent panels ("Fig. 2c"; "Fig. 1c/1e" for STM data).
  - The SM section on magnetic-field dependence is empty.
  - These errors collectively reduce confidence in the analysis.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Strengthen and diversify the gap spectroscopy.**
   - Report all fit parameters, the modulation amplitude, and the effective electron temperature (calibrated, e.g., on a reference superconductor).
   - Compare, with equal numbers of parameters, two isotropic gaps, an anisotropic single gap, and Dynes-broadened models, using information criteria.
   - Add temperature-dependent STS from 0.3 K to Tc for both gaps, and field-dependent STS resolving which gap fills first.
   - Add QPI or Bogoliubov-QPI maps to assign Δ1 and Δ2 to the Γ and K/K′ pockets.
   - Where possible, add a disorder or impurity study distinguishing mixed-parity from conventional multiband s-wave states.
2. **Recast the theory for the actual misfit structure and make it quantitative.**
   - Model same-orientation stacking, including the effect of the SnS layer and charge transfer, rather than the 2H inverted bilayer.
   - Explain why decoupling matters when global inversion is already broken.
   - Provide phase diagrams in (V0, V1, V2, λ, t⊥) showing robustness.
   - Explicitly include the on-site repulsion claimed to enhance the triplet component.
   - Fit or at least match the experimental gap ratio (~0.6) and gap scale using realistic TaS2 SOC and t⊥ values (e.g., from DFT or Wannier models).
   - State clear predictions that distinguish A1g/A2u mixing from A1g-only multiband pairing: in-plane Hc2 beyond the Ising limit, disorder sensitivity, and gap-anisotropy patterns between spin-split bands.
3. **Substantiate decoupling and quantify doping.**
   - Provide full kz-dependent Fermi-surface maps and an explicit upper bound on t⊥ derived from the M–L data.
   - Compare Fermi-surface volumes with 2H and monolayer TaS2 to quantify SnS→TaS2 charge transfer, and discuss its impact on Tc and the CDW.
   - Clarify the units and magnitude of the spin polarization.
   - Ideally, compare with a misfit or intercalated variant of different spacer thickness to vary t⊥ within one material family.
   - Reconcile the CDW seen in STM with its absence in transport.
4. **Either substantially support or temper the SnS superconductivity claim, and fix the presentation.**
   - Measure the temperature dependence of the SnS gap versus the 1H gaps.
   - Measure STS on SnS terraces of different thickness or over buried steps, together with tip-height and setpoint dependence, to exclude tunneling into the underlying TaS2.
   - Perform DFT or ARPES on SnS-derived states at EF, and discuss proximity in the strong-hybridization limit.
   - Otherwise, present the SnS gap as an induced or shared condensate.
   - Correct the listed inconsistencies:
     - resistivity fit values and the Bloch–Grüneisen formula;
     - Tc values;
     - coherence lengths;
     - the 3 kG versus 0.5 T critical field;
     - Eq. (9);
     - t⊥ labels;
     - figure cross-references;
     - lock-in frequencies;
     - the empty SM section;
     - typos.
   - Restrict the Hc2 two-band fit to the out-of-plane orbital limit, or use a model with Ising SOC for the in-plane field.
