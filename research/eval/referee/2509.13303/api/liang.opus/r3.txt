Review outline:

**Manuscript:** "Mixed Triplet-Singlet Order Parameter in Decoupled Superconducting 1H Monolayers of Transition-Metal Dichalcogenides" — (SnS)1.15(TaS2), combining transport, spin-/kz-ARPES, STM/STS and a tight-binding BCS model.

---

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **The question addressed is important and timely.** Is the unconventional, multi-gap superconductivity seen in 1H-TaS2 (exfoliated monolayers, 4Hb-TaS2, MBE monolayers) intrinsic to an isolated 1H layer, or set by its environment? Misfit compounds are an attractive bulk route to "stacked monolayers." They are cleavable and large-area, and they avoid the substrate and encapsulation issues of exfoliated or MBE films.
- **Main claimed advances:**
  - High-resolution STS on a bulk 1H termination showing a fully gapped, spatially uniform, two-feature spectrum, with Δ1 ≈ 490 µeV and Δ2 ≈ 300 µeV.
  - Transport showing Tc ≈ 3.05 K (close to monolayer TaS2), positive-curvature Hc2(T), and an in-plane Hc2 about 1.95× the Pauli limit.
  - Spin-ARPES and kz-ARPES used to argue for spin-valley locking and negligible interlayer hopping.
  - A minimal model in which Ising SOC plus finite-range attraction (suppressed on-site attraction) produces s/f-wave singlet–triplet mixing. This mixing creates a K/K′ gap imbalance and a two-gap DOS that is suppressed by interlayer hopping t⊥.
  - An unexpected superconducting gap (Δ ≈ 440 µeV) with vortices on the nominally semiconducting SnS termination.
- **Novelty is moderate and partly incremental.**
  - The two-gap spectrum and its magnitudes (≈0.25/0.46 meV) closely reproduce the exfoliated-monolayer results of Ref. [35]. That work shares theory co-authors and already linked multi-gap features to layer number.
  - The singlet–triplet mixing framework in non-centrosymmetric TMDs (Refs. [69, 75]) is established.
  - Superconductivity in TaS2-based misfits has been reported before; ARPES decoupling in TaS2 heterostructures was shown in Ref. [41].
  - The genuinely new elements are therefore:
    - the STS on a misfit 1H surface;
    - the SnS-termination gap and vortices;
    - the explicit t⊥-dependent calculation.
  - The impact depends on whether these elements are shown to discriminate between the proposed mechanism and conventional alternatives. In the present form they do not.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Broad, multi-probe dataset on one material.** Transport (ρ(T), angle- and T-dependent Hc2), spin-resolved and photon-energy-dependent ARPES, HRTEM/XRD, and STM topography and spectroscopy on two distinct terminations. Few studies combine all of these on the same system.
- **High-quality spectroscopy.**
  - Spectra at 0.3 K are hard-gapped with no residual zero-bias DOS, in contrast to 4Hb-TaS2.
  - They are uniform over 45 nm (150 spectra).
  - The d²I/dV² shows two reproducible features.
  - This makes the misfit surface a cleaner platform than 4Hb-TaS2 for gap-structure studies.
- **Simple, symmetry-grounded theoretical scenario.**
  - The model gives a physically transparent reason why multi-gap features would correlate with weak interlayer coupling.
  - It identifies concrete microscopic ingredients: Ising SOC, finite-range attraction, reduced on-site attraction.
  - It could guide future experiments on other 1H-TMDs (NbSe2, TaSe2, alloyed systems).
- **Potentially striking SnS observation.** Vortex imaging and a full gap on the SnS termination are intriguing. Coexisting, possibly distinct superconductors in a single crystal would interest the community if substantiated.
- **Self-consistent coherence lengths.** ξ ≈ 30 nm from Hc2 and ≈35 nm (main text) or 45 nm (SM) from vortex profiles agree, supporting the basic superconducting characterization.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**3.1 The two-gap spectroscopic evidence is not unique, and alternative explanations are not excluded.**
- **Fitting details are missing, and the comparison with a single gap is not like-for-like.**
  - For the two-gap fit, the manuscript does not report the weights N1/N2, the effective temperature, any Dynes broadening, the lock-in modulation amplitude, or the tip condition.
  - The single-gap fit (Supp. Fig. S2) returns T_eff ≈ 900 mK at a base temperature of 270–300 mK. This suggests the fit was not constrained at the same T_eff as the two-gap fit.
  - Without a common, independently calibrated energy resolution (e.g., from a superconducting tip or a reference Al sample), a 300 µeV "kink" is a subtle shoulder in d²I/dV². It cannot be cleanly separated from these alternatives:
    - an anisotropic single gap on several pockets;
    - Dynes-type broadening;
    - lock-in smearing (modulation not given for Fig. 4; 870 Hz in captions vs 907 Hz in Methods).
- **Conventional multiband or anisotropy origins are not ruled out.**
  - The 1H Fermi surface has distinct Γ and K/K′ pockets with different orbital character and electron–phonon coupling. A conventional anisotropic s-wave gap (the 1 + e(k) admixture, which the SM itself admits) gives two DOS features.
  - The CDW (Q ≈ 0.354 Q_Bragg, visible in topography) is not discussed. Fermi-surface reconstruction can likewise produce multiple gaps.
  - The argument against these alternatives — that they "should not depend on interlayer coupling" — is not tested in this paper. t⊥ is not varied in any experiment here; the comparison relies on other materials from other groups, with different Tc, filling, disorder and CDW.
- **A proximity-coupled (McMillan-type) explanation is not considered.**
  - The 1H layer is sandwiched between SnS layers that are metallic (finite DOS at E_F, superconducting gap of 440 µeV).
  - A strongly coupled S/N or S/S′ bilayer generically produces two spectral features in both layers: an intrinsic gap plus an induced/minigap feature.
  - The SnS gap lies between Δ2 and Δ1. This is arguably more consistent with interlayer gap mixing than with independent condensates.
  - This scenario directly contradicts the "decoupled monolayer" narrative and must be addressed quantitatively.
- **No bulk thermodynamic or momentum-resolved confirmation.**
  - There is no specific heat, penetration depth, thermal conductivity, quasiparticle interference, or temperature/field dependence of the two gaps separately.
  - Supp. Fig. S4 shows the full spectra vs B, but the small-gap feature is not tracked.
  - Hence the assignment of Δ2 to K vs K′ imbalance (the key theoretical prediction) is untested.

**3.2 The central premise that the 1H layers are electronically decoupled is not convincingly established and is partly contradicted by the data.**
- **Transport indicators point to a coupled, anisotropic 3D superconductor rather than isolated monolayers.**
  - The Hc2 anisotropy ratio of 19 is moderate.
  - The in-plane Hc2 exceeds the Pauli limit by only ~1.95. Gated or exfoliated Ising monolayers exceed it by factors of ~4–8 or more, and bulk 2H-NbSe2 already exceeds it modestly.
  - There is a single sharp resistive transition.
  - The same critical field (~3 kG at ~0.5 K) is found on both terminations by STM.
  - These features are at least as consistent with interlayer Josephson/proximity coupling as with true 2D isolation. The authors should explain how isolated layers yield identical Hc2 on SnS and TaS2.
- **The ARPES evidence is qualitative and does not discriminate.**
  - **Spin-ARPES does not test decoupling.** Out-of-plane spin polarization at K/K′ is expected regardless of interlayer coupling. Hidden spin polarization is seen even in centrosymmetric 2H compounds (Refs. [39, 40]). Here the 1H layers are stacked with the same orientation, so bulk inversion is broken anyway.
  - **Polarization units are unclear.** The axis labelled "P [%]" spans ±0.5; if literally percent, the polarization is negligible.
  - **The kz test is weak.** Supp. Fig. S6 shows EDCs at a single momentum with no extracted dispersion, no fit, and no quantitative upper bound on t⊥. The inner potential is taken from another compound.
  - **The relevant quantity is not tested.** The model requires t⊥ ≪ 0.8λ at the Fermi-level K pockets, which is never checked directly.
- **Charge transfer and band-filling effects are ignored.**
  - In sulfide misfits, charge transfer from the MS layer to TX2 is well documented. It changes band filling, CDW and the pairing scale, and could by itself explain the Tc enhancement relative to 2H-TaS2 (0.8 K → 3.05 K).
  - The assertion that the Tc "is also evident" as proof of decoupling is therefore unconvincing.
  - Fermi-surface areas (Luttinger count) from ARPES, compared with 2H-TaS2, are needed.

**3.3 The theoretical model does not correspond to the studied material, and the agreement with experiment is only qualitative.**
- **Wrong stacking geometry for the mechanism.**
  - The model is a 2H bilayer, with the SOC term λ o_k σz τz opposite in the two layers. In it, t⊥ restores global inversion and thereby suppresses singlet–triplet mixing.
  - The paper itself stresses that in (SnS)1.15(TaS2) all 1H layers have the same orientation, so the crystal lacks inversion for any t⊥.
  - In the actual material, interlayer coupling would therefore not be expected to suppress the mixing.
  - The model's key "t⊥ switches off the two-gap feature" mechanism is thus not tested by, nor directly relevant to, this compound.
  - It is equally unclear whether the model is consistent with exfoliated few-layer data from Ref. [35].
- **Missing symmetry-lowering ingredients of the misfit.**
  - The incommensurate rectangular SnS layer breaks C3 and σh. This permits Rashba SOC and in-plane triplet components excluded by assumption (Ref. [70]; Sz conservation).
  - The CDW and the moiré potential are also omitted.
  - Their effect on the claimed s/f mixing should be assessed.
- **Quantitative mismatch and parameter sensitivity.**
  - In the model, the small-to-large gap ratio on the K pockets is ~0.1. Experimentally Δ2/Δ1 ≈ 0.6.
  - Model gap scales (~0.015–0.02 t ≈ 4–5 meV) exceed experiment by an order of magnitude.
  - The t⊥ at which the feature disappears is quoted inconsistently: 0.8λ ≈ 0.24 eV in Fig. 5e, versus 0.2 eV in the SM.
  - Energy axes differ between Fig. 5 (eV/t up to 0.15) and Supp. Figs. 10–12 (up to 0.04).
  - Only one interaction set (V0 = 0.25t, V1 = V2 = 0.3t) is shown. No phase diagram in (V0, V1, V2, λ) demonstrates robustness.
  - The text states that "on-site Coulomb repulsion" enhances the triplet, but the model only weakens the on-site attraction. No repulsive U is included.
- **The alternative is dismissed rather than computed, and the formalism has errors.**
  - The 1 + e(k) (Γ vs K) anisotropy alternative is dismissed in one sentence without calculation.
  - Eq. (9) defines Δ^(a) with V^(s), presumably a typo for V^(a).
  - Irreps are labelled A1g/A2u of "D3," whereas the monolayer point group is D3h, where g/u labels are not meaningful. The triplet ε_A2u/ε_A1g ratios plotted in Fig. 5e are not defined operationally in the SM.

**3.4 The claim of intrinsic (non-proximity) superconductivity in SnS, and parts of the transport analysis, are insufficiently supported.**
- **Arguments against proximity are weak.**
  - The SnS sheet is ~2 atomic layers thick (≪ ξ ≈ 30 nm) and metallic. It is sandwiched between superconducting 1H layers on both sides.
  - In this geometry a nearly full induced gap, with matching Hc2 and vortices, is expected from proximity, inverse proximity, or simple hybridization.
  - Vortices and Hc2 therefore do not distinguish intrinsic from induced superconductivity.
  - The comparison with the 1T layer of 4Hb-TaS2 is not apt: that layer is a Mott/CDW insulator, not a metal.
  - Ref. [78] (semiconductor nanowire, Fermi-surface mismatch) does not apply to an atomically thin layer.
  - The "absence of a second proximity gap" is itself model-dependent.
  - The "internal pressure" explanation, by analogy with SnS superconductivity above 40 GPa, is speculative and unquantified. No strain or lattice-parameter estimate is given.
- **The two-gap Hc2 analysis is internally inconsistent.**
  - The Gurevich dirty two-band formula is used, but the fitted intraband couplings differ between field orientations (λ11 = 0.88 vs 1.37; λ12 = 0.35 vs 0.49). Coupling constants are material properties and must be common to both orientations.
  - λ12/λ11 ≈ 0.4 is not "mostly decoupled."
  - Describing D1 and D2 as "electron and hole" diffusivities is unexplained.
  - For H ∥ ab, an orbital-only model is inappropriate for an Ising superconductor, where spin–orbit/Pauli physics dominates.
  - Positive Hc2(T) curvature also arises from dimensional crossover (Klemm–Luther–Beasley) and from the Ising/finite-thickness physics invoked elsewhere, so it is not evidence for two gaps.
  - Tc is quoted variously as 3.05, 3.1 and 3.14 K.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Make the spectroscopy discriminating.**
   - Report all fit parameters (N1/N2, T_eff, Dynes Γ, lock-in amplitude) and calibrate the energy resolution independently.
   - Show single-gap, anisotropic-gap, McMillan bilayer and two-gap fits with identical T_eff and Γ.
   - Measure the temperature dependence of Δ1 and Δ2 up to Tc, and the field dependence of the small-gap feature separately.
   - Use Bogoliubov quasiparticle interference to locate each gap in k-space and test the predicted K/K′ imbalance against Γ/K anisotropy.
   - Check for gap modulation at the CDW wavevector and near controlled impurities. The triplet-rich small gap should be more fragile to non-magnetic disorder than an s-wave gap.
   - Ideally, add bulk probes (specific heat, λ(T)) to confirm two gaps in the bulk.
2. **Quantify the decoupling.**
   - Extract kz dispersion at the Fermi-level K and Γ pockets and give an explicit upper bound on t⊥ relative to λ.
   - Determine charge transfer from Fermi-surface areas and compare with 2H-TaS2.
   - Measure c-axis resistivity or anisotropy, fine-angle Hc2 near θ = 90°, and BKT-type I–V characteristics.
   - Reconcile these results with the identical Hc2 on the two terminations and with the modest Pauli-limit violation.
   - Compare with a controlled reference sample (2H-TaS2 or a misfit with different spacer thickness), ideally with STS under the same conditions. This would give a direct experimental test of the t⊥ dependence the paper emphasizes.
3. **Align the theory with the actual material and make it predictive.**
   - Model same-orientation stacking coupled through an SnS-derived band. Include Rashba SOC from σh breaking and, if possible, the CDW.
   - Show explicitly whether coupling still suppresses mixing in this geometry.
   - Provide a parameter-space map (V0, V1, V2, λ, t⊥) and explicit calculations of the 1 + e(k) and multiband alternatives.
   - Match experimental observables (Δ2/Δ1 ≈ 0.6, absolute scales after rescaling, Δ_i(T), Hc2∥ab) and state distinguishing predictions, e.g., field-orientation dependence, impurity sensitivity, or a QPI signature of s+f mixing.
   - Correct the formalism and notation: Eq. (9), D3h vs D3 labelling, the definition of ε_A2u/ε_A1g, and the inconsistent t⊥ thresholds and axes.
4. **Substantiate or temper the SnS claims, and clean up the presentation.**
   - Perform a proximity analysis (McMillan or Usadel for an atomically thin metallic layer between two superconductors).
   - Measure gap evolution across 1H/SnS step edges and the temperature dependence of the SnS gap relative to the 1H gaps.
   - Either provide evidence (e.g., distinct Tc or Hc2 for the SnS gap) or reframe "intrinsic SnS superconductivity" and "internal pressure" as hypotheses.
   - Redo the Hc2 fit with shared coupling constants and an Ising-appropriate model for H ∥ ab.
   - Fix inconsistencies:
     - Tc values;
     - lock-in frequencies;
     - figure cross-references (Fig. 4 captions cite Fig. 1c/1e; "Fig. 2c" does not exist);
     - the spin-polarization units;
     - ξ_vortex of 35 nm (main text) vs 45 nm (SM);
     - the Discussion sentence "in the strongly coupled case, the f-wave component becomes comparable," which should read weakly coupled/decoupled;
     - typos ("supposed," "below," "signature," "constituent").
   - Cite and compare more fully prior work on TaS2/NbSe2 misfits (e.g., Ref. [54] and related Ising-superconductivity studies in misfit compounds).
