Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Question addressed.** The manuscript asks whether the unconventional, multi-gap superconductivity reported in isolated 1H-TaS₂ (exfoliated monolayers, 4Hb-TaS₂) is intrinsic to the 1H layer or depends on its environment. To test this, the authors study the bulk misfit compound (SnS)₁.₁₅(TaS₂), in which SnS spacers are argued to decouple the 1H sheets electronically.
- **Combination of probes.** The work combines four techniques on one material:
  - transport (T_c ≈ 3.05 K, H_c2 anisotropy, in-plane H_c2 above the Pauli limit);
  - spin- and k_z-resolved ARPES;
  - STM/STS: a two-gap spectrum on the 1H termination (Δ₁ ≈ 490 µeV, Δ₂ ≈ 300 µeV) and a single gap on the SnS termination (≈ 440 µeV) with vortices;
  - a BCS tight-binding model in which interlayer hopping t⊥ suppresses singlet–triplet (s/f-wave) mixing, and with it the multi-gap feature.
- **Potential significance.**
  - A bulk, cleavable crystal hosting "monolayer-like" 1H-TaS₂ would be a valuable platform. It would allow bulk thermodynamics, ARPES and STM on effectively isolated Ising superconductors.
  - The claim of intrinsic superconductivity in the SnS layer is unusual and, if established, interesting.
- **Novelty is more limited than the title suggests.**
  - The two-gap STS spectrum closely reproduces the exfoliated-monolayer result (Simon et al., Nat. Commun. 2024: 0.25/0.46 meV). That work shares theory co-authors and a similar singlet–triplet interpretation.
  - Misfit compounds as decoupled 2D superconductors have precedent (e.g., Devarakonda et al., Science 2020).
  - (SnS)₁.₁₅TaS₂ has already been studied by STM/ARPES (Li et al., Adv. Mater. 2024).
  - The new elements are therefore:
    - the low-temperature STS in this host;
    - the SnS-termination gap;
    - the t⊥-dependent mixing model.
  - The central claim of a "mixed triplet–singlet order parameter" is inferred indirectly rather than demonstrated.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Multi-probe, internally coherent dataset on high-quality crystals.**
  - Sharp (00l) XRD reflections, HRTEM confirmation of the alternating stacking, and RRR ≈ 12.
  - A spatially uniform, fully gapped STS spectrum over 45 nm, with 150 averaged spectra.
- **Clean, hard-gap spectra.** Unlike 4Hb-TaS₂, the 1H spectrum shows no residual zero-bias DOS. This makes the sub-gap structure (kink near 300 µeV, two features in d²I/dV²) more convincing than in previous gapless systems.
- **Consistent Ising/2D phenomenology.**
  - In-plane H_c2 is about twice the Pauli limit.
  - The angular dependence of H_c2 follows the 2D Tinkham form.
  - T_c is enhanced toward the monolayer value.
  - The ARPES k_z scan along M–L shows no dispersion.
- **Symmetry-based, falsifiable theory.**
  - The model identifies minimal ingredients: Ising SOC/broken inversion, finite-range attraction with suppressed on-site attraction, and interlayer hopping.
  - It makes a qualitative prediction that could organise the literature: multi-gap features appear in decoupled systems and disappear in bulk 2H.
- **Intriguing SnS-layer superconductivity.**
  - A gap with vortices and CdGM-like core states is observed on the nominally semiconducting SnS termination.
  - This could motivate further work on emergent superconductivity in misfit spacer layers.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

- **(i) The two-gap spectroscopic evidence is not sufficient to support a distinct order-parameter claim.**
  - **Weak model comparison.**
    - The only evidence is a fit of a single 0.3 K spectrum to a sum of two isotropic BCS DOS (Eq. 5), compared against a single-gap fit.
    - The single-gap fit requires T_eff ≈ 900 mK, while the base temperature is 270 mK. The T_eff, Dynes broadening and weights N₁/N₂ of the two-gap fit are not reported.
    - A two-parameter-richer model fitting better is expected. No comparison is made with physically motivated alternatives, such as an anisotropic single gap or a distribution of gaps over the Γ and K pockets.
  - **Missing tests that discriminate the scenarios.**
    - No temperature dependence of Δ₁ and Δ₂ is shown. In the mixed-parity scenario both close at a single T_c with specific relative T-dependences; in a weakly coupled two-band picture (Table I) they would behave differently.
    - No field dependence of the individual gap features is analysed. Supp. Fig. 4 shows only the total suppression.
    - No momentum resolution (e.g., Bogoliubov QPI) is provided to locate the small gap on specific Fermi pockets.
    - No superconducting-tip measurement or careful energy-resolution analysis is given. The lock-in modulation is not stated for Fig. 4, and the claimed gaps differ by only ~190 µeV.
  - **Gap ratios.** 2Δ₁/k_BT_c ≈ 3.7 and 2Δ₂/k_BT_c ≈ 2.3 are ordinary multiband values. Nothing in the spectrum specifically points to triplet admixture rather than conventional multiband or anisotropic s-wave pairing.

- **(ii) The theory is not quantitatively or structurally connected to the measured system.**
  - **Wrong structural reference.**
    - The model is a 2H bilayer of *inverted* 1H layers, where t⊥ restores global inversion.
    - The authors themselves state that in (SnS)₁.₁₅TaS₂ all 1H layers have the *same* orientation and the crystal lacks inversion. Interlayer coupling here would therefore not restore inversion.
    - The proposed mechanism for suppressing mixing (sensitivity to global inversion) is thus not the relevant one for this material. Only the t⊥ = 0 limit is used, and the 2H comparison is not tested in this work.
  - **Mismatched energy scales.**
    - Model gaps are ~15–20 meV (main-text Fig. 5c peaks at eV/t ≈ 0.07–0.08 with t = 0.25 eV). The Supplementary Fig. 10 axis range differs from Fig. 5c, which is itself an inconsistency.
    - These gaps are ~40× larger than experiment, computed on a coarse 40×40 k-grid with 1 meV broadening.
    - The predicted small/large gap ratio on the K pockets is ~0.1, against ~0.6 measured.
    - The triplet/singlet ratio in the Fig. 5e inset (≲0.3) does not obviously match the text's claim that the weights are "comparable".
    - The parameters (λ = 0.3 eV > t = 0.25 eV) and the omission or treatment of the Γ pocket are not justified against DFT or ARPES.
  - **Alternative not excluded.**
    - The authors acknowledge (SM Sec. VII.C.3) that conventional 1/e(k) mixing also produces Γ vs K gap variation.
    - The data probe only t⊥ ≈ 0, so the argument that this alternative "does not depend on t⊥" cannot discriminate between the scenarios here.
  - **Technical errors and unclear statements.**
    - Eq. (9) uses V^(s) for Δ^(a).
    - The self-consistency equations (11)–(12) lack the tanh/temperature factor and the momentum sum.
    - "Spin-degenerate bands of a single 1H layer" is contradictory under Ising SOC.
    - "Imbalance of gap strength on K and K′" is unclear. Time-reversal symmetry fixes |Δ| at K↑ = K′↓, so the splitting should be between the inner and outer spin-split pockets.
    - The Discussion reads "in the strongly coupled case, the f-wave component becomes comparable", which presumably should say "weakly".
    - "On-site Coulomb repulsion enhances the triplet component" is asserted, but the model only reduces V₀; no repulsion is included.

- **(iii) The "intrinsic, independent" SnS superconductivity and the "isolated 1H" picture are in tension, and both are under-supported.**
  - **Evidence points to a common condensate.**
    - The SnS gap (440 µeV) is close to Δ₁, the STM critical field is the same (~3 kG) on both terminations, and coherence lengths are similar.
    - Transport shows a single sharp transition.
    - These observations point naturally to one condensate shared by hybridised or proximitised TaS₂ and SnS states. Independent superconductors would generally have different T_c and H_c2.
  - **Arguments against proximity are not decisive.**
    - Vortices also appear in proximitised layers.
    - The 4Hb comparison involves a Mott-insulating 1T layer, a very different situation.
    - The cited theory of reduced induced gaps under Fermi-surface mismatch depends on interface transparency, which is not estimated.
    - The internal-pressure hypothesis (SnS superconducts above 40 GPa) is speculative and not quantified.
  - **Effect on the 1H interpretation.**
    - If SnS is metallic (finite DOS at E_F) and superconducting, the 1H layers are not electronically isolated in the superconducting channel.
    - Well-known Sn→TaS₂ charge transfer in such misfits also modifies the Fermi surface.
    - A second gap could then arise from TaS₂–SnS band hybridisation or proximity (McMillan-type) rather than from singlet–triplet mixing. This alternative is not considered.

- **(iv) Decoupling evidence and data consistency need substantial strengthening.**
  - **Spin-ARPES does not test decoupling.**
    - Because all 1H layers share one orientation and the bulk lacks inversion, out-of-plane spin polarisation is allowed regardless of interlayer coupling.
    - The reported polarisation axis (±0.5 "%") is either mislabelled or extremely small. No error bars or sign reversal between K and K′ are shown.
  - **Limited k_z evidence.**
    - The k_z test uses a feature at E_B ≈ −0.35 eV at M rather than Fermi-level states.
    - The quoted k_z values appear inconsistent with Eq. (5) and V₀ = 9 eV: 25 eV → 2.96 Å⁻¹ seems to neglect the work function, and 49–59 eV is said to span 1.5 BZ. The c-axis repeat and k_z calibration need to be stated.
  - **"Strong transport anisotropy" not measured.** The claim rests only on the H_c2 anisotropy ratio of 19, which is moderate; ρ_c is not measured.
  - **Questionable H_c2 two-gap fit.**
    - The fit uses an orbital dirty-limit model for the in-plane field, where H_c2 is Pauli/Ising-limited.
    - The fitted BCS coupling constants differ between field orientations (λ₁₁ = 0.88 vs 1.37; D₁ = 11.01 vs 0.67). Coupling constants should be intrinsic and orientation-independent, which undermines the claimed transport support for two gaps.
    - Upward curvature of H_c2(T) can also arise from 2D/Ising or dimensional-crossover effects.
  - **Numerical inconsistencies.**
    - T_c is quoted as 3.05, 3.1 and 3.14 K.
    - ξ_vortex is 35 ± 5 nm in the main text but 45 ± 10 nm in the SM; ξ from H_c2 = 0.59 T is ≈ 24 nm, not 30 nm.
    - The fitted residual resistivity of 145 µΩ cm contradicts the ~35 µΩ cm seen in Fig. 2a. C = 3.7 ± 5 mΩ cm has an error bar larger than the value.
    - The Bloch–Grüneisen integrand contains a typo, (1 − e⁻¹).
    - The STM H_c2 of 3 kG at ~0.5 K is described as in "excellent agreement" with transport, which gives ~0.5 T.
  - **Literature tension not addressed.** MBE monolayer 1H-TaS₂ shows a *single*, f-wave-like gap. This directly challenges the conclusion that multigap superconductivity is intrinsic to isolated 1H layers and needs discussion.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

- **Discriminating spectroscopy.**
  - Provide T-dependent STS (0.3 K → T_c) on both terminations, tracking Δ₁(T) and Δ₂(T) separately and comparing with mixed-parity versus weakly coupled two-band predictions.
  - Provide field-dependent STS (in-plane and out-of-plane), exploiting the distinct Zeeman/Ising response expected for the triplet component.
  - Use Dynes-broadened fits with all parameters reported (T_eff, Γ, N₁/N₂, modulation amplitude), plus an anisotropic-gap model and statistical model comparison (e.g., reduced χ², AIC/BIC).
  - Use superconducting tips if possible.
  - Attempt Bogoliubov QPI or high-resolution ARPES to assign the gaps to the Γ/K pockets or the spin-split K sub-pockets.
  - Add bulk probes, e.g., specific heat C(T, H) or penetration depth, to confirm the gap structure is not surface-specific.

- **Make the theory appropriate and quantitative.**
  - Model same-orientation 1H layers separated by a SnS spacer, including possible TaS₂–SnS hybridisation and charge transfer.
  - Use DFT/ARPES-constrained bands, including the Γ pocket and a realistic Ising splitting.
  - Tune interactions so gaps are ~0.3–0.5 meV, use a k-mesh fine enough to resolve them, and compare the computed dI/dV directly with the data, including the gap ratio.
  - Show explicitly that a conventional anisotropic s-wave (1 + e(k)) solution cannot reproduce the spectrum at t⊥ = 0, and identify an observable that separates the two scenarios (e.g., impurity sensitivity, field-angle dependence, in-plane H_c2 beyond the Ising value).
  - Correct Eqs. (9), (11)–(12) and the inconsistent statements listed above.

- **Resolve the SnS superconductivity question.**
  - Measure the local T_c and Δ(T) on SnS versus 1H terminations.
  - Study steps and terraces of different SnS/TaS₂ stacking to look for a proximity length dependence.
  - Characterise SnS-derived bands and charge transfer by ARPES/DFT.
  - Discuss whether TaS₂–SnS hybridisation or proximity could itself generate the second gap on the 1H termination.
  - Temper the "independent superconductor" and "internal pressure" claims unless supported quantitatively.

- **Strengthen decoupling evidence and correct inconsistencies; adjust claims.**
  - Measure ρ_c/ρ_ab.
  - Show k_z dispersion at E_F with explicit k_z calibration (c-axis repeat, work function).
  - Present spin-ARPES at both K and K′ with error bars and correct units, noting that spin polarisation alone does not prove decoupling.
  - Refit H_c2 with a model including Ising SOC and Pauli limiting, using orientation-independent coupling constants.
  - Unify T_c and ξ values and fix the resistivity-fit parameters, figure cross-references (Fig. 2c, Fig. 4 → Fig. 1 panels) and typos.
  - Discuss the MBE-monolayer single-gap result.
  - Retitle or rephrase so that singlet–triplet mixing is presented as a proposed interpretation consistent with the data, not a demonstrated order parameter.
