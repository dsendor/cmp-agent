Referee report

Manuscript: "Mixed Triplet-Singlet Order Parameter in Decoupled Superconducting 1H Monolayers of Transition-Metal Dichalcogenides" (arXiv:2509.13303v1)

1. Summary of the submission

The authors study the misfit compound (SnS)1.15(TaS2), grown by chemical vapor transport. They combine resistivity and upper critical field measurements, spin-resolved and photon-energy-dependent ARPES, and low-temperature STM/STS on both cleaved terminations. Their main claims are:

(a) The SnS layers electronically decouple the TaS2 into isolated 1H sheets. The evidence offered is the absence of kz dispersion at M, spin-valley locking seen in spin-ARPES, a Hc2 anisotropy of about 19, an in-plane Hc2 about twice the Pauli limit, and a Tc of about 3.1 K, close to that of monolayer TaS2.

(b) Tunneling on the 1H termination shows a two-gap spectrum, with Delta1 = 490 ueV and Delta2 = 300 ueV. The upward curvature of Hc2(T) is fitted by a two-band dirty-limit model.

(c) A BCS mean-field model of a 2H bilayer gives a multi-gap tunneling DOS when the interlayer hopping t_perp is small and the pairing interaction is finite-range with suppressed on-site attraction. The model has Ising SOC, NN and NNN attraction, and a tunable t_perp. In this model the multi-gap structure comes from A1g (s-wave singlet) / A2u (f-wave triplet) mixing, which interlayer coupling suppresses. The title and abstract present the order parameter as a mixed singlet-triplet state.

(d) The SnS layer hosts an intrinsic superconducting gap of 440 ueV that the authors say is not proximity induced.

The material platform is attractive, and the low-temperature STS on the 1H termination is clean and spatially uniform. The question, whether multi-gap features in 1H TMDs are intrinsic to the isolated layer and tied to inversion breaking, is timely. My view is that (a) is reasonably supported, with some caveats. The specific experimental claim in (b) needs considerably more characterization before it can carry the weight placed on it. The singlet-triplet attribution in (c), which is the title claim, is not established by the data: it is one model that can produce two features in the DOS, tested against no prediction that would tell it apart from conventional alternatives. The intrinsic-SnS claim in (d) rests on arguments that do not exclude proximity, and it is also in tension with the interpretation of (b). I recommend major revision.

2. Major issues

2.1 The title claim is not tested. The only experimental input to the singlet-triplet attribution is a tunneling DOS with two features, together with a literature correlation between multi-gap spectra and weak interlayer coupling. A momentum-anisotropic or multiband s-wave gap gives the same DOS. NbSe2 is the standard example, with different gaps on the Gamma and K pockets, and the CDW-reconstructed Fermi surface is another obvious source of anisotropy. The supplement concedes that mixing 1 and e(k), both A1g, "can also lead to a variation of the gap" between the K and Gamma pockets. It then dismisses this with the statement that such mixing "is not expected to depend strongly on interlayer coupling", which is asserted rather than shown. The theory therefore needs at least the following control calculations:
  - lambda -> 0 at t_perp = 0. This removes inversion breaking, and with it the triplet channel, inside the same model. Does the two-gap feature disappear?
  - The same t_perp sweep with the triplet channel switched off by hand, to show that a purely A1g anisotropic gap does not respond to t_perp in the same way.
Experimentally, the authors should identify and attempt at least one measurement that separates the two scenarios. Candidates are the in-plane-field dependence of the two gap features in STS, which differs for the singlet and Ising-protected triplet components; the temperature dependence of both features; and the sensitivity of the smaller gap to nonmagnetic disorder, since an f-wave admixture is not protected by Anderson's theorem. Without some such test, the title and abstract should be reworded to say that the data are consistent with, and motivate, a singlet-triplet scenario, not that they demonstrate a mixed order parameter.

2.2 The two-gap STS evidence needs more support. Fig. 4a and S2 compare a two-gap fit to a single isotropic BCS fit. The single-gap fit needs an effective temperature of 900 mK at a base temperature of 270 mK, which already signals that something other than thermal smearing broadens the spectrum. The following should be reported:
  - All fit parameters for both models, including T_eff, any Dynes Gamma, and the weights N1 and N2, with uncertainties and a goodness-of-fit measure. The two-gap model has two more free parameters, so a better fit alone is expected.
  - A fit with an anisotropic single gap, for example a continuous distribution of gap values or an angular form, as a third alternative. If that fits as well, "two gaps" should be "an anisotropic gap".
  - The lock-in modulation amplitude and the effective energy resolution for the low-energy spectra. The methods give no Vmod for Fig. 4, and the frequency is 907 Hz in the methods but 870 Hz in the Fig. 1 captions.
  - Reproducibility across tips, cleaves and samples. S3 is 150 spectra along one 45 nm line.
  - The temperature dependence of Delta1 and Delta2 from STS. This is the standard test for two-band superconductivity, where both close at the same Tc with different shapes, and it is cheap to do with the existing setup.
  - The field dependence of the smaller gap. S4 shows spectra at 0, 1, 2 and 3 kG, but the small-gap feature is not analyzed. In two-band systems the smaller gap is typically suppressed much faster.
The "kink" at 300 ueV in Fig. 4c is a shoulder on the main d2I/dV2 peak, and the two features are marked only at positive bias. The authors should show that the feature appears symmetrically at negative bias and that it is robust.

2.3 Proximity from the SnS layer is an alternative explanation for the second gap on the 1H termination. The authors argue that SnS superconducts intrinsically with Delta = 440 ueV. If so, the exposed 1H layer sits directly on a superconducting SnS layer, and the tunneling DOS of a thin bilayer of two different superconductors in good contact generically shows two features, McMillan-type. That would give a second gap with no reference to singlet-triplet physics. The SnS gap lies between Delta2 and Delta1 (Fig. 4c), which fits this picture. Conversely, if SnS does not superconduct intrinsically, its gap is induced and the "independent superconductor" claim fails. Either way, the 1H/SnS coupling has to enter the analysis of the 1H spectrum. As it stands, the manuscript claims both that the layers are electronically decoupled and that each layer's gap is unaffected by the other, without showing that these are mutually consistent.

2.4 The arguments for intrinsic superconductivity in SnS do not exclude proximity. The arguments given are: the critical field is the same in both layers (S4, 3 kG); vortices are seen on the SnS termination; the coherence length is the same as that from Hc2; there is only one SnS gap; the gap is close in size to Delta1; and 4Hb-TaS2 provides a contrast. A single SnS bilayer is well under a nanometer thick, far below xi of about 30 nm. A proximitized atomically thin layer in good contact with the 1H layer would show the same Hc2, would show the vortex lattice of the underlying superconductor, would share its coherence length, and could have an induced gap close to the parent gap. Most of the listed observations are therefore what proximity predicts, not evidence against it. The 4Hb comparison is weak, because the 1T layer there is a correlated insulator with a CDW gap, while the SnS surface here has finite DOS at EF. Charge transfer between SnS and TaS2 is well documented in misfit compounds and would be a natural origin for that DOS. The authors attribute it to the SnS layer itself because it is spatially uniform, but uniformity does not establish that. The Stanescu-Das Sarma argument [78] applies to weak tunnel coupling with Fermi-surface mismatch, which is not obviously the regime of a covalently bonded misfit interface. Discriminating measurements would be: the temperature dependence of the SnS gap compared with the 1H gaps (an intrinsic SnS superconductor with an independent Tc would show it); vortex core profiles on both terminations measured in the same field and compared; and tunneling at SnS step edges or on regions with different numbers of SnS layers, if available. The "internal pressure" explanation, by analogy with SnS superconducting above 40 GPa, is speculative and should be labeled as such or removed.

2.5 The Hc2 analysis is internally inconsistent. The two-band dirty-limit fit (SM Eq. 3, Table I) returns different coupling constants for the two field orientations: lambda11 = 0.88 vs 1.37 and lambda12 = 0.35 vs 0.49. Diffusivities may depend on orientation, but the BCS coupling matrix is a property of the material and must be shared by both fits. A joint fit with common lambda_ij is needed. In addition:
  - For H || ab in an Ising superconductor with an anisotropy of 19, in-plane Hc2 is set mainly by spin (Ising/Pauli) physics and by 2D geometry, not by orbital depairing. An orbital Gurevich-type model is the wrong functional form for that orientation, so the fit says little about two gaps.
  - Upward curvature of Hc2(T) has several known origins besides two bands [63-66], including dimensional crossover in layered systems, and the authors cite them. Given that the material contains two kinds of layers, both possibly superconducting, the curvature could equally reflect the SnS/TaS2 two-component structure, not two gaps within 1H.
  - The internal tension with the STS interpretation needs discussion. The Hc2 model assumes the dirty limit, in which intraband scattering averages out gap anisotropy and suppresses a sign-changing triplet admixture. The STS interpretation requires a clean enough limit for a strongly anisotropic s+f gap to survive. The authors should estimate the mean free path from rho0 and compare it with xi.
  - In S1 the H || c curves at the lowest temperatures show abrupt steps between 0.2 and 0.3 T. These look like flux jumps or contact artifacts, and they directly affect the 50% criterion at low T. They should be explained.
  - The Pauli-limit factor is quoted as 1.95. Using 1.86 Tc with Tc = 3.05 K and the extrapolated 10.68 T, I get about 1.88. More importantly, an enhancement of about 2 is modest for Ising superconductivity in a "true monolayer" limit, where gated MoS2 and monolayer NbSe2 show much larger ratios. Please discuss what this implies for the degree of decoupling or for the strength of Ising SOC.

2.6 The model and the material differ in a key symmetry. The theoretical mechanism is that a second, inverted layer (2H stacking) restores global inversion, and that increasing t_perp suppresses the triplet admixture. The manuscript states that in the misfit compound the 1H layers have the same orientation, so the crystal "lacks overall inversion symmetry", and the layers are shifted by half a unit cell along b. In that stacking, interlayer coupling does not restore inversion, and singlet-triplet mixing stays symmetry-allowed at any t_perp. The 2H bilayer model is therefore not a model of this compound at finite t_perp. Within the authors' own picture, the misfit stacking should not need decoupling to keep the admixture. This weakens the claimed link between the misfit result and the "multi-gap correlates with decoupling" narrative, and it should be addressed directly. Since the structure is incommensurate along one axis, the symmetry statements also need to be made more carefully.

2.7 The theory parameters need justification and quantitative comparison.
  - The hopping t_perp is k-independent. In H-type TMDs, interlayer hopping is strongly momentum-dependent: large near Gamma and strongly suppressed at K by symmetry. Since the two-gap feature in the model lives on the K pockets, a constant t_perp overstates coupling where it matters.
  - In the model the two-gap feature disappears at t_perp of about 0.8 lambda, roughly 0.24 eV for lambda = 0.3 eV. The authors should state the realistic effective t_perp for bulk 2H-TaS2 at the relevant pockets, together with a quantitative ARPES upper bound on t_perp in the misfit (from S6). This is needed to show that 2H falls on the single-gap side of the crossover and the misfit on the multi-gap side. Otherwise the model does not actually connect to the claimed experimental correlation.
  - lambda = 0.3 eV exceeds t = 0.25 eV. Please justify this against the known spin splitting of TaS2.
  - The scales of Fig. 5c,d (coherence peak near 0.075 to 0.09 eV/t) and S10 to S12 (peak near 0.017 eV/t) differ by a factor of about 5. Please give the parameters used for Fig. 5, and explain why the main-text and SM calculations differ.
  - The model gives a gap ratio of order 0.1 on the K pockets (SM) and a weak low-energy shoulder, while the experiment gives Delta2/Delta1 of about 0.6 with a much stronger feature. The comparison is qualitative at best and should be presented that way.
  - The inset of Fig. 5e gives triplet/singlet ratios of about 0.27 and 0.08 at t_perp = 0. The text describes these weights as "comparable".
  - With V0 < V1 = V2 on a triangular lattice, E-irrep states (chiral d+id or p+ip) are commonly competitive or favored. Please state whether the self-consistent solutions were started from generic initial conditions and whether time-reversal-breaking or E-irrep solutions were checked and found higher in free energy.
  - The physical statement about K and K' needs correcting. The main text says the s+f superposition creates "an imbalance of the gap strength on K and K' points." Time reversal relates (K, up) to (K', down), so the gap magnitude of a given band cannot differ between K and K'. What differs is the gap on the two spin-split bands, Delta_s +/- Delta_f. The SM panels plot spin-resolved components, which explains the K/K' asymmetry in each panel, but the main-text wording is misleading.
  - The main text describes the pairing interaction inconsistently: "on-site Coulomb repulsion can significantly enhance the triplet component" in Sec. III, "weak on-site repulsion" in the Discussion, and "suppress the on-site attraction" elsewhere. The SM uses an attractive V0 = 0.25t. Please describe the interaction consistently.
  - The model omits the CDW. QCDW = 0.3543 Q_Bragg is noticeably different from the 1/3 of 2H-TaS2. Since the CDW reconstructs the K-pocket Fermi surface, it is a natural alternative source of gap anisotropy and should at least be discussed. The argument from [41] that interlayer coupling changes the band structure only slightly does not address this.

2.8 The case for decoupling should be tightened.
  - Out-of-plane spin polarization at K in spin-ARPES does not show decoupling or global inversion breaking. ARPES is surface sensitive and sees the hidden spin polarization of the top 1H layer even in centrosymmetric bulk 2H compounds, as shown in [39]. This evidence should not be listed as confirming decoupling.
  - The polarization axis in Fig. 3 is labeled P [%] with a range of +/-0.5. If this is literally percent, the polarization is far too small to be convincing. If it is a fraction, the label is wrong. Please clarify and give error bars.
  - The kz-independence argument (S6) uses V0 = 9 eV taken from another compound [41]. Please give the c-axis lattice constant of the misfit used to assign M and L, show that the photon-energy range really spans these points, and give a quantitative bound on any kz dispersion or M-point splitting.
  - The Discussion cites "strong transport anisotropy", but only Hc2 anisotropy is shown. A rho_c/rho_ab measurement would directly support the decoupling claim.
  - A Tc close to the monolayer value is suggestive, but charge transfer from SnS is an obvious alternative cause of the enhancement from 0.8 K. Please discuss it.

2.9 The literature correlation is thin. The empirical correlation between multi-gap spectra and weak interlayer coupling rests on a few heterogeneous datasets: a bulk 2H STM gap [73], exfoliated monolayers [35], MBE monolayers [53], and 4Hb. The MBE monolayer reportedly shows a single gap, which runs against the claimed correlation; it is set aside on the grounds of its low Tc. The 4Hb evidence is "residual DOS consistent with a destroyed second gap", which is indirect. The manuscript should state these limitations plainly and not describe multi-gap superconductivity as "an intrinsic property of isolated 1H-TaS2 layers".

3. Minor issues and inconsistencies

- Tc is given as 3.1 K (abstract), 3.05 K (text and fit), and "around 3.14 K" (Fig. 2a caption). Please harmonize, and give the transition width and criterion.
- The coherence length is 35 +/- 5 nm from the vortex in the main text but 45 +/- 10 nm in SM Sec. V. Taking xi from the decay length of a phenomenological exponential fit to the zero-bias DOS is only approximate, and this should be said.
- The STM critical field (3 kG at about 0.5 K, S4) is said to agree "excellently" with transport. Fig. 2b gives Hc2 || c of about 0.5 T at T/Tc of about 0.16, and the extrapolated value is 0.59 T. Correspondingly, xi_Hc2 of about 30 nm at 400 mK implies Hc2 of about 0.37 T, while 0.5 to 0.59 T gives 24 to 26 nm. Please reconcile, and state that the criteria differ (50% resistive vs disappearance of the STM gap).
- SM resistivity: the residual resistivity rho_i,0 = 145 uOhm cm conflicts with rho of about 35 uOhm cm at 10 K in Fig. 2a. The prefactor C = 3.7 +/- 5 mOhm cm has an uncertainty larger than its value. rho_s of about 23 mOhm cm is about 60 times the 300 K resistivity, so the saturation is negligible over the measured range and the parallel-resistor discussion of "saturating resistivity" is not supported by Fig. 2a, which is close to linear. In the Bloch-Grueneisen integrand, (1 - e^-1) should read (1 - e^-x).
- Fig. 2a caption: "1.7 K >= T >= 300 K" is reversed, and the data extend below 1.7 K.
- Fig. 4 caption: the spectra are said to be "shown in Fig. 1c" and "Fig. 1e". These should be 1d and 1f (topographies). Fig. 1e caption: "same location as (c)" should be (d).
- Sec. II text: "we present the first derivative of the spectra" while the figure shows d2I/dV2. Both describe the derivative of dI/dV, but please use one convention.
- Discussion: "In contrast, in the strongly coupled case, the f-wave component becomes comparable with the s-wave". This should be the weakly coupled, or decoupled, case. The error inverts the main conclusion as written.
- Sec. III: "minimal, single-orbital tight-binding model ... of a single 2H-TaS2 layer" is followed by a bilayer model. Please clarify.
- Footnote [71], "The superposition of the two is obviously an eigen-function of inversion", is unclear. Please explain which superposition is meant.
- SM Sec. VII.C.2: Figs. 11 and 12 are described as "two isolated H layers" at t_perp = 0.1 and 0.2 eV, which is not isolated. "The increase of the singlet-triplet mixing has diminished" should read "the singlet-triplet mixing has diminished". The panel titles read "t=0.1 eV" where t_perp is meant.
- The SM figure reference "Figs. VII C 1, 8, 9" is a broken cross-reference. There are several typos ("signlet", "mimcs", "Enerrgy", "bellow", "suppose to", "miss-match", "highe", "denity", "constituate", "signiture").
- Please give sample information: number of crystals measured by each technique; whether STM, ARPES and transport used the same growth batch; composition (EDS or WDS); and in-plane or off-axis XRD or TEM confirming the misfit periodicity and excluding (SnS)(TaS2)2 or other stacking variants. A Tc distribution across samples would also help.
- Bulk evidence for the CDW (XRD superlattice, Raman, or ARPES gap) would clarify why no resistive anomaly is seen while the STM FFT shows CDW peaks. Please also comment on the incommensurate QCDW.
- Ref. [34] is garbled, and Refs. [39] and [41] contain encoding errors.

4. Assessment

The material is interesting, and the measurements are mostly of good quality. A well-documented two-gap spectrum on decoupled 1H-TaS2 in a bulk crystal, with a solid analysis of the 1H/SnS interplay, would be a useful contribution on its own. As submitted, the manuscript reaches for a stronger conclusion than its evidence supports. The singlet-triplet admixture is inferred from a DOS shape that conventional anisotropic or multiband s-wave pairing, or a proximity bilayer, reproduces equally well. The model's key symmetry mechanism (inverted 2H stacking) does not match the stacking of this compound. The SnS "intrinsic superconductivity" argument relies on observations that proximity predicts, and the Hc2 two-band fit is internally inconsistent. I would reconsider the manuscript after the authors (i) add STS temperature and field dependence of both gap features and of the SnS gap, (ii) address the proximity and bilayer alternative for both terminations, (iii) redo the Hc2 analysis with shared coupling constants and an appropriate in-plane model, (iv) add the theoretical control calculations and realistic parameter estimates described in 2.1 and 2.7, and (v) tone down the title and abstract unless a discriminating test is added.

Recommendation: major revision.
