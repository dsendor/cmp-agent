Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Topic.** The manuscript reports ARPES and DFT on Co1/4TaSe2 (P63/mmc, Co on the 2a site, A-type AFM along c, TN = 178 K). It claims observation of g-wave altermagnetic spin splitting in a layered, intercalated TMD.
- **Why it matters.** Confirmed layered altermagnets with high ordering temperatures are scarce. A van der Waals altermagnet would be valuable for heterostructures, proximity effects with superconductors or topological materials, and moiré engineering. The topic is timely and of broad interest.
- **Novelty is limited.** The conceptual step is incremental relative to the isostructural Co1/4NbSe2, for which the following already exist:
  - the symmetry analysis;
  - neutron, µSR and susceptibility characterization;
  - DFT and ARPES claims of Γ′–M′ splitting at kz = π/2c, including work from overlapping authors (refs. 37–40).
- **Magnetic characterization is not new.** The structure and ordering of Co1/4TaSe2 itself were established in ref. 41.
- **What is new.** The new content is therefore mainly the ARPES evidence. Its strength determines the paper's significance.
- **Possible added value from Ta.** Stronger SOC of Ta 5d relative to Nb 4d could make the TaSe2 analogue more interesting. The manuscript does not exploit or discuss this.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Symmetry argument.** The case for altermagnetism is sound in principle:
  - the opposite-spin Co sublattices are related by a mirror or screw operation, not by translation or inversion;
  - this yields the expected bulk g-wave form, with nodal planes at kz = 0, kz = π/c and the vertical Γ-K-H-A planes.
- **Sample quality.** The crystals appear good. Susceptibility, resistivity (dρ/dT kink) and heat capacity give a consistent TN = 178 K, in agreement with ref. 41.
- **Data coverage.** The ARPES Fermi surfaces and constant-energy contours at kz ≈ π/2c agree reasonably with DFT (Γ′ pocket, petals, dog-bone pockets around M′). The data set includes:
  - azimuthal rotation from Γ′–K′ toward Γ′–M′;
  - photon-energy (kz) variation;
  - temperature cycling across TN.
- **Qualitative consistency.** The trends follow expectations for a g-wave altermagnet:
  - broadening of the near-EF feature grows when rotating toward Γ′–M′;
  - it shrinks toward kz ≈ 0;
  - it is suppressed above TN.
- **Platform value.** If substantiated, the work extends the altermagnet family beyond MnTe and CrSb to an exfoliable, chemically tunable platform.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**(i) The quantitative evidence for the spin splitting is internally inconsistent and below resolution.**
- **Theory–experiment mismatch.** DFT gives a momentum separation of about 0.01 Å⁻¹ between the split Fermi crossings near M′. The Voigt fit of the 55 eV MDC gives about 0.09 Å⁻¹, roughly nine times larger. The manuscript does not acknowledge or explain this discrepancy. As presented, the fitted splitting is not "consistent with our DFT results."
- **Resolution claim does not add up.** The quoted "instrumental (Gaussian) width" of about 0.08 Å⁻¹ conflicts with the stated 0.1° angular resolution.
  - At hν = 55 eV, 0.1° corresponds to Δk ≈ 0.006 Å⁻¹.
  - The 0.08 Å⁻¹ width must therefore be dominated by intrinsic lifetime, kz, disorder or surface broadening, not by the instrument.
  - The fitted peaks are separated by roughly one linewidth, so the decomposition is near the Rayleigh limit and weakly constrained.
- **Fit statistics are missing.** There are no parameter uncertainties, no residuals, and no statement of which parameters were fixed or free. There is no single- versus double-peak model comparison (F-test, AIC/BIC). The "clear hump" in Fig. 2(h) is a weak shoulder of modest amplitude relative to the plotted noise band.
- **Mismatched comparison cut.** The 48 eV MDC is taken "along Γ−M," not at an equivalent momentum on the same band. Band velocity and position differ at kz = 0, so the single-versus-double lineshape comparison is not like-for-like.

**(ii) Alternative explanations for the broadening and "double-peak" structure are not excluded.**
- **No spin resolution.** No spin-resolved ARPES or other spin-sensitive probe is presented. All spin assignments rest on DFT overlays, including "complete spin polarization" of the −150 meV contours.
- **kz broadening is a confound.**
  - Final-state kz broadening is largest where the band disperses along kz, i.e. near kz = π/2c.
  - It is smallest at kz = 0, where dE/dkz = 0.
  - The reported trend (broad at 55 eV, narrow at 48 eV) is therefore also expected from kz broadening alone.
  - The same applies to the rotation-dependent broadening if kz dispersion is anisotropic in-plane.
- **Other non-magnetic sources of a second feature:**
  - bilayer (interlayer) splitting intrinsic to 2H stacking;
  - backfolding from the 2×2 Co superlattice;
  - surface versus bulk components;
  - inhomogeneous Co occupancy or vacancies at the cleaved surface.
  
  A non-magnetic or paramagnetic-cell DFT comparison is needed to show the split pair is absent without magnetic order.
- **kz assignment is unsupported.** No inner potential, photon-energy map, or kz-dispersion data are shown, although the Introduction claims kz-dependent measurements.
  - With c ≈ 12.45 Å and typical inner potentials, the 48→55 eV change may correspond to Δkz closer to π/c than π/2c.
  - The identification of "kz ≈ 0 / π/4c / π/2c" is therefore critical and unverified.

**(iii) The temperature-dependent evidence is qualitative and partly contradictory.**
- **Too few temperatures.** Only two temperatures are measured, 7 K and 200 K, the latter just 22 K above TN.
  - There is no intermediate series tracking the splitting or "gaps" against an order-parameter-like dependence.
  - Thermal broadening, possible surface aging or adsorption, and Fermi-level or chemical-potential drifts during cycling are not controlled. No re-cooling reversibility check is presented.
- **Gaps are claimed but not shown.** The abstract claims "closing of energy gaps," yet no gap is identified, quantified, or tied to a DFT magnetic gap. The EDC changes in Fig. 3(f) are attributed to a mix of thermal broadening, matrix elements, splitting suppression, and gap closing, without discrimination.
- **The Γ′ pocket is described inconsistently.**
  - It is called "electron-like" on p. 4 and "hole-like" on p. 6.
  - The Results say intensity is "potentially" shifted downward; the Conclusion asserts "closing of the small Γ′ pocket."
- **Linewidth trend needs explanation.** A "narrower band" above TN, despite higher temperature, needs quantitative linewidth analysis. Without it the observation cannot be interpreted.

**(iv) The theoretical modelling and physical interpretation are underdeveloped.**
- **DFT setup is incomplete:**
  - no statement on spin–orbit coupling, which matters for Ta 5d;
  - no DFT+U, and no sensitivity of the splitting to U;
  - no calculated Co moment to compare with the neutron value of 1.35 µB;
  - no mention of band renormalization or EF shifts applied for the overlays;
  - no non-magnetic reference calculation.
- **SOC and the spin-group picture.** With SOC and moments along c, spin is no longer a good quantum number and nodal planes may be lifted. This should be discussed, especially for comparison with the Nb analogue.
- **Speculative discussion.** RKKY coupling, the "dual localized–itinerant" character, "magnetic gaps," and the "absence of precursor fluctuations" are asserted without supporting data. No temperature series above TN is shown, so the absence of fluctuations cannot be assessed.
- **Transport interpretation.** The resistivity argument is loosely phrased. The stronger T-dependence below TN reflects freezing-out of spin-disorder scattering.
- **Differentiation from Co1/4NbSe2.** The paper does not compare the splitting magnitude and location with the NbSe2 compound, so its contribution beyond refs. 38 and 40 is unclear.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Provide direct or more discriminating evidence of spin splitting.**
   - Spin-resolved ARPES along Γ′–M′ at kz ≈ π/2c would be the most direct test; comparing ±kM′ directions, related by the altermagnetic symmetry, would add contrast.
   - Alternatively, use magnetic circular or linear dichroism, or laser or low-hν ARPES with better kz and k resolution.
   - Compare the same band at the same k∥ across kz, and within the nodal Γ′–K′ plane, as an internal control.
2. **Make the quantitative analysis rigorous and self-consistent.**
   - Reconcile the 0.09 Å⁻¹ fit with the 0.01 Å⁻¹ DFT prediction, and correct or justify the stated "instrumental width."
   - Report full fit parameters with uncertainties and residuals, plus single- versus double-peak model selection.
   - Simulate ARPES spectra from DFT, including kz broadening (finite photoelectron escape depth) and the measured linewidth, to show whether the predicted splitting can produce the observed lineshape.
3. **Establish the kz assignment and exclude non-magnetic origins.**
   - Show a full photon-energy-dependent map with the inner potential.
   - Present non-magnetic and paramagnetic DFT, with and without SOC, and DFT+U, to show that the split pair near M′ is uniquely magnetic.
   - Discuss bilayer splitting, superlattice backfolding, and surface Co termination or occupancy; core-level or LEED/STM characterization of the cleaved surface would help.
4. **Strengthen the temperature study and tighten the presentation.**
   - Measure a temperature series through TN, ideally with re-cooling, and extract splitting and linewidth versus T, compared with the magnetic order parameter.
   - Quantify any gaps and connect them to specific DFT features.
   - Moderate speculative claims (RKKY, precursor fluctuations) or support them with data.
   - Add explicit comparison with Co1/4NbSe2 to highlight what is new.
   - Fix inconsistencies:
     - the electron-like versus hole-like Γ′ pocket;
     - "gray diamonds in Fig. 2(d)" should read Fig. 3(d);
     - the "APRES" typos;
     - DFT c = 12.47 Å versus experimental 12.4535 Å;
     - the abstract's "closing of energy gaps" versus the hedged Results.
