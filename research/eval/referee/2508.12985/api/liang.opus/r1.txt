Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Topic and claim.** The manuscript reports ARPES and DFT evidence for g-wave altermagnetic spin splitting in the Co-intercalated TMD Co1/4TaSe2 (P6₃/mmc, Co on 2a, type-A AFM, T_N ≈ 178 K). The topic is timely. Layered, van der Waals altermagnets with T_N well above 77 K are scarce, and such systems are attractive for heterostructures, moiré engineering and spintronics.
- **Symmetry argument.**
  - The two Co sublattices at (0,0,0) and (0,0,½) are not related by inversion or by a pure lattice translation.
  - They are related by the 6₃ screw / m_z (z = ¼) operations.
  - This makes the compound a symmetry-allowed bulk g-wave altermagnet, analogous to MnTe and CrSb but layered.
- **Novelty is incremental relative to prior work.**
  - The isostructural Co1/4NbSe2 has already been identified as an altermagnet, with ARPES evidence of splitting along Γ′–M′ at k_z = π/2c, by overlapping author groups (Refs. 37, 38, 40).
  - The magnetic structure, moment (1.35 μ_B) and T_N of Co1/4TaSe2 were already established by neutron diffraction (Ref. 41).
  - The new elements are therefore the substitution Nb → Ta (stronger SOC, slightly higher T_N) and the ARPES/DFT comparison.
  - The manuscript does not articulate what physics is gained beyond the Nb analogue, for example the role of Ta 5d SOC or differences in splitting magnitude.
- **Strength of the central evidence.**
  - The key experimental signature is a shoulder in a spin-integrated MDC, at a separation comparable to the quoted resolution width.
  - The DFT-predicted splitting at the relevant crossing is ~0.01 Å⁻¹.
  - The significance of the result therefore hinges on whether the spectroscopic evidence is unambiguous, which at present is doubtful.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Material and symmetry.**
  - The material is a credible altermagnet candidate on symmetry grounds.
  - The magnetic structure is independently established by neutrons (Ref. 41).
  - T_N is confirmed here by susceptibility, resistivity (dρ/dT) and heat capacity.
- **Data quality and DFT agreement.**
  - High-quality synchrotron ARPES data (SSRL 5-2, 7 K) show Fermi-surface topology (Γ′ pocket, petals, dog-bone pockets) in good qualitative agreement with spin-polarized DFT.
  - Constant-energy contours at −100 and −150 meV are also consistent with DFT.
- **Sound experimental logic.**
  - The paper compares a direction predicted to be spin-degenerate (Γ′–K′) with one predicted to be split (Γ′–M′).
  - It also compares k_z ≈ π/2c with k_z ≈ 0.
  - It includes measurements below and above T_N.
  - Each is, in principle, a correct discriminating test.
- **Broader relevance.**
  - Establishing an intercalated-TMD altermagnet family (Nb, Ta hosts) is valuable for the community.
  - It could enable exfoliation-based and moiré studies.
- **Presentation.** The synthesis and DFT methods are described clearly enough for reproduction.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

- **(i) The spectroscopic evidence for spin splitting is at or below the resolution limit and internally inconsistent.**
  - *Inconsistent numbers.*
    - DFT gives a separation of ≈0.01 Å⁻¹ between the two dog-bone Fermi crossings near M′.
    - The Voigt fit to the 55 eV MDC (Fig. 2h) yields two peaks separated by ~0.09 Å⁻¹, an order of magnitude larger.
    - The authors do not reconcile these values.
    - Either the fitted "second peak" is not the DFT-predicted spin partner, or the DFT comparison is invalid.
  - *Inconsistent resolution figures.*
    - The quoted angular resolution of 0.1° at hν = 55 eV corresponds to Δk ≈ 0.006 Å⁻¹.
    - Yet an "instrumental (Gaussian) width" of ~0.08 Å⁻¹ is used in the fit.
    - The origin of this 0.08 Å⁻¹ is unexplained; it could come from k_z broadening, sample mosaicity, intrinsic lifetime, or fixed fitting choices.
    - A two-Voigt fit whose components have widths comparable to their separation is poorly constrained.
    - No goodness-of-fit comparison against a single-peak model is provided (χ², AIC/BIC, F-test), nor are parameter uncertainties.
  - *Qualitative rather than quantitative broadening argument.*
    - The "gradual broadening" in Fig. 2(f,g) is indicated only by hand-drawn white bars.
    - Linewidth changes with rotation angle can arise from changing band velocity, matrix elements, k_z broadening, or overlap with neighbouring bands.
    - The paper gives no fitted MDC widths versus angle compared with the DFT-predicted splitting versus angle.
  - *No energy-scale estimate.* The energy splitting corresponding to the claimed Δk is never estimated (ΔE ≈ v_F·Δk), so its size cannot be judged against the 20 meV energy resolution.

- **(ii) The k_z assignment and the "nodal-plane" control are not established.**
  - *Insufficient photon-energy range.*
    - With c = 12.45 Å, Γ–A spans π/c ≈ 0.25 Å⁻¹.
    - For typical inner potentials (V₀ ~ 10–15 eV), the 48 → 55 eV range shifts k_z by roughly 0.2–0.25 Å⁻¹, i.e. nearly a full Γ→A distance, not Γ→π/2c.
    - Without a measured photon-energy map, a stated V₀ and periodicity analysis, 55 eV could lie near k_z = π/c.
    - That plane is itself a nodal plane (as the authors themselves state), which would undercut the interpretation.
    - The manuscript mentions photon-energy-dependent measurements and "increased k_z dispersion" above T_N, but shows no such data.
  - *Intrinsic k_z broadening.*
    - At 50–55 eV the photoelectron escape depth is ~5 Å, giving intrinsic k_z broadening of order 0.1–0.2 Å⁻¹, comparable to the half-zone.
    - The spectra therefore average over k_z values where the splitting changes sign (it is odd about k_z = 0 and k_z = π/c).
    - This averaging can blur or mimic splitting.
    - It must be modelled, for example by k_z-integrating the DFT spectral function, before drawing conclusions.
  - *Confounded control comparison.*
    - The "control" MDC (48 eV, Γ–M) differs from the 55 eV Γ′–M′ cut in photon energy, matrix elements, k_z and possibly band character.
    - So the single-peak versus double-peak contrast is not a clean test.
    - The same-photon-energy nodal direction (Γ′–K′) is shown in Fig. 2(f) but not fitted.

- **(iii) Alternative origins of band doubling are not excluded, and the theory is incomplete.**
  - *Measurements are spin-integrated.* A doubled or broadened feature could instead arise from:
    - the two inequivalent TaSe₂ layers per unit cell ("bilayer"/interlayer splitting, which is strongly k_z-dependent);
    - 2×2 backfolding;
    - nonmagnetic bands near the dog-bone pocket;
    - Co-vacancy disorder.
  - *Missing DFT comparisons.* No nonmagnetic or paramagnetic DFT is shown to demonstrate that the doubling is absent without magnetic order.
  - *No spin-resolved ARPES or alternative probe.* Neither spin-resolved ARPES nor any alternative (spin-resolved MDCs or circular dichroism with an appropriate symmetry analysis) is provided.
  - *Spin–orbit coupling is omitted.*
    - The DFT appears to neglect SOC, although Ta 5d SOC is large (hundreds of meV in 2H-TaSe₂ derived bands).
    - With moments along c, SOC changes the magnetic-group analysis and can lift the "nodal" degeneracies.
    - It can also produce splittings comparable to or larger than the claimed altermagnetic one.
    - DFT+U or a moment comparison with experiment (1.35 μ_B) is also missing, as is any statement of how the DFT Fermi level or bands were shifted for the overlay.
  - *Surface termination.*
    - In a type-A structure each Co plane is ferromagnetic.
    - A cleaved surface with a partial Co layer of uncertain coverage breaks compensation within the ARPES probing depth.
    - Surface-derived FM-like or Zeeman-like splitting, or surface states, could contribute.
    - The cleavage plane, Co surface coverage and domain population are not discussed.

- **(iv) The temperature-dependent claims and magnetic characterization are over-interpreted.**
  - *Thermal effects are not separated out.*
    - The abstract claims "closing of energy gaps" and "band reconstruction", but no gap is identified or quantified.
    - Only one temperature above T_N (200 K) is shown, without Fermi–Dirac normalization or symmetrization.
    - At 200 K the Fermi edge broadens by ~4k_BT ≈ 70 meV, which alone can explain the reduced intensity at E_F and the "narrower band".
    - No temperature series shows order-parameter-like evolution tracking T_N.
    - Surface aging and thermal-cycling reproducibility are not addressed.
  - *Internal contradictions.*
    - The Γ′ pocket is called "electron-like" on p. 4 and "hole-like" on p. 6.
    - Its change is described as a "potential downward shift" in Results but as "closing of the small Γ′ pocket" in the Conclusion.
    - The intensity loss is attributed to matrix-element changes without support.
    - Statements on RKKY coupling and "absence of strong precursor fluctuations above T_N" are not backed by the presented data.
  - *Magnetic data overstated.*
    - Susceptibility cannot "confirm type-A ordering", as the abstract claims; that rests on Ref. 41.
    - "Easy-axis anisotropy" requires both B∥c and B⊥c data, but only B∥c is shown.
    - The pronounced FC/ZFC bifurcation and low-T upturn (possible spin-glass, weak FM, defect or Co-disorder contributions) are not discussed.
    - No composition analysis (EDX/WDS) or single-crystal diffraction verifying 2×2 Co order in these specific crystals is provided.
    - The resistivity argument ("strong scattering off spin fluctuations" below T_N) is physically garbled; it should refer to the reduction of spin-disorder scattering.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Make the splitting analysis quantitative and statistically rigorous.**
   - Reconcile the DFT (~0.01 Å⁻¹) and experimental (~0.09 Å⁻¹) separations, and state ΔE ≈ v_F·Δk.
   - Justify the instrumental width from the stated resolution, or measure it on a reference feature.
   - Report fit uncertainties and single- versus double-peak model selection (AIC/BIC).
   - Plot fitted MDC widths or splittings versus in-plane angle (0–30°) and versus photon energy, overlaid on DFT predictions.
   - Apply the same fitting to the Γ′–K′ nodal cut at 55 eV as an internal control.
2. **Establish k_z rigorously and model k_z broadening.**
   - Present a full photon-energy-dependent map (for example 40–100 eV) with the extracted inner potential and k_z periodicity.
   - Show explicitly which hν corresponds to k_z = 0, π/2c and π/c.
   - Compare data with k_z-integrated, matrix-element-free DFT spectral functions.
   - Ideally, show that the splitting reverses or vanishes symmetrically about the nodal planes.
3. **Exclude non-altermagnetic explanations and complete the theory.**
   - Include DFT+SOC (with moments along c) and the resulting magnetic-group / spin-group analysis.
   - Add nonmagnetic or paramagnetic DFT, and preferably DFT+U, with a comparison of the computed and experimental moments.
   - Show unfolded spectral weight for the 2×2 cell and state any energy shifts used in overlays.
   - Provide spin-resolved ARPES on the dog-bone pocket if at all possible; this is the most direct test.
   - Discuss surface termination and Co coverage after cleaving (e.g. core-level spectroscopy, STM, LEED) and possible surface FM contributions.
4. **Strengthen and temper the temperature and magnetic sections; fix inconsistencies.**
   - Add a temperature series spanning T_N (e.g. 7, 100, 150, 170, 185, 200 K) with Fermi–Dirac-divided EDCs/MDCs, and quantify any gap or shift versus T.
   - Verify reversibility on cooling.
   - Show B⊥c susceptibility and discuss the FC/ZFC bifurcation.
   - Provide composition and structural characterization of the measured crystals.
   - Shown or remove the mentioned photon-energy data and the supplementary figures referenced in the text.
   - Correct the electron/hole labelling of the Γ′ pocket and the figure cross-references (e.g. "gray diamonds in Fig. 2(d)" should be Fig. 3(d)).
   - Harmonize the Results and Conclusion wording on the Γ′ pocket.
   - Tone down unsupported mechanistic statements (RKKY, precursor fluctuations, "gap closing").
   - Add an explicit comparison with Co1/4NbSe2 (splitting size, SOC role, T_N) to clarify the paper's distinct contribution.
