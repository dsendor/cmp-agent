Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Topic and claim.**
  - Altermagnetism is a timely topic.
  - Experimentally confirmed layered (van der Waals) altermagnets with high ordering temperatures are scarce, so a well-characterized vdW altermagnet would interest a broad readership (spintronics, heterostructures, moiré engineering).
  - The manuscript claims Co1/4TaSe2 (P6₃/mmc, Co on 2a, type-A AFM with moments ∥ c, T_N = 178 K) is a g-wave altermagnet. The evidence is ARPES, DFT, susceptibility, resistivity and heat capacity.
  - The headline experimental result is a spin-integrated MDC "hump" along Γ′–M′ at hν = 55 eV (k_z ≈ π/2c). It is interpreted as altermagnetic band splitting that is absent at hν = 48 eV (k_z ≈ 0) and suppressed above T_N.
- **Symmetry is not new.**
  - Co on the 2a site of P6₃/mmc with A-type order along c is symmetry-equivalent to NiAs-type MnTe.
  - The g-wave form (nodal k_z = 0, k_z = π/c and Γ-K-H-A planes; maximum splitting near k_z = π/2c along Γ′–M′) therefore follows directly from known spin-group analysis.
- **Novelty is incremental.**
  - The isostructural, isomagnetic sister compound Co1/4NbSe2 has already been reported as an altermagnet by overlapping authors, using the same ARPES/DFT methodology and the same Γ′–M′ cut (Refs. 37–40).
  - The present work is thus largely a substitution Nb → Ta.
  - The most physically distinctive aspect of Ta (much stronger 5d spin–orbit coupling and its interplay with the altermagnetic splitting) is not explored.
  - The statement that Co1/4TaSe2 is "unique among the discussed altermagnetic materials due to its layered van der Waals structure" contradicts the manuscript's own discussion of Co1/4NbSe2.
- **Overall.** The physics question is significant, but the novelty relative to the existing literature and the strength of the spectroscopic evidence fall short of top-tier standards in the current form.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Well-motivated material.**
  - Single crystals with a sharp, well-defined T_N (susceptibility, resistivity kink, λ-anomaly in heat capacity).
  - Consistent with prior neutron work (Ref. 41) establishing the A-type structure with moments ∥ c.
- **Sound symmetry analysis.**
  - The sublattices are related by the 6₃ screw / m_z mirror but not by inversion or translation, so the system qualifies as an altermagnet.
  - The expected g-wave nodal structure is correctly identified.
- **Reasonable overall ARPES–DFT agreement.** The main Fermi-surface topology matches the calculation (Γ′ pocket, petals along Γ′–K′, dog-bone pockets at M′). This is useful baseline data for this compound.
- **Multi-parameter consistency.** The data span in-plane angle (Fig. 2f), photon energy/k_z (Fig. 2g,h) and temperature (Fig. 3). All are qualitatively consistent with the expected angular, k_z and temperature dependence of an altermagnetic splitting.
- **Practical relevance.**
  - A vdW, high-T_N altermagnet candidate in the TMD family is attractive for heterostructures and proximity effects.
  - The paper could stimulate spin-resolved and transport follow-up studies.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

- **(i) Quantitative inconsistency between the claimed ARPES splitting and DFT, and with the stated resolution.**
  - **Mismatch in size.**
    - The text states that DFT predicts a momentum separation of only ~0.01 Å⁻¹ between the opposite-spin Fermi crossings of the dog-bone bands near M′.
    - The two-Voigt fit of the 55 eV MDC yields ~0.09 Å⁻¹, about an order of magnitude larger.
    - The manuscript does not acknowledge or explain this discrepancy. If DFT is correct, the observed hump cannot be the altermagnetic splitting of these bands. If the experiment is correct, the claimed "excellent agreement" with DFT fails exactly at the key result.
  - **Resolution statements do not add up.**
    - The quoted "instrumental (Gaussian) peak width of ~0.08 Å⁻¹" is irreconcilable with the stated 0.1° angular resolution. At E_kin ≈ 50 eV, 0.1° corresponds to ≈ 0.006 Å⁻¹.
    - The 0.08 Å⁻¹ width must therefore be dominated by intrinsic effects: k_z broadening from finite photoelectron escape depth, lifetime, disorder from Co (non-)ordering, or surface inhomogeneity.
    - Calling the splitting "close to the momentum resolution limit" is therefore misleading. A splitting comparable to an intrinsic linewidth requires much stronger justification than a single asymmetric MDC.
  - **Statistical treatment is absent.**
    - There is no comparison of one- versus two-component fits (χ², F-test, AIC/BIC) and no uncertainty on the fitted separation.
    - It is not stated which parameters (widths, Lorentzian/Gaussian ratio, background) were constrained.
    - A "clear hump" is asserted from a single cut at a single photon energy.

- **(ii) Spin-integrated ARPES cannot by itself establish spin splitting; alternative origins of band doubling are not excluded.**
  - **Competing origins of a second peak.** The measurements are entirely spin-integrated. A second feature, or an increased MDC width, along Γ′–M′ can arise from many non-magnetic or non-altermagnetic sources, none of which is ruled out:
    - folding of 2H-TaSe₂ bands by the 2×2 Co superlattice;
    - interlayer (bilayer) bonding–antibonding splitting in the two-layer unit cell;
    - SOC-induced splittings of Ta 5d states;
    - surface or surface-resonance states;
    - partial Co disorder or stacking variants;
    - k_z-integrated contributions from neighbouring k_z.
  - **Statements based on DFT alone.**
    - The "complete spin-polarization" of constant-energy contours at −150 meV (Fig. 2d) rests solely on the DFT overlay, not on measurement.
    - The red/blue spin labels in Figs. 2a, c and d are theoretical only.
    - The text should not present these as observations.
  - **Surface termination is not addressed.**
    - For an A-type structure, the top magnetic layer is ferromagnetically ordered.
    - The termination after cleaving (Co-covered, Co-depleted, or half-covered) and its effect on the symmetry probed by surface-sensitive ARPES are not discussed.
    - Uncompensated surface magnetization or symmetry breaking could produce splittings unrelated to the bulk altermagnetic order.
  - **Missing direct evidence.**
    - No spin-resolved ARPES, magnetic circular dichroism, or any spin-sensitive probe is provided.
    - The comparable Co1/4NbSe2 and MnTe/CrSb works set a benchmark: claims of "observation of altermagnetic spin-splitting" increasingly require spin resolution or an equivalently decisive control.

- **(iii) The k_z assignments and photon-energy control experiment are insufficiently established.**
  - **Photon-energy-to-k_z mapping looks inconsistent.**
    - The manuscript assigns hν = 55, 51 and 48 eV to k_z ≈ π/2c, π/4c and 0, respectively.
    - With c ≈ 12.47 Å, π/2c ≈ 0.126 Å⁻¹. With a typical inner potential (V₀ ≈ 10–15 eV), changing hν from 48 to 55 eV shifts k_z by ≈ 0.2–0.25 Å⁻¹, roughly π/c rather than π/2c.
    - No inner potential, no hν-dependent k_z map, and no criterion (e.g. periodicity of a k_z-dispersive band) is shown to justify the assignments.
    - The introduction states that hν-dependent measurements reveal "increased band dispersion in the out-of-plane momentum." These data appear nowhere in the main text and must be shown.
  - **k_z broadening is not discussed.**
    - At ~50 eV the escape depth is ~5 Å, giving Δk_z on the order of 0.2 Å⁻¹, comparable to the whole 0 → π/2c range.
    - Any measurement therefore integrates over a substantial k_z window, including nodal regions.
    - This affects both the expected magnitude of the observed splitting and the interpretation of its "vanishing" at 48 eV.
  - **The control comparison is not like-for-like.**
    - The 48 eV comparison (Fig. 2h, bottom) is along Γ–M at a different photon energy. Matrix elements, cross-sections and final states all change.
    - Single- versus double-peak lineshapes at different hν are therefore not a clean control.
    - The cleanest control is the same hν and geometry above and below T_N, quantified with MDC fits; this is not provided.
  - **Angle dependence is qualitative.**
    - The angular evolution in Fig. 2f is described only through a hand-drawn "white line" indicating the feature width.
    - No quantitative MDC widths or fitted separations versus in-plane angle are compared with the DFT-predicted g-wave angular form factor.

- **(iv) Temperature-dependent claims and theoretical treatment are underdeveloped; some conclusions are overstated or speculative.**
  - **Temperature dependence: only two temperatures.**
    - Only 7 K and 200 K are compared. At 200 K, thermal broadening (~3.5 k_BT ≈ 60 meV) and Debye–Waller effects alone can wash out a 0.09 Å⁻¹ double-peak structure and reduce spectral weight near E_F.
    - No intermediate temperatures, no order-parameter-like temperature dependence of the splitting, and no thermal cycling back to low temperature are shown to exclude surface aging.
    - Fermi–Dirac normalization of the 200 K data is not described.
    - The abstract's "closing of energy gaps" is never quantified: no gap values, locations in k, or EDC fits.
  - **Inconsistent descriptions of the Γ′ pocket.**
    - It is called "electron-like" (Fermi-surface description) and "hole-like" (Fig. 3 discussion).
    - The conclusion speaks of the "closing of the small Γ′ pocket," while the text only says spectral weight is suppressed, "potentially indicating a downward shift."
    - The orbital/matrix-element explanation for the intensity changes is speculative.
  - **DFT details are incomplete.** Several omissions matter for a 5d/3d system:
    - It is not stated whether spin–orbit coupling is included. This is essential for Ta 5d bands, and for moments ∥ c it changes the degeneracy structure beyond the non-relativistic spin-group picture.
    - It is not stated whether a Hubbard U (or other correction) is applied to Co 3d.
    - The DFT Co moment is not reported for comparison with the neutron value of 1.35 μ_B.
    - Any Fermi-level shift or bandwidth renormalization used for the overlay is not disclosed.
    - Band unfolding to the 1×1 TaSe₂ zone, which is relevant for comparing ARPES intensities with a 2×2 supercell calculation, is not discussed.
    - The maximal energy (not only momentum) splitting is not given.
  - **Speculative discussion and unaddressed magnetometry.**
    - The discussion of RKKY coupling, "dual localized–itinerant character," "momentum-selective gap openings," and "absence of precursor fluctuations" is not supported by specific data in the manuscript.
    - The susceptibility shows a pronounced FC/ZFC bifurcation and a low-temperature upturn. These are not discussed, yet they could indicate a ferromagnetic impurity phase, Co inhomogeneity, or canting.
    - Canting is not symmetry-allowed for the 6′/m′mm′-type configuration with moments ∥ c, so if present it would be relevant to the altermagnetic classification.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

- **Resolve the theory–experiment discrepancy quantitatively.**
  - Explicitly compare the DFT-predicted Fermi-wavevector splitting (~0.01 Å⁻¹, plus the energy splitting) with the fitted ~0.09 Å⁻¹ separation, and explain the difference.
  - Candidate explanations to examine: different bands, SOC, U-dependence, surface effects.
  - Provide a full fitting protocol: constrained and free parameters, uncertainties, and a statistical comparison of one- versus two-peak models.
  - Correct the resolution statement. Separate the true instrumental momentum resolution from the intrinsic linewidth, and simulate the expected MDC lineshape by convolving DFT bands with realistic k_z broadening and resolution.
- **Provide spin-sensitive or otherwise decisive evidence.**
  - Ideally, add spin-resolved ARPES (S_z, given moments ∥ c) at the dog-bone crossings along Γ′–M′. Show sign reversal between symmetry-related Γ′–M′ directions (60° apart) and vanishing along Γ′–K′.
  - If unavailable, add strong controls:
    - ARPES on non-magnetic 2H-TaSe₂ and/or the paramagnetic state at identical hν and geometry, to exclude folding, bilayer and SOC splittings;
    - DFT for the non-magnetic and paramagnetic (e.g. disordered local moment) state;
    - surface-sensitive characterization of the cleaved termination (LEED/STM, core levels), with a discussion of the uncompensated top ferromagnetic layer.
- **Establish k_z and temperature dependences rigorously.**
  - Show the full hν-dependent map, the inner-potential determination, and quantified k_z broadening; recompute the k_z assignments accordingly.
  - Present fitted splitting/width versus in-plane angle and versus k_z, overlaid on the DFT g-wave form factor.
  - Measure several temperatures through T_N, e.g. 7, 100, 150, 170, 185 and 200 K, with thermal cycling. Show that the extracted splitting follows the magnetic order parameter, beyond trivial thermal broadening, and quantify any "gap closing" with EDC fits after Fermi–Dirac normalization.
- **Strengthen the theory, sharpen novelty, and fix inconsistencies.**
  - Report DFT+SOC (and DFT+U sensitivity) results, the Co moment, orbital-projected DOS (the "quarter of DOS from Co" claim), unfolded spectral functions, and the maximal altermagnetic energy splitting.
  - Explicitly contrast Co1/4TaSe2 with Co1/4NbSe2 (magnitude of splitting, SOC effects, T_N, moment, which bands are split) to clarify what is new. Remove or revise the "unique layered vdW" claim.
  - Address the FC/ZFC bifurcation and low-T upturn in χ. Verify Co stoichiometry and ordering (XRD superstructure intensities, EDX).
  - Trim the speculative RKKY / itinerant–localized discussion or support it with data.
  - Fix errors and inconsistencies:
    - electron- versus hole-like Γ′ pocket;
    - "gray diamonds in Fig. 2(d)," which should read Fig. 3(d);
    - "APRES," which should read "ARPES";
    - the garbled sentence on FM/AFM distinctions in the introduction;
    - the c lattice parameter, 12.47 Å versus 12.4535 Å;
    - supplementary figure references.
