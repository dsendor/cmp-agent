# Referee Report

**Manuscript:** "Observation of Altermagnetic Spin-Splitting in an Intercalated Transition Metal Dichalcogenide" (Sprague et al., arXiv:2508.12985)

## Summary

The authors report ARPES and DFT on Co₁/₄TaSe₂, a Co-intercalated 2H-TaSe₂ (P6₃/mmc, Co on the 2a site, 2×2 in-plane superstructure). Susceptibility, heat capacity and resistivity show a transition at T_N = 178 K. Citing prior neutron work, they assign A-type AFM order with moments along c. They argue the magnetic structure is altermagnetic, with sublattices related by a mirror/screw operation rather than translation or inversion. DFT predicts g-wave spin splitting that is maximal along Γ′–M′ in the k_z = π/2c plane. ARPES at hν = 55 eV (assigned to k_z ≈ π/2c) shows:

- a two-peak MDC near the dog-bone pocket close to M′;
- a single peak at hν = 48 eV (k_z ≈ 0);
- spectral changes between 7 K and 200 K.

The authors conclude that Co₁/₄TaSe₂ is a layered g-wave altermagnet.

The material is a sensible candidate. On symmetry grounds, the Co sublattice in this structure is analogous to Mn in NiAs-type MnTe, so altermagnetism is plausible a priori. A van der Waals altermagnet with T_N well above 77 K would be of real interest. However, the central claim, direct observation of altermagnetic spin splitting, is not supported at the level the title and abstract imply. The main problems are:

- a large quantitative mismatch between experiment and theory;
- the absence of any spin-resolved measurement;
- weak control over k_z;
- a thin temperature-dependent dataset.

I detail these below.

## Major Issues

### 1. The measured splitting is inconsistent with the DFT prediction by about an order of magnitude

The text states that DFT gives a momentum separation between the spin-split Fermi crossings of the dog-bone pocket of **~0.01 Å⁻¹**. The two-Voigt fit in Fig. 2(h) gives a peak separation of **~0.09 Å⁻¹**. These differ by a factor of ~9. Yet the paper repeatedly describes ARPES and DFT as in "excellent agreement."

Either the observed doublet is not the DFT-predicted spin-split pair, or the calculation substantially underestimates the exchange splitting. Possible reasons include the absence of U, the treatment of the Co moment, or SOC. The authors must:

- address this discrepancy explicitly;
- report the DFT splitting in both momentum and energy, at the Fermi level and as a function of in-plane angle;
- explain why a 0.01 Å⁻¹ splitting would be visible at all.

### 2. Resolution statements are internally inconsistent

The Methods quote an angular resolution of 0.1°. At hν = 55 eV (E_kin ≈ 50 eV), this corresponds to Δk ≈ 0.006 Å⁻¹. The text instead quotes an "instrumental (Gaussian) peak width of ~0.08 Å⁻¹," which is more than ten times larger.

The 0.08 Å⁻¹ figure presumably reflects intrinsic broadening: k_z broadening, lifetime, sample quality or surface disorder. It should not be called instrumental. This matters for two reasons:

- The argument that the splitting is "close to the resolution limit" is used rhetorically to make a marginal feature seem significant.
- If the true resolution is ~0.006 Å⁻¹, the 0.08 Å⁻¹ intrinsic linewidth must be explained.

### 3. The two-peak MDC fit is statistically underdetermined

Two Voigt components separated by roughly one linewidth will almost always fit an asymmetric peak better than one component. A "hump" alone is not strong evidence. The authors should:

- compare single- and double-peak models with a proper criterion (reduced χ², F-test, or AIC/BIC);
- give fit uncertainties on peak positions, widths and amplitudes;
- show fits with widths constrained to physically motivated values;
- show MDCs at several binding energies, demonstrating that the two branches disperse as predicted (parallel and steep, per DFT) rather than appearing only at E_F;
- quantify the MDC width or splitting versus in-plane angle (Fig. 2f) and versus photon energy (Fig. 2g) and compare it with DFT.

The white lines in Fig. 2(f,g) indicating "approximate width" are qualitative and not adequate evidence.

### 4. No spin-resolved measurement

The paper's title claims observation of *spin* splitting. All data are spin-integrated. A band doubling is not by itself evidence of spin polarization. The strongest alternative is that 2H-structured TMDs with two layers per cell already host interlayer bonding/antibonding doublets. These are k_z-dependent and become degenerate on the k_z = π/c plane. Further alternatives include:

- surface versus bulk states (the cleaved surface likely has a disordered or partially occupied Co layer);
- backfolded bands from the 2×2 superstructure;
- k_z-projected band manifolds.

The authors should do at least one of the following:

- perform spin-resolved ARPES, even at a few momenta, on the doublet near M′;
- show a nonmagnetic DFT calculation along Γ′–M′ that rules out a nearby orbital doublet;
- show a systematic temperature dependence demonstrating that the doublet merges at T_N (see point 6).

Without these, the title should be softened, for example to "Signatures of…" or "Evidence for…".

### 5. k_z assignment and k_z resolution

The claims hinge on 55 eV ↔ k_z ≈ π/2c and 48 eV ↔ k_z ≈ 0. However:

- The inner potential is not given.
- No photon-energy scan (k_z map) is shown in the main text. The introduction mentions photon-energy-dependent measurements, but they do not appear.

With c = 12.47 Å, the full k_z zone is only ~0.5 Å⁻¹. At E_kin ≈ 45–50 eV, the photoelectron escape depth (~5–7 Å) implies a k_z broadening of ~0.15–0.2 Å⁻¹. That is a substantial fraction of the π/2c ≈ 0.25 Å⁻¹ distance between the "maximal" plane and the nodal plane. A clean on/off contrast between 55 and 48 eV is therefore not expected.

The 48 eV comparison is also not apples-to-apples. At a different k_z the band dispersion, Fermi crossing position and matrix elements all change. A single peak at 48 eV does not by itself demonstrate that a splitting has vanished. The authors should:

- show the full hν dependence with the extracted V₀;
- show k_z-integrated DFT spectral functions to compare with experiment;
- ideally, track the MDC width continuously through hν.

### 6. Temperature dependence is too limited to support the claims

Only two temperatures are shown, 7 K and 200 K, and 200 K is just 22 K above T_N. Several problems follow:

- **Thermal effects are uncontrolled.** At 200 K, thermal broadening (4k_BT ≈ 70 meV) and phonon contributions are substantial. EDCs near E_F should be divided by the resolution-convolved Fermi–Dirac function before arguing about "suppression of spectral intensity" or a "downward shift" of the Γ′ pocket.
- **Narrowing is asserted, not quantified.** The statement that the high-T spectrum shows a "narrower band" needs quantitative MDC fits at 200 K. A narrowing upon heating would in fact be a meaningful observation, because thermal effects should broaden.
- **Sample integrity is unaddressed.** There is no evidence of reversibility after thermal cycling, and no check of sample aging or surface degradation over the heating sequence.
- **No order-parameter behaviour.** A series of temperatures through T_N, showing the splitting closing in an order-parameter-like manner, would be the single most convincing spin-integrated evidence. I strongly encourage it.
- **Gaps are asserted but not shown.** The abstract claims "closing of energy gaps," but no gap is identified or quantified anywhere in the text or figures.
- **Results and conclusions disagree.** The Conclusion states "the closing of the small Γ′ pocket," whereas the Results say only "potentially indicating a downward shift." The conclusions should match the evidence.

### 7. Overstatements and unsupported interpretive claims

- **Abstract on susceptibility.** The abstract says "Magnetic susceptibility measurements confirm type-A antiferromagnetic ordering." Susceptibility cannot determine the magnetic propagation vector; this assignment rests on the neutron study [41].
- **Final discussion paragraph.** It invokes RKKY coupling, "band hybridization features appearing below T_N," "momentum-selective gap openings," and the "absence of strong precursor fluctuations above T_N." None of these is demonstrated by the data. A single temperature above T_N cannot establish the absence of precursor fluctuations.
- **Localized versus itinerant picture.** This discussion is internally inconsistent:
  - DFT is said to show moments "well localized on Co," while the reduced moment is attributed to Co "forming an itinerant band."
  - DFT gives ~10% opposite-sign moment on TaSe₂, whereas neutrons are cited as finding none.
  - The DFT Co moment itself is never reported; it should be compared with the neutron value of 1.35 μ_B.

### 8. DFT details are insufficient

The following information is missing or inadequate:

- whether spin–orbit coupling is included; this is essential for Ta 5d, and spin is not a good quantum number with SOC;
- whether a Hubbard U was used, and the sensitivity of the splitting to U;
- the calculated moments and the energy difference between competing magnetic configurations (A-type versus FM and other AFM patterns);
- how Fermi surfaces were obtained. A 9×9×4 mesh is adequate for self-consistency but too coarse for Fermi-surface contours; was Wannier interpolation used?
- whether any energy shift or renormalization was applied for the overlays in Fig. 2(a,c,d).

The paper should also state the spin space group and the specific operation relating the two Co sublattices (e.g., [C₂‖C₆z t_c/2] or [C₂‖M_z]). It should then show explicitly which vertical planes are nodal; this would make the g-wave assignment and the Γ′–K′ versus Γ′–M′ contrast transparent. As written, the text says both "mirror symmetry" and "neither translation nor inversion" without spelling this out. A short comment on whether SOC with moments along c permits an anomalous Hall response (cf. MnTe) would also connect to the introduction's motivation.

### 9. Sample characterization

- **Composition and Co ordering.** There is no evidence of stoichiometry (EDX/WDS), Co ordering on the 2a site, or the 2×2 superstructure (XRD or LEED) for the specific crystals measured. Co content and ordering are known to vary in intercalated TMDs, and Co₁/₃-type ordering would change the symmetry entirely.
- **Susceptibility features.** Fig. 1(d) shows a pronounced FC/ZFC bifurcation and a low-T upturn. For a clean A-type antiferromagnet with B ∥ c, these require comment: possible causes include impurity phases, off-stoichiometry, domain-wall moments or canting.
- **Surface termination.** The cleaved surface's termination and Co coverage should be discussed, given ARPES surface sensitivity.

### 10. Novelty relative to prior work

The same group and collaborators have reported closely analogous ARPES/DFT evidence for altermagnetic splitting in isostructural Co₁/₄NbSe₂ [38, 40]. The magnetic structure of Co₁/₄TaSe₂ was already reported in [41]. The manuscript should state clearly what is new here and compare quantitatively with Co₁/₄NbSe₂. Candidate points of comparison include:

- stronger SOC from Ta;
- the T_N difference;
- splitting magnitudes.

The claim that this compound is "unique among the discussed altermagnetic materials due to its layered van der Waals structure" should acknowledge that Co₁/₄NbSe₂ is equally layered.

## Minor Issues

1. The Γ′ pocket is called "electron-like" when discussing Fig. 2 but "hole-like" when discussing Fig. 3(c); please reconcile.
2. "gray diamonds in Fig. 2(d)" should read Fig. 3(d).
3. Typos: "APRES" (Fig. 3 caption and Conclusion), "altermagetic," "van-der Waals," "This is a intercalated," "these finding."
4. The first paragraph of the Introduction is garbled ("The distinguishing factor separating these phases in the latter case spin sublattices are related by…"); please rewrite.
5. The statement that the exchange splitting in altermagnets is "on the scale of the Hund's rule coupling (i.e., electronvolts)" sits awkwardly next to a predicted splitting here of ~0.01 Å⁻¹. Please comment on why the splitting is so small in this compound.
6. The resistivity argument should be clarified. Above T_N, spin-disorder scattering saturates; below T_N, its freezing out produces the steeper ρ(T). "Strong scattering of carriers off spin fluctuations" is imprecise.
7. Fig. 2(h): please label which k-direction and Fermi crossing the MDC corresponds to, and mark the DFT-predicted crossing positions on the plot.
8. Fig. 2(f,g): indicate the DFT-predicted splitting for each cut and give quantitative MDC widths.
9. The MDC integration window (±13 meV) is smaller than the stated energy resolution (20 meV); please confirm that the window choice does not affect the fit.
10. The heat capacity data (Supplementary Fig. 1) and Supplementary Fig. 3 are referenced; make sure the Supplement contains the photon-energy dependence mentioned in the Introduction.
11. The Moiré-lattice paragraph is speculative and not tied to the results. Consider shortening it or moving it to the outlook.
12. Reference [32]: the journal name "J. Condens. Matter Phys." appears incorrect (likely J. Phys.: Condens. Matter).

---

## Assessment and Recommendation

The material is a promising altermagnet candidate, and the combination of thermodynamic, transport, ARPES and DFT data is a reasonable starting point. However, the key spectroscopic evidence is a single two-component fit to a marginally resolved MDC. That fit disagrees with the authors' own DFT by roughly a factor of nine, has no spin resolution, and rests on weakly constrained k_z assignments. The temperature dependence is limited to two points and is interpreted beyond what the data support.

I cannot recommend publication in its current form. I would be glad to see a revised version that includes:

1. a resolution of the DFT–ARPES splitting discrepancy and corrected resolution statements;
2. a statistically justified fitting analysis and quantitative angle and photon-energy dependence of the MDC width;
3. a k_z map with V₀ and k_z-integrated simulations;
4. either spin-resolved ARPES, or a dense temperature series showing the doublet merging at T_N, together with nonmagnetic DFT ruling out orbital doublets;
5. complete DFT details (SOC, U, moments, spin group);
6. tempered claims in the title, abstract and discussion.

**Recommendation: Major revision.**
