Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Topic.** The manuscript reports magneto-photoluminescence (PL) of a hBN-encapsulated, ~3° twisted bilayer of the A-type layered antiferromagnet CrSBr.
  - The A-exciton energy is used as a proxy for the angle between the two layer magnetizations.
  - For fields along the easy b-axis, the twisted bilayer shows a pronounced hysteresis: AP→P near ±0.24 T and P→AP near 0 T at spot 1.
  - A naturally exfoliated (pristine) bilayer switches at ~0.18 T in both sweep directions, with essentially no hysteresis.
  - No hysteresis appears along the a- or c-axes in either sample.
- **Interpretation.** The authors use a macrospin two-sublattice model with a moiré-averaged interlayer exchange field h_E.
  - They argue that the twist lowers h_E from 0.27 T to 0.14–0.19 T.
  - This lowering would open a field window where P and AP states are both metastable.
  - They further claim the bilayer responds "coherently" as a monodomain across the moiré supercell, unlike the domain or spin-texture physics of twisted CrI₃.
- **Potential significance.**
  - Small-angle twisting as a knob for the interlayer exchange of an air-stable, optically addressable A-type antiferromagnet is of broad interest for 2D magnetism and moiré physics.
  - A quantitative, all-optical route to extracting h_E, h_x and h_y would be useful to the community.
  - A "coherent moiré-averaged" regime that contrasts with the domain-dominated CrI₃ picture would be conceptually attractive, if established.
- **Novelty relative to prior work.** Novelty is moderate and needs sharper positioning against:
  - Orthogonally twisted CrSBr with programmable or multistep hysteresis (Boix-Constant et al., Adv. Mater. 2025 and Nat. Mater. 2024);
  - Twist-assisted antiferromagnetic tunnel junctions (Chen et al., Nature 2024);
  - Moiré-magnetism predictions for twisted CrSBr (Liu, Zhang & Lu, PNAS 2025);
  - Exciton–magnetization coupling in bilayers (Wilson et al., Nat. Mater. 2021).
- **Assessment.** The central experimental observation (twist-correlated easy-axis hysteresis) is interesting. However, its attribution to a coherent, exchange-tuned macrospin mechanism is, in my view, not yet established (see Section 3).

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Clean, striking primary data.**
  - The spot-1 loop (Fig. 2c) shows abrupt, step-like switching between two well-defined exciton energies (1.320 and 1.336 eV) with a large loop width.
  - The axis-selectivity is clear: hysteresis appears only along b, with none along a or c, for all three spots (Figs. 2, S9, S11, S12).
- **Systematic measurement protocol.**
  - All three crystallographic directions are measured with a vector magnet.
  - Forward and reverse sweeps are recorded, together with minor loops at different B_max (Fig. S10).
  - Spatial mapping at three positions reveals clear site-to-site variation of the switching fields.
- **Transparent, analytically tractable model.**
  - The free energy (Eq. S1), stability conditions (Eq. S2) and characteristic fields (Table S1) are explicit and reproducible.
  - I checked several entries and they are internally consistent: h_P− = 2h_E − h_x, h_⊥x,y = 2h_E + h_x,y, and h_P = h_E.
  - The model gives a compact language (AP, C and P branches; Fig. S2) for organizing the data.
- **Candid reporting of model failure.**
  - The authors openly state that the model's h_AP+ does not match experiment.
  - They also state that the model predicts hysteresis in the pristine bilayer, which is not observed.
  - This honesty is valuable and points to a genuinely open question in vdW antiferromagnets.
- **Relevance.** Optically read, field-programmable P/AP states in an air-stable 2D antiferromagnet are relevant to excitonic and spintronic device concepts.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**3.1 The theoretical model does not explain the central result; the "good agreement" claim is overstated.**
- **Wrong prediction for the control sample.**
  - With the authors' own extracted parameters (Table S2), the model predicts for the pristine bilayer AP stability up to h_AP+ = 0.569 T and P stability down to h_P− = 0.18 T.
  - That is a ~0.39 T-wide hysteresis, larger than that "explained" for the twisted sample (spot 1: 0 to 0.407 T).
  - Experimentally, the pristine bilayer shows no hysteresis.
  - The mechanism invoked for the twisted sample therefore predicts the wrong behavior for the control. The model cannot be credited with explaining why twisting creates hysteresis.
- **Post hoc exclusion of h_AP+.**
  - The observed AP→P field is the very field that defines the loop width. It is excluded from the fit because including it "produces unphysical values" (Suppl. Note 2).
  - Measured versus predicted h_AP+ differ by large factors: 0.237 vs 0.407 T (spot 1), 0.21 vs 0.30 T (spot 2), and 0.18 vs 0.569 T (pristine).
  - Discarding the only observable that fails, then claiming agreement "in a wide range of experiments", is circular.
  - The remaining three equations (S8–S10) are exactly determined, so the "agreement" is not a test of the model at all.
- **Inconsistent treatment of the two switching fields.**
  - The discrepancy (observed switching well below the coherent-rotation instability) is the classic Brown's paradox: reversal proceeds by nucleation and domain-wall motion, not uniform rotation.
  - If AP→P is nucleation-limited, P→AP is too.
  - Treating h_P− as the coherent instability field while declaring h_AP+ unreliable lacks justification. The extracted h_E, h_x and h_y inherit an unknown systematic error.
- **A simpler alternative is not considered.**
  - Consider layer-wise (spin-flip) reversal of one layer, with an exchange field h_ex and a nucleation/pinning coercivity h_c.
  - This picture gives switching at h_ex ± h_c, i.e., a loop centered at the exchange field with width 2h_c.
  - Pristine: h_c ≈ 0, h_ex ≈ 0.18 T. Twisted spot 1: loop center ≈ 0.12 T, width ≈ 0.24 T.
  - This explains both samples naturally. It implies the twist (or interface) mainly adds pinning or coercivity and only moderately reduces exchange, which is close to the opposite of the "coherent averaging" narrative.

**3.2 Attribution of the reduced exchange to the moiré (twist) is not isolated from fabrication and interface effects.**
- **Inadequate control sample.**
  - The control is a naturally exfoliated bilayer. The twisted sample is two separately exfoliated monolayers stacked by PC/PDMS dry transfer at 100 °C.
  - Interlayer exchange in CrSBr depends steeply on interlayer distance and interface cleanliness.
  - Trapped hydrocarbons, bubbles, strain or a slightly enlarged vdW gap would reduce h_E and could create pinning sites, with or without a twist.
  - An artificially stacked ~0° bilayer from the same process, and ideally several twist angles, is needed to attribute the effect to the twist.
- **Anisotropy changes point to sample differences.**
  - The extracted hard-axis field rises from h_y = 1.06 T (pristine) to 1.42–1.61 T (twisted). The intermediate-axis field changes from 0.36 to 0.18–0.29 T.
  - The hard-axis term is dominated by shape (demagnetizing) anisotropy plus a magnetocrystalline contribution. A 50% increase is hard to reconcile with "twisting primarily tunes interlayer exchange".
  - This suggests different strain, dielectric or encapsulation environments, or extraction artifacts.
  - It is unclear from the Methods whether the pristine bilayer is hBN-encapsulated identically.
- **Twist angle and moiré never characterized.**
  - The "3°" is the nominal rotator setting. No SHG, polarization-resolved determination of each layer's b-axis, PFM/TEM, or AFM of the reconstructed stack is shown.
  - Twist-angle relaxation during hot pickup is common.
  - Fig. 1a is a schematic, not data.
- **Spatial variation is equally consistent with inhomogeneous contact.**
  - h_P− varies from ~0 to 0.16 T and h_E from 0.144 to 0.19 T across spots a few µm apart.
  - This variation could equally reflect inhomogeneous interlayer contact, local twist-angle disorder, or strain. No independent local structural information discriminates these.

**3.3 Claims of "coherent monodomain" moiré averaging and a "canted state" are not supported by the measurements.**
- **No direct evidence at the relevant length scales.**
  - The diffraction-limited laser spot (~1 µm) averages over ~10⁴ moiré cells (λ_moiré ≈ 7–9 nm for a rectangular 3.5/4.7 Å lattice at 3°). The data cannot distinguish coherent averaging from fine-scale domain textures.
  - No quantitative criterion is given, e.g., comparing the domain-wall width √(A/K) or the intralayer exchange length with λ_moiré, as in Hejazi et al.
  - No comparison is made with the position-dependent J_inter of Liu et al. (ref. 33), including whether some registries become ferromagnetic.
  - The analogy to "coherence in correlated electronic states" is rhetorical.
- **Intermediate energies at spots 2 and 3 are ambiguous.**
  - The intermediate energies and gradual steps (Fig. 4c,d) are assigned to a canted state. They are equally consistent with mixed AP/P domains within the laser spot.
  - Mixed domains would contradict the monodomain claim.
  - No spectral decomposition is presented to tell the two apart: one shifted peak would indicate canting, while a superposition of 1.318 and 1.336 eV peaks with varying weights would indicate domains.
  - Linewidth evolution is likewise not shown.
  - The Suppl. Note 1 argument that the P→AP versus P→C outcome is "stochastic" and "non-deterministic" also undercuts the claimed deterministic control.
- **The exciton-energy calibration is assumed, not verified, for the twisted stack.**
  - ΔE_X ∝ cos²(θ/2) originates from spin-dependent interlayer hopping (Wilson et al.), which itself depends on stacking registry.
  - The twisted sample shows a *larger* AP–P exciton shift (16 meV vs 12 meV) despite supposedly weaker interlayer coupling. This is not discussed.
  - The proxy is therefore not validated for the twisted stack. Interpreting intermediate energies as specific canting angles is unjustified without an independent magnetometric probe such as RMCD, Kerr, SHG or NV magnetometry.

**3.4 Experimental rigor, consistency and overstated application claims.**
- **Field precision versus near-zero switching fields.**
  - Spot-1 switching fields of "3 mT" and "0 mT" are quoted, while the field step is 10 mT and the stated uncertainty ±15 mT.
  - Switching at ~0 T in both sweep directions, and even with B_max = 0 T (Fig. S10), is precisely where remanent or trapped flux in split-coil superconducting magnets (typically several mT) matters.
  - No Hall-probe calibration or offset correction is reported. h_P− ≈ 0 is the key input fixing h_x = 2h_E for spot 1.
- **Missing protocol details and controls.**
  - Laser power is "600 nW to 240 µW" without stating which power was used for the loops. Laser heating can strongly shift and narrow switching fields near 4.7 K.
  - Temperature dependence, sweep-rate dependence and cycle-to-cycle statistics of the switching fields are absent. These are the standard tests distinguishing thermally activated nucleation from coherent instability.
  - Only one twisted sample is reported.
  - The c-axis curves for the twisted bilayer reach only 1.323 eV at 1.9–2 T, not the 1.320 eV P-state value. Saturation, and hence h_⊥y, is therefore uncertain.
- **Internal inconsistencies and overstated claims.**
  - Text inconsistencies:
    - The P→AP transition is called a "redshift" (it is a blueshift from 1.320 to 1.336 eV).
    - h_P− is called the field for "forward switching (negative to positive)", although h_P− is defined for the P→AP transition on decreasing field.
    - Fig. 1c shows a finite pristine hysteresis, while the text says "no hysteresis".
    - Section cross-references in the SI are empty ("subsection ,").
  - The memory claim is not supported. At spot 1 both sweep branches return to AP at B = 0, so no zero-field bistability between P and AP is demonstrated. The two degenerate AP configurations are not distinguished by the exciton energy. "Programmable magnetic memories" and "deterministic control" are thus not demonstrated.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Isolate the twist as the cause.**
   - Fabricate and measure, with the identical process and encapsulation:
     - an artificially stacked ~0° bilayer (tear-and-stack or two monolayers aligned by polarization), and
     - at least two further twist angles (e.g., ~1°, ~5–10°), on multiple samples.
   - Directly characterize the actual twist angle and interface: polarization-resolved PL/reflectance of each monolayer before and after stacking, SHG or Raman anisotropy, AFM for bubbles and contamination, and ideally PFM/TEM of the moiré.
   - Plot h_E, h_x, h_y and loop width versus twist angle, and compare h_E(θ) with the spatial average of the ab initio J_inter(r) of ref. 33.
2. **Replace or extend the macrospin model so it explains both samples.**
   - Include nucleation- or pinning-limited reversal, e.g., a layer-wise spin-flip model with exchange field h_ex and coercivity h_c, a Kondorsky-type model, or micromagnetic or atomistic simulations with a moiré-modulated J_inter(r).
   - Show that the same framework yields no hysteresis for the pristine bilayer and the observed loop for the twisted one, using all four measured fields without discarding h_AP+.
   - Report fits with uncertainty propagation, and state which conclusions (e.g., the ~45% exchange reduction) are robust to the model choice.
   - Provide the quantitative criterion (domain-wall width or exchange length vs. λ_moiré) supporting the coherent-averaging regime.
3. **Substantiate the magnetic-state assignments independently.**
   - Add RMCD, MOKE or SHG, or NV/scanning-SQUID imaging on the same spots, to confirm that AP/P assignments and the ΔE_X ∝ cos²(θ/2) calibration hold in the twisted stack.
   - Spatially map the switching fields with sub-µm steps.
   - Decompose the spectra at intermediate fields for spots 2 and 3 into single shifted versus double peaks to discriminate a canted state from mixed domains.
   - Discuss why the AP–P exciton shift is larger in the twisted sample.
4. **Strengthen experimental rigor and calibrate the claims.**
   - Calibrate the magnet offset and remanence with a Hall sensor or a paramagnetic reference, and use finer field steps (≤1–2 mT) near zero field.
   - State the laser power used for each loop, and show power-, temperature- (4.7 K to near T_N) and sweep-rate dependences plus switching-field statistics over many cycles.
   - Extend the c-axis sweeps beyond 2 T to confirm saturation.
   - Fix the textual inconsistencies (redshift/blueshift, forward/reverse definitions, Fig. 1c vs. text, empty SI cross-references).
   - Either demonstrate zero-field P/AP bistability (e.g., via minor loops or bias engineering) or tone down the "programmable memory" and "deterministic control" claims accordingly.
