**Review outline:**

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Topic.** The manuscript reports magnetic hysteresis along the easy (b) axis in a ~3° twisted, hBN-encapsulated bilayer of CrSBr. The magnetic state is read out through the A-exciton photoluminescence (PL) energy, which depends on the relative orientation of the two layer magnetisations. A naturally stacked (pristine) bilayer is reported to show essentially no hysteresis.
- **Interpretation.** The authors use a two-sublattice (macrospin) model in which twisting reduces the spatially averaged interlayer exchange, h_E.
  - This reduction makes the parallel (P) state metastable over a wider field range.
  - Three measured characteristic fields (h_P−, h_⊥x, h_⊥y) are inverted to extract h_E, h_x and h_y.
- **Spatial variation.** Measurements at three positions are interpreted as position-dependent effective exchange.
- **Conceptual claim.** The authors propose a "coherent moiré-averaging" regime. Strong intralayer exchange and a short moiré period make the twisted bilayer behave as a single-domain antiferromagnet with a renormalised J*_inter. This contrasts with the domain/spin-texture physics reported for twisted CrI₃.
- **Potential significance.**
  - It would offer a simple, quantitative route to tuning interlayer exchange by twist in an air-stable A-type antiferromagnet with a strong optical readout.
  - It would complement earlier work on orthogonally twisted CrSBr (Boix-Constant et al., Nat. Mater. 2024; Adv. Mater. 2025) and twisted-CrSBr tunnel junctions (Chen et al., Nature 2024).
- **Novelty caveat.** Novelty is moderate.
  - Twist/stacking-programmed hysteresis in CrSBr has already been demonstrated, as has the use of exciton energy as a magnetisation probe.
  - The new elements are the small-angle regime and the averaged-exchange interpretation.
  - Both currently rest on a single twisted sample and a model with an acknowledged major inconsistency.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Clear phenomenology.**
  - Fig. 2c and Fig. S10 show sharp, reproducible, spin-flip-like switching with a loop width of ~0.24 T at spot 1.
  - Minor-loop measurements (B_max = 0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3 T) are consistent with two metastable states.
- **Complete field geometry.**
  - Measurements along all three crystal axes show hysteresis only along the easy axis.
  - This is qualitatively consistent with the macrospin picture, in which a/c-axis fields give a unique canted state.
- **Transparent, analytic model.**
  - The free energy, the stability conditions and the characteristic fields (Table S1) are given explicitly.
  - The extracted pristine parameters (h_x ≈ 0.36 T) agree reasonably with bulk FMR values (Cham et al.), which lends some credibility to the procedure.
- **Honest reporting.** The authors openly report the h_AP+ anomaly instead of hiding it. This flags a genuine open question relevant to CrSBr and CrI₃ bilayers.
- **Broad interest.** Spatially resolved loops and the prospect of twist as a "static" exchange-tuning knob (versus gating, strain or pressure) will interest the 2D-magnetism and moiré communities.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**(i) The theoretical model does not explain the central experimental contrast (twisted vs. pristine), and the parameter extraction is selectively anchored.**

- **The model predicts hysteresis in the pristine bilayer, which is not observed.**
  - Using the authors' own pristine parameters (Table S2), the model predicts h_AP+ = 0.569 T versus h_P− = 0.18 T.
  - This implies a ~0.39 T-wide loop, yet the measured loop is essentially closed (0.18 T in both directions).
  - For twisted spot 1, the predicted h_AP+ is 0.407 T versus 0.237 T measured.
  - The model therefore does not discriminate "no hysteresis" from "hysteresis" in the way the title and abstract claim. The pristine/twisted difference is then attributed to an unexplained failure of the very quantity (h_AP+) that defines the loop width.
- **The extraction uses one instability field and discards the other.**
  - Equations S8–S10 treat the measured P→AP field as the exact macrospin instability h_P− = 2h_E − h_x.
  - The equally model-defined AP→P instability h_AP+ is discarded as "unreliable", with no physical argument for why one should be reliable and not the other.
- **The anomaly is probably the classic coercivity paradox, which also affects h_P−.**
  - Real reversal proceeds by thermally assisted nucleation at defects or edges and by domain-wall propagation, well below the uniform-instability field (Brown's paradox).
  - Nucleation-assisted switching would equally shift the measured P→AP field away from 2h_E − h_x.
  - All extracted h_E and h_x values, and the inferred "twist-reduced exchange", therefore inherit an uncontrolled systematic error.
- **The spot-1 value is fine-tuned.**
  - Spot 1 has h_P− = 0, which forces h_x = 2h_E exactly.
  - It is suspicious that the system sits precisely at the stability boundary of the P state at zero field. The authors should argue why this is not an artefact of nucleation or of the field offset (see iv).

**(ii) Attribution of the effect to the twist (and specifically to moiré-averaged exchange) is not established.**

- **The evidence base is one twisted sample versus one pristine bilayer, with no twist-angle verification.**
  - The 3° value is the rotator setting during pickup. No post-fabrication determination of the actual angle is given (e.g., polarisation-resolved SHG, layer-resolved linear dichroism, Raman anisotropy, or edge-angle AFM).
  - The AFM data mentioned in the Methods are not shown.
  - It is unstated whether the two monolayers come from one flake (tear-and-stack) or two separate flakes; this determines how precisely the axes can be aligned.
- **There is no artificially stacked 0° control.**
  - The pristine bilayer has the native CrSBr registry. The twisted sample is made of two separately transferred monolayers heated to 100 °C on a PC stamp.
  - Interfacial contamination, a non-native stacking offset, strain, doping and the dielectric environment all differ between the two samples.
  - Each of these is known to modify CrSBr exchange and anisotropy: doping (Tabataba-Vakili et al.), strain (Henríquez-Guerra et al.) and stacking/pressure (Song et al.; Li et al. for CrI₃).
  - It is also not stated whether the pristine bilayer is encapsulated identically.
  - An artificially stacked 0° bilayer is required to separate "twist" from "artificial interface".
- **Spectroscopic differences point to interfacial changes beyond exchange.**
  - The AFM-state exciton energy differs: 1.336 eV (twisted) versus 1.332 eV (pristine).
  - The AFM–FM shift is larger in the twisted sample (16 vs 12 meV), and the c-axis FM energy differs (1.323 vs 1.318 eV).
  - Within the Wilson et al. picture, these numbers encode interlayer electronic hybridisation. Their changes indicate an altered interface, but are not discussed or quantitatively connected to the reduced h_E.
- **Twist-specific physics is ignored.**
  - At θ ≈ 3°, the two layers' easy axes are misaligned by 3°, so the applied field is not collinear with both easy axes.
  - The model assumes identical anisotropy axes for both sublattices.
  - The extracted anisotropies also change substantially from the pristine values: h_y rises from 1.06 T to 1.4–1.6 T and h_x varies by a factor of 2 between spots. This undercuts the premise that twisting "primarily tunes the interlayer exchange".

**(iii) The "coherent moiré averaging / monodomain" claim and the "position-dependent exchange" mapping are not directly supported.**

- **No quantitative criterion is given for the averaging regime.**
  - The authors should compare the moiré period (~7–9 nm for a 3° twist of the a ≈ 3.5 Å, b ≈ 4.8 Å lattice) with the intralayer exchange length / domain-wall width √(A/K).
  - They should compare the interlayer-exchange modulation amplitude (which Liu et al., PNAS 2025, suggest may change sign between registries) with the intralayer stiffness, using the criterion of Hejazi et al.
  - Without such an estimate, "coherent averaging" is an assumption rather than a result.
- **The optical probe cannot test the monodomain claim.**
  - A diffraction-limited PL spot (~1 µm) averages over ~10⁴ moiré cells and cannot distinguish a coherent monodomain from sub-micron domains.
  - The gradual, multi-step transitions at spots 2 and 3 (Fig. 4c,d) are interpreted as a canted state. They are equally consistent with partial or multidomain switching within the spot, which directly contradicts the monodomain picture.
- **The "spatial exchange mapping" is statistically weak and driven by anisotropy.**
  - With the stated errors (±15/25/50 mT), h_E = (h_P− + h_⊥x)/4 has an uncertainty of roughly ±7 mT.
  - Spot 1 versus spot 2 (0.144 vs 0.160 T) is barely resolved.
  - The large change in h_P− between spots comes mainly from h_x (0.288 → 0.18 T), not h_E. This contradicts the narrative of position-dependent J*_inter.
  - Three spots, without a map or a correlation with any structural feature (local twist, bubbles, strain), are insufficient to support "spatially programmable" exchange.
- **The bilayer–bulk comparison overlooks coordination number.**
  - A bilayer layer has one neighbouring layer, a bulk layer has two.
  - The pristine h_E (0.27 T) versus bulk 0.395 T should therefore be compared accounting for this factor. Otherwise the "good agreement" used to validate the procedure is ambiguous.

**(iv) Overstated application claims, and experimental and presentation deficiencies.**

- **Memory claims are not supported by the data.**
  - At spot 1, h_P− ≈ 0, so the P state is not stable at zero field. The bistable window (0 < h < 0.24 T) requires a bias field.
  - The two zero-field AP variants (↑↓ vs ↓↑) are degenerate and indistinguishable by exciton energy.
  - "Programmable magnetic memories" and "ferromagnetic order persisting near zero field" are therefore overstated.
- **The field calibration and resolution are inadequate for the quoted values.**
  - Switching fields of "3 mT" and "0 mT" are quoted, but the step size is 10 mT.
  - Superconducting split-coil magnets typically have remanent or trapped-flux offsets of several mT, and no calibration (e.g., Hall probe, or a reference sample swept through zero) is reported.
  - Because spot 1's interpretation hinges on switching exactly at zero field, this is critical.
- **Key physics tests are missing.**
  - There is no temperature dependence (loop width vs T, T_N of the twisted bilayer).
  - There is no sweep-rate or waiting-time dependence and no switching-field statistics over many cycles.
  - These would directly test thermally activated nucleation versus macrospin instability, which is the very issue behind the h_AP+ anomaly.
- **Presentation inconsistencies.**
  - The main text describes the forward-sweep transition as a "redshift from 1.336 to 1.320 eV at 3 mT", whereas Fig. 2c shows a blueshift (P→AP) near zero field.
  - "Forward switching h_P−" is defined inconsistently between Fig. 4 and the theory.
  - The Supplement contains many broken cross-references ("subsection ,", "Sec. ()").
  - Fig. S12 omits the a-axis data for spot 3 claimed in the main text, and its caption mislabels panels.
  - The B exciton is described as "newly observed" in the twisted sample even though it appears in the pristine spectrum.
  - The peak-energy fitting procedure and its error bars are not described.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Establish causality with proper controls and structural characterisation.**
   - Fabricate an artificially stacked 0° bilayer by the identical process and encapsulation.
   - Measure several samples and at least two or three twist angles (e.g., 0°, ~1–2°, ~3°, ~5–10°) to show a systematic h_E(θ) trend.
   - Determine the actual local twist angle (SHG, layer-resolved polarisation or Raman, edge-angle AFM, or TEM/4D-STEM on a sister sample) and show the AFM data mentioned in the Methods.
   - Compare the extracted reduction of h_E with first-principles moiré-averaged exchange (e.g., Liu et al., PNAS 2025).
2. **Extend the theory to address switching dynamics and twist-specific ingredients.**
   - Include nucleation and thermally activated reversal: an energy-barrier/Néel–Brown treatment, or micromagnetic simulations with a moiré-modulated J_inter(r) in an explicit two-layer model.
   - Test whether the h_AP+ anomaly, the absent pristine hysteresis and the trustworthiness of h_P− can be explained consistently.
   - Include the 3° easy-axis misalignment between layers.
   - Provide a quantitative criterion and estimate for the coherent-averaging regime (moiré period vs exchange length; exchange-modulation amplitude vs stiffness), and ideally simulate when it breaks down at smaller angles.
   - Support all this experimentally with temperature- and sweep-rate-dependent loops and switching-field statistics.
3. **Substantiate the monodomain and spatial-variation claims.**
   - Acquire field-dependent hyperspectral PL or reflectance maps across the whole twisted region, ideally complemented by RMCD/MOKE microscopy or NV/scanning-SQUID magnetometry.
   - Use these to distinguish a coherent monodomain from sub-spot domains and to correlate the switching fields with topography, bubbles and strain.
   - Add full error propagation to Table S2.
   - Discuss explicitly that the spot-to-spot variation is dominated by h_x rather than h_E, and what that implies physically.
   - Treat the bilayer–bulk coordination-number difference in the h_E comparison.
4. **Recalibrate claims and improve presentation.**
   - Tone down the memory/device statements, or demonstrate zero-field bistability and a readout scheme.
   - Calibrate the magnet's zero-field offset, use finer field steps near zero, and report switching fields with realistic uncertainties.
   - Correct the redshift/blueshift and forward/reverse inconsistencies and fix all broken cross-references.
   - Supply the missing spot-3 a-axis data and the PL fitting procedure.
   - Discuss the different exciton energies and AFM–FM splitting in the twisted sample in terms of interlayer hybridisation.
   - Position the work more carefully relative to Boix-Constant et al. (2024, 2025), Chen et al. (2024) and theoretical work on twisted CrSBr, stating clearly what is new beyond those studies.
