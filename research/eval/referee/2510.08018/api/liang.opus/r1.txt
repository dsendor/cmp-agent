Review outline:

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Claim.** The manuscript reports that bilayer CrSBr assembled with a nominal ~3° twist shows pronounced magnetic hysteresis between antiparallel (AP) and parallel (P) layer configurations for fields along the easy (b) axis. The pristine bilayer shows essentially none. The hysteresis is read out through the magnetisation-dependent A-exciton energy in photoluminescence.
- **Interpretation.** The authors use a two-sublattice (Stoner–Wohlfarth-type) macrospin model. In it, twisting reduces a spatially averaged interlayer exchange h_E (≈0.27 T → 0.14–0.19 T). Strong intralayer exchange is argued to enforce "coherent averaging" over a small (~7–9 nm) moiré cell. The predicted outcome is monodomain switching rather than the moiré domains or noncollinear textures known from twisted CrI₃.
- **Potential significance.**
  - It would identify a distinct regime of moiré magnetism, in which the moiré only renormalises a single coupling constant. This is set by a large ratio of intralayer exchange to interlayer exchange, and by a moiré length below the magnetic exchange length.
  - It would offer a simple fabrication knob, the twist angle, for interlayer AFM coupling in an air-stable magnetic semiconductor with strong magneto-excitonic readout.
- **Novelty relative to prior work.**
  - The advance is incremental-to-moderate. Twist/stacking-programmed hysteresis in CrSBr has already been shown for orthogonal (90°) twists (Boix-Constant et al., Nat. Mater. 2024; Adv. Mater. 2025). Twist-assisted AFM tunnel junctions have also been reported (Chen et al., Nature 2024).
  - Small-angle moiré magnetism in CrSBr has been predicted theoretically (Liu et al., PNAS 2025).
  - The genuinely new elements are the small-angle regime and the proposed "averaged-exchange monodomain" picture. Neither is yet established by direct structural or magnetic-imaging evidence.
- **Internal tension in the theory.** The model's central prediction, the AP→P switching field h_AP+, disagrees with experiment for both pristine and twisted samples. The model also predicts hysteresis in the pristine bilayer that is not observed. This weakens the claim that the theory "captures" the twist-induced hysteresis.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Clear, striking phenomenology.**
  - The spot-1 loop is sharp and square, with a large exciton contrast (~16 meV).
  - The loop is reproducible over sweeps and minor loops (Fig. S10).
  - Hysteresis is strictly confined to the easy axis, with none along a or c, at all three spots.
- **Comprehensive vector-field dataset.**
  - Field sweeps along all three crystal axes are shown for both pristine and twisted samples.
  - Polarisation-resolved PL and differential reflectance (DR/R) characterisation are included.
  - Together these allow an over-determined parameter extraction, which is a methodologically sensible approach.
- **Transparent, analytic, falsifiable model.**
  - The closed-form characteristic fields (Table S1) and the stability analysis (Fig. S2) are clear and easy to test.
  - The authors openly acknowledge the h_AP+ anomaly rather than hiding it.
- **Spatial variability as a lever.** Position-dependent loops (Fig. 4) hint that local stacking or relaxation can tune switching fields. This is relevant to engineered AFM memory and spin-filter concepts.
- **Topical and broad interest.** The work connects moiré engineering, 2D magnetism and magneto-excitonics in CrSBr, which is currently a very active platform.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**Reason 1 — The causal link between "twist" and "reduced exchange/hysteresis" is not established; key controls are missing.**
- **Twist angle and moiré never characterised.**
  - The only evidence for θ ≈ 3° is the nominal rotator setting.
  - There is no SHG, polarisation-resolved angle determination of the two b-axes, TEM/4D-STEM, PFM/torsional AFM, or Raman-based evidence for the angle, moiré period, or lattice reconstruction.
  - The Methods describe AFM, but no AFM data (thickness, step height, bubbles, interface cleanliness) are shown.
  - Twist angles in tear-and-stack or dry-stacked devices routinely relax by ~0.5–1° and vary spatially. The spot-to-spot variation in Fig. 4 may therefore reflect angle disorder, bubbles or strain rather than "moiré averaging".
- **The comparison is between non-equivalent samples.**
  - The twisted device is assembled from two monolayers, picked up with PC at 100 °C, hBN-encapsulated and solvent-cleaned. The pristine bilayer is, as described, an exfoliated natural bilayer on SiO₂.
  - Each of these differences can independently reduce or modify h_E, h_x and switching behaviour: interfacial contamination increasing interlayer spacing, transfer-induced strain (Ref. 43), dielectric environment, and charge doping (Ref. 32 shows doping controls magnetism and exciton energies in few-layer CrSBr).
  - The A-exciton AFM energy already differs between samples (1.336 vs 1.332 eV), signalling a different electrostatic or strain environment.
  - Indispensable controls are missing: an artificially stacked 0° bilayer made with the identical process, and an hBN-encapsulated natural bilayer.
- **A single device at a single angle cannot support "twist-tuned".**
  - Demonstrating tunability requires several devices and, ideally, a twist-angle series (e.g., 0°, ~1°, 3°, 5–10°).
  - Such a series would show a systematic h_E(θ) trend that can be compared with the averaged exchange from Ref. 33.

**Reason 2 — The theoretical model does not actually explain the hysteresis, and the parameter extraction is selectively constrained.**
- **The measured loop width is not reproduced.**
  - The loop width is set by h_AP+ − h_P−. The model predicts h_AP+ = 0.407 T (spot 1) and 0.569 T (pristine), against measured values of 0.237 T and 0.18 T, a factor of ~1.7–3 discrepancy.
  - The model also predicts a ~0.39 T-wide loop in the pristine bilayer, which is not observed.
  - The model's mechanism ("lower h_E widens P stability") therefore cannot be distinguished from the pristine case within the model itself. The statement of "good agreement between experiments and analytic model" is overstated.
- **Inconsistent treatment of the two spinodal fields.**
  - Both h_AP+ and h_P− are metastability-limit (spinodal) fields of a coherent macrospin.
  - It is well known (Brown's paradox) that real magnets switch below spinodal fields via nucleation and domain-wall propagation, thermal activation, or edge and defect sites.
  - The authors discard h_AP+ as "unreliable" yet retain h_P− = 2h_E − h_x as exact for extracting h_E. No physical justification is given for why one spinodal should be reliable and the other not.
  - A nucleation-limited picture would undermine the extracted h_E values and the "monodomain coherent switching" interpretation at the same time.
- **The extracted parameters are not self-consistent with "twist primarily tunes exchange".**
  - Across the samples, h_y varies from 1.06 T (pristine) to 1.42–1.61 T (twisted). h_y should be dominated by shape anisotropy (μ₀M_s) plus intrinsic terms, and is not expected to change by 50% with twist.
  - h_x varies by nearly a factor of 2 between spots of the same device (0.18–0.29 T).
  - With the stated errors (±15/±25/±50 mT), h_E varies only modestly across spots (0.144–0.19 T, ≈ ±10–15 mT uncertainty). The spot-to-spot differences in h_P− are thus driven as much by h_x as by h_E. This contradicts the claim of "position-dependent modulation of the average exchange".
  - h_⊥y for the twisted sample (1.8–1.9 T) lies at the edge of the 2 T sweep range. The c-axis curve in Fig. 2d is still evolving at 2 T, and the FM energy (1.323 eV) differs from the a/b saturation value (1.320 eV). Saturation is therefore likely not reached, and h_y is likely biased.
- **Spot 1 is a boundary case.**
  - h_P− = 0 forces h_x = 2h_E exactly. For h_x > 2h_E the P state is stable at zero and reversed fields, and switching then depends on coercive and nucleation physics outside the model.
  - The reported "3 mT vs 0 mT" asymmetric switching is in fact a negative h_P−, which the extraction cannot represent.

**Reason 3 — Claims of "coherent moiré averaging" and "monodomain switching" are not directly evidenced.**
- **The probe cannot resolve the relevant length scales.**
  - Confocal PL averages over a ~1 µm spot, roughly 10⁴ moiré cells and many potential domains.
  - Sharp steps at spot 1 are consistent with monodomain switching. They are equally consistent with an avalanche-type multidomain reversal that completes within one 10 mT field step.
  - At spots 2 and 3, the gradual transitions are attributed to a "canted state". They could equally be spatially averaged mixed AP/P domains, as reported in twisted CrI₃. The exciton-energy model ΔE ∝ cos²(θ/2) cannot distinguish a uniform canted state from a domain mixture without linewidth or line-shape analysis. Two-peak versus single-shifted-peak spectra should be shown at intermediate fields.
- **No quantitative criterion for the averaging regime.**
  - The manuscript provides no estimate comparing the moiré period with the exchange length or domain-wall width, √(A/K), using CrSBr intralayer stiffness and anisotropy (Refs. 27, 42).
  - It provides no comparison with the amplitude and sign variation of the local interlayer exchange predicted in Ref. 33.
  - It does not apply the Hejazi–Balents-type criterion for when textures form.
  - Without these, "coherent averaging" is an assumption of the model rather than a result. The ~47% reduction of h_E is never compared with the averaged J(r) from first-principles predictions.
- **The canted-state assignment is ad hoc.**
  - The supplement states that the choice between AP and canted states on decreasing field is "non-deterministic" and stochastic.
  - This makes the model unfalsifiable in exactly the regime where spots 2 and 3 deviate.

**Reason 4 — Data-quality, resolution and overstatement issues.**
- **Field resolution and control.**
  - Switching fields of "3 mT" and "0 mT" are quoted, while the field step is 10 mT and the stated error is ±15 mT. The claimed sub-step asymmetry and "ferromagnetic order persisting near zero field" are not resolvable.
  - Remanent-field offsets of split-coil vector magnets (typically several mT) are not addressed.
  - Field alignment relative to each layer's b-axis is not discussed. With a 3° twist the layers differ by 3°, and small misalignment between the field and the crystal axes can strongly affect easy-axis metastability.
- **Missing measurement conditions and checks.**
  - Excitation powers span 600 nW–240 µW, but the power used for each figure is not specified.
  - Laser heating can shift switching fields and loop widths. No power, temperature or sweep-rate dependence is shown, although these are the natural tests of the thermally activated, nucleation-limited switching implied by the h_AP+ anomaly.
- **Overstated memory and device claims.**
  - For spots 2 and 3 (h_P− = 0.14–0.16 T), the zero-field state is AP on both branches.
  - For spot 1, bistability at zero field is marginal.
  - The two AP states (A↑B↓ vs A↓B↑) are not distinguished by the PL readout.
  - Field-free, non-volatile, readable memory is therefore not demonstrated. The abstract's "programmable magnetic memories" and the summary's "deterministic control" are not supported.
- **Presentation inconsistencies.**
  - h_P− is described as the "forward switching" field in Fig. 4, but defined as P→AP on decreasing |h| in the theory. This should be clarified.
  - The pristine bilayer is described both as having a "very narrow hysteresis" and "no hysteresis".
  - The B exciton is described as "newly observed" in the twisted bilayer, although it also appears in pristine PL (1.367 eV).
  - Supplementary cross-references are broken (empty "Sec. ()" and "subsection" references).
  - Fig. S12 labels one panel as the a-axis while the caption omits it.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Structural verification and proper controls.**
   - Determine the local twist angle and its spatial variation, using polarisation-resolved SHG or Raman of each layer's b-axis, PFM/torsional AFM, or TEM.
   - Show AFM topography of the measured region and identify bubbles or contamination at spots 1–3.
   - Fabricate and measure, with the identical stacking process and hBN encapsulation, a 0°-aligned artificial bilayer and an encapsulated natural bilayer.
   - Add at least one or two further twist angles and several devices, and plot the extracted h_E(θ) against averaged J(r) from Ref. 33 or a DFT-based moiré average.

2. **Rework the theory to address switching kinetics consistently.**
   - Either incorporate nucleation-limited or thermally activated switching (e.g., Néel–Arrhenius barriers, finite-size macrospin, edge nucleation), or justify why h_P− but not h_AP+ follows the coherent-rotation limit.
   - Test this with temperature-, sweep-rate- and laser-power-dependent loops.
   - Extract parameters from independent quantities wherever possible: h_⊥x, h_⊥y and the saturation curvature, or magnon or AFMR frequencies, as in Ref. 42. Then check h_P− as a prediction rather than as an input.
   - Report full error propagation for h_E, h_x and h_y.
   - Extend c-axis sweeps beyond saturation to fix h_y.

3. **Provide direct evidence for the averaging and monodomain regime.**
   - Add a quantitative criterion comparing the moiré period to the exchange length and domain-wall width, together with the predicted amplitude and sign of J(r).
   - Ideally include micromagnetic or atomistic simulations of the moiré bilayer, showing that textures are suppressed at 3° and yield the measured effective h_E.
   - Experimentally, use spatially resolved magnetic imaging (Kerr/RMCD microscopy, NV magnetometry or MFM) to distinguish monodomain switching from multidomain switching, and a uniform canted state from AP/P domain mixtures at spots 2 and 3.
   - Show full PL spectra at intermediate fields, distinguishing a single shifted line from a two-component line shape.

4. **Tighten measurements, claims and presentation.**
   - Use finer field steps (≤1 mT) near zero, and calibrate and report magnet remanence and field–crystal-axis alignment for each layer.
   - Specify the excitation power for every dataset.
   - Moderate the memory and "deterministic" claims, or demonstrate field-free bistability and readout of the two AP configurations (e.g., via layer-sensitive SHG or circular dichroism).
   - Fix the inconsistent switching-field terminology, the broken supplementary references, the figure-caption mismatches and the conflicting statements about pristine hysteresis.
   - Expand the discussion of prior twisted- and orthogonal-CrSBr work to clarify precisely what is new in the small-angle regime.
