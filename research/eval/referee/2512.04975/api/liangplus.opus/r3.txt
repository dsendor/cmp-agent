# Referee report: "Tracing the horizon of tetragonal-to-monoclinic distortion in pressurized trilayer nickelate La4Ni3O10"

**Review outline:**

## 1. Significance and novelty

The structural phase boundary of La4Ni3O10 under pressure matters for the debate on whether superconductivity requires the undistorted tetragonal phase. The disputed intermediate orthorhombic Bmab phase (Li et al., Ref. 22) bears directly on that debate. The manuscript combines:
- high-temperature powder XRD,
- helium-medium single-crystal XRD in a diamond-anvil cell at 300, 80 and 20 K,
- PBE structural relaxations,
- polarized Raman spectroscopy.

The new elements are the low-temperature part of the T–P boundary and the explicit claim of a direct I4/mmm→P21/c transition. The room-temperature pressure-induced transition itself has been reported before (Refs. 19–22). The Raman claim of a third, lower-symmetry phase below T_DW would also be significant if it were established.

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- It reports the first P–T mapping of the structural boundary down to 20 K under good hydrostatic conditions (helium medium, ruby checked before and after each run).
- The crystallographic bookkeeping is careful: consistent non-standard settings (F4/mmm, B21/a) make the small distortions comparable across phases, a real service given the confusion in the literature.
- The DFT relaxation is performed in the lowest-symmetry cell, so it can in principle access all candidate structures. The authors candidly discuss the PBE pressure offset and the critical role of the force threshold.
- Raman data with temperature dependence, mode counting and a full mode list (SI) add an independent probe of the density-wave (DW) state.
- Several observations are useful regardless of interpretation:
  - an ambient-pressure SXRD null result for DW satellites on flux-grown crystals,
  - anomalous temperature dependence of the 350 and 470 cm⁻¹ Ag modes.

## 3. Concerns

### A. Must address

**1. The experimental observables used do not discriminate Bmab from P21/c.**

The key claim is a direct transition "with no trace of an intermediate orthorhombic Bmab phase". Most of the evidence presented is common to both space groups:
- **(½,½,0) superlattice reflections (Fig. 2b).** These are the X-point tilt signature and appear in Bmab as well as P21/c. Both give a 2-fold superstructure of the primitive I-cell.
- **Splitting and broadening of hk0 reflections (Fig. 2a, Fig. 1 insets).** These measure in-plane a≠b, i.e. orthorhombic strain plus 90° twinning about c. This occurs equally for Bmab. The caption's attribution to "monoclinic distortion of the lattice" is not justified by hk0 data.
- **Centering-violating spots (Fig. 2c).** These are attributed to twin domains. A 90°-rotated B-centered domain gives A-centered spots in the parent frame, so these spots would equally appear for twinned Bmab.

The phase-diagram points in Fig. 1 are therefore defined by the onset of tetragonal symmetry breaking, not by the onset of monoclinicity. The discriminating evidence reduces to two items:
- (i) β − 90° ≈ 0.0–0.2°, which by the authors' own statement is "negligible" at 300 K (Fig. 4b);
- (ii) a better R-factor for B21/a than Bmab (SI Table S4).

B21/a has more refinable parameters, so a lower R is expected regardless of whether the extra freedom is physical. The authors should:
- identify the reflection classes allowed in B21/a but extinct in Bmab;
- show their presence and intensity as a function of pressure, especially just below the 10.5 GPa (300 K) and 13–14 GPa (80/20 K) onsets;
- rule out twin overlap and multiple diffraction (Renninger) in the DAC as their origin;
- compare refinements with identical twin models and give number of parameters, reflections, completeness and a Hamilton R-ratio test;
- show h0l/0kl-type splitting that directly reflects β ≠ 90°, rather than hk0 splitting.

**2. The Landau/group-theoretical picture is internally inconsistent and not addressed.**

The text calls the transition both "weak first-order" (jump in β at onset, p. 8) and "continuous bypassing the orthorhombic phase" (same paragraph). Generically, a direct I4/mmm→P21/c transition requires condensation of two distinct irreducible representations. If P21/c needs two irreps, simultaneous onset is either first-order or fine-tuned (a multicritical point), and an intermediate Bmab window is the generic alternative. The authors should:
- give the symmetry-mode decomposition (e.g. ISODISTORT), specifying which X-point irreps generate Bmab and P21/c;
- state which is primary;
- state whether β − 90° couples linearly or as a bilinear product of two order parameters.

Two observations are expected regardless of whether a Bmab window exists, and so do not support the direct-transition claim:
- that (b−a)/(a+b) vanishes linearly while θ vanishes as (Pc−P)^½ is just secondary coupling to θ²;
- that both vanish at the same Pc follows from the same coupling.

Only the simultaneous vanishing of β − 90° is informative. In Fig. 5 this rests on a three-point fit with α fixed at ½ on a 1 GPa grid, and β is the parameter whose scaling "quickly breaks down". A Bmab window narrower than about 1 GPa is not excluded. The authors should:
- compute enthalpies of separately constrained Bmab and P21/c relaxations, ΔH(P), on a fine grid (≤0.1 GPa) across 14–17 GPa;
- show that ΔH is resolvable relative to the stated 1 mHa total-energy convergence and the 2×4×4 k-mesh;
- check for hysteresis experimentally, on compression versus decompression at 300 K and on heating/cooling in the PXRD data of Fig. S1.

**3. The conclusion that superconductivity emerges only in the tetragonal phase is not supported by this work.**

No transport or susceptibility is measured on these crystals under the same conditions.
- The authors' room-temperature boundary is 10.5 GPa. The Introduction cites ≈15 GPa for the room-temperature transition (Refs. 19–22). This discrepancy is not discussed; possible causes include oxygen stoichiometry, pressure medium and onset criterion.
- The Introduction notes superconducting onsets reported from 5 to 40 GPa depending on medium. Onsets below about 14 GPa at low temperature would fall inside the monoclinic region of Fig. 1.

The authors should either:
- measure Tc on crystals from the same batch in helium, or
- restate the conclusion as consistent with selected literature, with explicit comparison of boundaries and media.

**4. The Raman mode counting does not establish symmetry lower than P21/c, and the space-group assignment (Pc/P21) does not follow.**

- **Selection rules versus polarization geometry.** Table I's caption says the Y and Z polarizations lie in the (bc) plane "but not aligned with a particular axis". For P21/c (unique axis b), Bg has αbc. At a general angle φ between Y and b, Bg leaks into the parallel channel (term 2αbc sinφ cosφ) and Ag leaks into the crossed channel. The statement that Ag appears only in YY and Bg only in YZ therefore holds only for Y∥b or Y∥c. The "31 modes in parallel > 24 Ag" argument then fails, because up to 48 modes may appear in YY. Only the total, 51 versus 48, remains. This margin of three modes depends on counting each mode once across both channels, which must be shown explicitly. Twin domains within the laser spot further mix the tensor components.
- **Alternative origins of new modes.** Each of these could produce a few new low-temperature modes and needs to be excluded:
  - zone-folded modes from an incommensurate DW (translational symmetry breaking, not point-group lowering);
  - DW amplitude modes;
  - two-phonon features;
  - intergrowths or impurity phases. The batch contains La2NiO4 and NiO (Methods); La3Ni2O7 intergrowths are common and order near 150 K; La2NiO4+δ shows charge order at 100–200 K.
- **Testable prediction for Pc/P21.** A k=0 loss of inversion predicts that the new modes coincide with IR phonon frequencies and that SHG appears. Neither is checked.

The authors should:
- perform angle-resolved polarized Raman with the crystal axes determined (e.g. by Laue or XRD on the same crystal);
- report the polarization angle relative to b;
- map multiple spots and crystals;
- compare new-mode frequencies with published IR phonons (e.g. Ref. 26).

Until then, the abstract's "further reduction in symmetry from monoclinic P21/c" and the "third structural phase" should be presented as hypotheses.

**5. The DW state is not demonstrated in the measured crystals, and the XRD null result is not quantified.**

Both the "no structural distortion associated with DW" claim and the "third structural phase associated with DW ordering" claim presuppose that these flux-grown crystals undergo the DW transition. However:
- no resistivity, susceptibility or heat-capacity data are shown;
- the oxygen content is not determined (EPMA gives only La:Ni);
- the authors themselves note sample dependence of the Raman response.

For the null result, the authors should:
- give an upper bound on satellite intensity relative to main and (½,½,0)-type reflections at the wavevectors reported in Ref. 25;
- state the dynamic range and counting statistics of the P24 data (Fig. S4).

The DESY data at 80 K could also directly test Pc and P21. Pc loses the 21 screw axis (0k0, k odd would appear), and P21 loses the c-glide (h0l, l odd would appear). A comparison of non-centrosymmetric and centrosymmetric refinements, including Friedel-pair differences, should be added. This would also address the stated XRD/Raman "puzzle".

### B. Should address

**6. Phase-diagram points need brackets and a stated criterion.**

- **80 K point.** Fig. 2a shows the 80 K reflection broadened at 10.5 GPa and round at 15 GPa, yet the point is placed at 13 GPa. It is unclear which intermediate data fix it, and the boundaries in Fig. 4 (80 and 20 K panels) appear to lie near 14 GPa.
- **20 K point.** The 20 K point (14 GPa) lies between 12.5 and 15 GPa data.
- **Error bars and data density.** Pressure brackets should be shown as horizontal error bars. The "linear suppression" rests on four points with no data between 0 and 10.5 GPa or above 300 K under pressure; describe it as a guide only.
- **Ruby calibration.** Specify the ruby temperature correction applied at 80 and 20 K, and whether pressure was changed at low temperature.

**7. Accuracy of β and lattice parameters from twinned DAC data.**

- Error bars "smaller than the symbols" appear to be statistical only. With four partially overlapping pseudo-merohedral domains, limited DAC angular coverage and 0.5° frames, systematic errors in β of order 0.05–0.1° are plausible. This is comparable to the 300 K values in Fig. 4b.
- Describe how domain overlap is handled in cell refinement and integration.
- Give realistic uncertainties, for instance from refinements using different reflection subsets and the scatter between them.

**8. DFT methodological details.**

State:
- the magnetic configuration (non-spin-polarized or otherwise), given the SDW;
- the smearing scheme and width;
- k-point convergence of the structural energy differences, not only the total energy;
- sensitivity of Pc and of the "no intermediate phase" result to the functional or a +U correction.

The quoted Pc = 16.47 ± 0.04 GPa conveys false precision; the main text itself estimates a 1–3 GPa PBE offset. Also note that the static T=0 calculation is compared with 80 K and 300 K data.

**9. Octahedral tilt data (Fig. 3c).**

- At low pressure the 80 K tilt (~14°) lies below the 300 K tilt (~15–16°), and the curves cross. This is opposite to the expected increase of distortion on cooling and needs comment.
- The 80 K data stop near 9.5 GPa with θ ≈ 7°, although the boundary is placed at 13 GPa. This makes the claimed agreement with the DFT square-root law hard to assess.
- Check that the 171.1° angle quoted at 6 GPa (θ ≈ 8.9°) matches the plotted 300 K value.
- The sentence "At 15 GPa, the octahedra is perfect" conflicts with the preceding statement that the octahedra are strongly distorted. Presumably "untilted" is meant.

**10. Equation-of-state fits (Fig. 4d).**

K0 changes from 155(4) GPa at 300 K to 182–185 GPa at 80/20 K, which is unusually large for this temperature range. The authors should:
- state whether K′ was fixed or refined;
- give the fit range and whether points on both sides of the transition were included;
- give the K0–K′ correlation.

### C. Minor

11. T_DW is quoted inconsistently: 135 K in the abstract, 130 K in the text, and "~120 K" and "120–150 K" for the Raman onset. Use one definition.

12. The subgroup chain "P21/c ⊂ Bmab ⊂ Fmmm ⊂ I4/mmm" mixes standard and non-standard settings and omits indices. Give the explicit chain with transformation matrices and the index at each step.

13. Fig. 2a uses different reflections at different temperatures (4 6 0, 6 4 0, −2 6 0), which complicates comparison of splittings. Use symmetry-equivalent reflections or justify the choice.

14. The Fig. 4 caption gives a 0–16 GPa range while the axes extend to 20 GPa. In Fig. 1, clarify that the DFT triangle is a T=0 static result subject to the 1–3 GPa offset.

15. The high-temperature PXRD up to 1223 K may involve oxygen loss (cf. Ref. 23). State whether the transition is reversible on cooling and whether the oxygen content was checked afterwards.

16. Raman methods: give the spot size, the orientation determination, and how the 26 K laser-heated temperature was estimated.

17. Language and typos:
- "unequivocally" is used repeatedly; tone it down;
- "propoposal", "PsuedoDojo";
- "Gabarino" versus "Garbarino";
- the missing parenthesis in "KTiOPO4 for Oxygen (40.4%";
- "At on the onset";
- lower-case chemical formulas in several references.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

1. **Discriminating diffraction analysis.** Add maps or lists of reflections allowed in B21/a and forbidden in Bmab, and their pressure dependence. Add an ISODISTORT mode decomposition, so the direct-transition claim rests on symmetry-specific evidence rather than on a 0.1° angle and R-factors.
2. **Same-sample characterization.** Provide resistivity or susceptibility, ideally under pressure in helium, and oxygen-content determination. This would anchor both the DW-related conclusions and the superconductivity–tetragonality link.
3. **Raman measurements resolving the symmetry question.** Use angle-resolved polarized Raman with known crystal axes, spatial mapping, comparison with IR phonon frequencies, and an SHG test for inversion breaking. Reanalyze the 80 K DESY data for violations of the 21 screw or c-glide conditions to discriminate Pc from P21.
4. **DFT phase stability.** Replace or supplement order-parameter fits with constrained-symmetry enthalpy curves ΔH(P) on a fine pressure grid. Test robustness to magnetism and +U.
