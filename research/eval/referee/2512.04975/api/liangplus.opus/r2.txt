**Review outline:**

## 1. Significance and novelty

The paper addresses a disputed question for pressurized trilayer nickelates. It asks whether La₄Ni₃O₁₀ passes directly from monoclinic P2₁/c to tetragonal I4/mmm under pressure, or through an orthorhombic Bmab phase as Li et al. [22] proposed. It combines several measurements:
- high-pressure single-crystal XRD with helium medium at 300, 80 and 20 K;
- high-temperature powder XRD;
- a PBE structural relaxation in the P2₁/c cell;
- polarized Raman scattering at ambient pressure, claimed to show a symmetry lower than P2₁/c below T_DW.

A P–T structural map down to 20 K, and a consistent treatment of the P2₁/c ↔ B2₁/a and I4/mmm ↔ F4/mmm settings, would be useful to the community. However, the central discriminating claims (monoclinic rather than orthorhombic; a "third structural phase") rest on very small effects. These currently lack the analysis needed to be called unequivocal.

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- Hydrostatic (He medium) single-crystal data over 0–16 GPa at three temperatures, with reconstructed reciprocal-space maps showing superlattice reflections at (½,½,0) and reflection splitting (Fig. 2).
- A low-temperature phase boundary (≈14 GPa at 20 K) that is directly relevant to the pressure window where superconductivity is reported.
- Explicit discussion of non-standard settings. This removes a real source of confusion (β ≈ 100° vs β ≈ 90.1°).
- DFT order parameters (θ, β−90°, basal distortion) shown on one pressure axis (Fig. 5). The tilt magnitude and the critical pressure are comparable to experiment.
- An informative negative result: no DW satellites in synchrotron SXRD on flux-grown crystals. This adds to the reproducibility debate around Ref. [25].

## 3. Concerns

### A. Must address

**1. The monoclinic-vs-orthorhombic distinction under pressure is not demonstrated, especially at 300 K.**
- The text (p. 8) states that at 300 K the monoclinic distortion "is negligible", and Fig. 4(b) at 300 K shows β ≈ 90.0° across the whole 2/m range.
- The superlattice reflections at (½,½,0) (Fig. 2b) and the a/b splitting in hk0 (Fig. 2a) arise from octahedral tilts and a ≠ b. Both are equally expected in Bmab, so they show departure from I4/mmm, not monoclinicity.
- The only evidence offered for B2₁/a over Bmab is a "much better" fit (Table S4). B2₁/a has more refinable parameters and twin fractions, so a lower R is expected regardless.

The authors should:
- (a) Use the reflection conditions that separate the two groups. In the LP setting, the b-glide of Bmab extinguishes 0kl with k odd, which B2₁/a allows. Show reciprocal-space sections and integrated intensities of these classes above background. Explicitly account for which twin domains can overlap them.
- (b) Report a Hamilton R-ratio or equivalent significance test with matched twin models.
- (c) Show h0l sections at high l. A finite β−90° should produce characteristic splitting between monoclinic twin domains, distinct from the a/b-swap splitting in hk0.
- (d) Give realistic β uncertainties. "Error bars smaller than symbols" (Fig. 4 caption) is contradicted by point-to-point scatter of ~0.2–0.4° at 80 and 20 K in Fig. 4(b). Unconstrained cell refinements in the tetragonal phase would set the systematic baseline for apparent β ≠ 90°.

Without this, the statement that the transition "bypasses the orthorhombic phase" is not established experimentally.

**2. The twin interpretation of the centering-violating reflections must be made quantitative.**
- This is the point of direct disagreement with Li et al. [22], who observed similar weak reflections and interpreted them differently. Fig. 2(c) shows yellow-circled spots below 11.3 GPa. The text says these "appear to violate centerings" but arise from "microscopic domains", and that four twin domains form (SI).
- The paper does not:
  - give the twin matrices;
  - show that all extra spots are indexed by the twin model, and none remain unindexed;
  - report refined domain fractions;
  - show that the number of domains is four rather than two. Four domains are expected for 4/mmm → 2/m and two for 4/mmm → mmm, so the count itself could discriminate monoclinic from orthorhombic.
- Fig. 2(c) labels "B-center" at 10.5 and 6 GPa, yet the circled spots apparently violate B-centering. Please state precisely which reflection condition each circled class violates. For each class, state whether it is accounted for by the a↔b twin (A-type spots) or not.

**3. The Raman evidence does not support "breaking of P2₁/c" or a specific noncentrosymmetric group (Pc/P2₁).**
- *Channel counts are not meaningful as measured.* Table I and p. 13 state that Y and Z lie in the (bc) plane "but not aligned with a particular axis". For b-unique 2/m, a general Y in the bc plane makes the yy channel contain α_bc contributions (B_g), and yz contain A_g. Pseudo-merohedral twinning, with domains rotated by 90° in plane, mixes them further. The 31 "parallel" modes exceeding 24 A_g is therefore not evidence. The statement on p. 16 that each mode "clearly follows the Raman selection rules" for A/B is also not substantiated.
- *The total count gives a thin margin.* The decisive count is 51 distinct modes vs 48 allowed, a margin of three. That margin is also the sole basis for excluding P1̄, which has the same 48 Raman-active modes.
- *Alternative origins are not excluded.* The extra lines could be:
  - zone-folded phonons and amplitude modes of the incommensurate DW, which appear below T_DW without any change of the average space group;
  - two-magnon or electronic excitations associated with the SDW;
  - second-order features;
  - lines from La₃Ni₂O₇, La₂NiO₄ or NiO intergrowths. The PXRD in Methods shows La₂NiO₄ and NiO side phases.
- *The group-subgroup analysis is too narrow.* Restricting candidate groups to translationengleiche subgroups (P1̄, Pc, P2₁, P1) with the same 17 Wyckoff sites is unjustified if the DW has a finite, incommensurate q. The authors acknowledge this on p. 16 but still propose Pc/P2₁ and a "third structural phase" in the abstract and conclusion.

Recommended steps:
- polarized spectra with Y∥b on a single-domain region, verified by micro-diffraction or polarization-angle dependence;
- a full mode table with fit uncertainties (currently in the SI);
- evidence that each new mode's intensity scales with a DW order parameter;
- a check for intergrowth phases at the probed spot;
- ideally, SHG or IR/Raman coincidence to test inversion breaking.

Otherwise the claim should be reworded as "new Raman-active excitations below T_DW", not a symmetry assignment.

**4. The presence and character of the DW in these specific crystals is not established, and the XRD null result is not quantified.**
- The paper cites T_DW from the literature but shows no resistivity, susceptibility or specific heat on its own flux-grown crystals.
- The oxygen content (La₄Ni₃O₁₀₋δ) is not determined. EPMA with a KTiOPO₄ standard confirms only La:Ni = 4:3. Possible K incorporation from the K₂CO₃ flux is not checked.
- Both T_DW and the structural behaviour are sensitive to δ. Together with the absence of satellites, this leaves open whether the samples host the DW of Ref. [25].
- For the DESY data, please:
  - state the q positions searched, including those reported by Zhang et al.;
  - give an upper limit on satellite intensity relative to main reflections;
  - compare that limit with the satellite/Bragg ratio implied in Ref. [25].

The high-pressure "no satellites down to 20 K" result is uninformative, since the DW is suppressed within a few GPa.

### B. Should address

**5. The DFT energetics and numerical robustness are insufficient to "definitely rule out" intermediate phases.**
- The Methods state total-energy convergence of 1 mHa (~27 meV). The relaxation also needed a 5×10⁻⁶ Ha/Bohr force threshold because structures are "energetically very close". Energy differences between P2₁/c and a Bmab-constrained relaxation are likely sub-meV per formula unit.
- Please report E(Bmab) − E(P2₁/c) and E(I4/mmm) − E(P2₁/c) versus pressure, with their numerical uncertainty.
- State the smearing and spin treatment (nonmagnetic?). Test sensitivity to the 2×4×4 k-mesh, which is coarse for a metal.
- The 1 GPa pressure grid cannot exclude an intermediate window narrower than ~1 GPa. Pc = 16.47 ± 0.04 GPa comes from three-point fits with free Pc and α, which overstates the precision.
- DFT overestimates the basal distortion by about 2×: ≈7×10⁻³ at 0 GPa in Fig. 5 vs ≈3×10⁻³ at 20 K in Fig. 4(c). This should be discussed alongside the claimed agreement.
- Zone-centre/X-point phonons, or a tilt-mode energy landscape, would strengthen the "no intermediate phase" statement.

**6. The order of the transition is stated inconsistently and not analysed.**
- p. 8 states both a "jump in β… weak first-order" and that the transition "is continuous". The DFT is fitted with mean-field (Pc − P)^½ scaling.
- Fig. 3(c) at 80 K shows θ ≈ 7° at ~10 GPa while the boundary is placed at ≈13 GPa, suggesting either a discontinuity or missing data.
- The authors should provide a group-theoretical (e.g., ISOTROPY) analysis of the order-parameter irreps for I4/mmm → P2₁/c. In particular, they should say whether a direct continuous transition is symmetry-allowed or requires coupled irreps, which would generically make it first order.
- Experimentally, please add:
  - superlattice intensity versus pressure at each temperature, as an order parameter;
  - compression/decompression hysteresis or phase coexistence;
  - the pressure path at low temperature (pressure changed isothermally in solid He, or the cell cooled at fixed membrane pressure).

**7. The phase-boundary determination is sparsely sampled and internally inconsistent across figures.**
- Fig. 1 and the text place the 300 K onset at 10.5 GPa, and Fig. 2(c) says F4/mmm at 11.3 GPa. The 2/m/4/mmm shading in Fig. 4 at 300 K appears to extend to ~12–13 GPa. Fig. 3(c) shows non-zero tilt (~1.5°) at ~11 GPa.
- Fig. 2(a) at 300 K has no data between 9 and 16 GPa.
- The "linear suppression" in Fig. 1 connects a single ambient-pressure point (1030 K) to points above 10 GPa. No data constrain the curve between them.
- Please state the criterion used for each point and give bracketing pressures with uncertainties. Compare the RT critical pressure explicitly with Refs. [19, 20, 22].

**8. The superconductivity statement overreaches.**
- The conclusion "affirms that superconductivity emerges only once the system is restored to the tetragonal phase". However, no superconductivity measurement is reported here, and the paper itself notes reported onset pressures span 5–40 GPa.
- The relevant comparison is the structure at T ≈ Tc (25–30 K) at the onset pressure, ideally on the same samples. The statement should be tempered to "consistent with".

**9. Use the existing ambient-pressure XRD to test for a lattice response at T_DW.**
- PXRD studies [36–38] report anomalous thermal expansion along b near T_DW. The DESY data (refinements at 230 and 80 K) could provide a, b, c, β and (b−a)/(a+b) versus T across 130 K.
- An anomaly at T_DW would directly support a DW-coupled distortion, independent of the Raman mode counting.

**10. The equation-of-state results need qualification.**
- K₀ rises from 155(4) GPa at 300 K to 182–185 GPa at 80/20 K, a large change for a 220 K temperature difference.
- Please state K′ and whether it was fixed. Say whether data on both sides of the transition were fitted jointly, and whether the different pressure ranges per temperature bias K₀.

### C. Minor

**11.** The Table I caption says the Poynting vector is "perpendicular to X", contradicting the Porto notation X(YY)X̄.
- Axis settings also differ between sections: Raman uses b unique with the (bc) plane; DFT has the stacking axis "along a and doubled"; Fig. 3 uses another. Please give one transformation table used throughout and state the crystal face probed in Raman.

**12.** T_DW is variously given as ≈135 K (abstract), 130 K, "120–150 K" and "below ~120 K". Use a single, measured value.

**13.** "At 15 GPa the octahedra is perfect" (p. 8) conflicts with the preceding description of strongly distorted octahedra. Presumably "untilted" is meant.

**14.** The Fig. 3(b) caption gives Ni1–O2–Ni2 = 171.1° (θ ≈ 8.9°) at 300 K, 6 GPa. Fig. 3(c) appears to show ≈6–7° at that pressure. Please check.

**15.** Fig. 2(a) uses a different reflection at each temperature (4 6 0, 6 4 0, −2 6 0). Use the same reflection, or overlay the splitting predicted by the twin law for each.

**16.** HP-setting a ≈ 3.7 Å is quoted generically, while 5.41/√2 ≈ 3.83 Å at low pressure. Specify the pressure.

**17.** In the Pc/P2₁ mode count, subtract the acoustic modes (51 A + 51 B includes them).

**18.** Explain how the 26 K sample temperature under laser heating was calibrated (e.g., Stokes/anti-Stokes ratio).

**19.** The claim that the transition is "impervious to… flux or floating zone" growth is not tested here; only flux crystals were studied.

**20.** Typos and formatting:
- "Gabarino" vs "Garbarino";
- "propoposal", "PsuedoDojo";
- the missing parenthesis in the EPMA standard;
- lower-case chemical formulas in many references;
- "n/a" in Ref. [22].

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

1. **Fine-step pressure scans with an order parameter.** Measure finely near Pc at 300 K and 20 K on both compression and decompression. Track superlattice intensity, θ and twin-resolved β against pressure. This would settle the order of the transition and the Bmab question together.
2. **A dedicated symmetry test for the low-temperature phase.** Combine axis-aligned, single-domain polarized Raman (angle-resolved) with SHG or IR. Repeat on a floating-zone crystal known to show DW satellites, to separate intrinsic from sample-dependent effects.
3. **Characterize the samples and bound the satellites.** Measure oxygen stoichiometry (TGA/iodometry), check for K, and run transport/magnetization on the measured batch. Repeat the satellite search with long exposures at the Ref. [25] wavevectors and report a quantitative upper limit.
4. **Strengthen the DFT.** Add an energy-landscape and phonon (soft-mode) analysis, together with an irreps decomposition of the P2₁/c distortion relative to I4/mmm. This would show whether "no intermediate phase" reflects symmetry or a fine energetic balance beyond PBE accuracy.
