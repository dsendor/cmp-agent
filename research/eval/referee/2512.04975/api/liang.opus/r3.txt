# Review outline

**Manuscript:** "Tracing the horizon of tetragonal-to-monoclinic distortion in pressurized trilayer nickelate La₄Ni₃O₁₀" (Ramakrishnan, Gao et al.)

## 1. Significance and novelty

**Scientific context**
- The structural ground state of the Ruddlesden–Popper (RP) trilayer nickelate La₄Ni₃O₁₀ under pressure is central to the debate on pressure-induced superconductivity (SC).
- The key open question is which phase hosts SC: tetragonal I4/mmm, an orthorhombic intermediate (Bmab, as proposed by Li et al. [22]), or monoclinic P2₁/c.
- A consistent pressure–temperature (P–T) structural phase diagram down to low temperature is not yet available in the literature.

**Claimed advances**
1. A P–T phase diagram from single-crystal XRD (SXRD) in a helium-medium diamond-anvil cell at 300, 80 and 20 K. It shows the structural transition temperature T_S suppressed from ~1030 K at ambient pressure to ~20 K at 14 GPa.
2. A claimed *direct* I4/mmm → P2₁/c transition with no Bmab intermediate. This is supported by:
   - fits favouring B2₁/a over Bmab (Table S4);
   - PBE relaxations in which the three order parameters vanish together at P_c ≈ 16.5 GPa.
3. A unified crystallographic framework connecting the settings P2₁/c ↔ B2₁/a and I4/mmm ↔ F4/mmm, including a description of four-domain pseudo-merohedral twinning.
4. No density-wave (DW) satellites detected by synchrotron SXRD down to 20 K, in contradiction with Zhang et al. [25].
5. Raman evidence of new phonon modes below ~130 K, interpreted as a third structural phase with symmetry lower than 2/m (Pc or P2₁).

**Novelty assessment**
- The ambient P2₁/c structure, its twinning, the high-temperature transition and the room-temperature pressure-induced monoclinic→tetragonal transition were all previously reported [19–24].
- The genuinely new elements are:
  - the low-temperature P–T boundary;
  - the explicit rebuttal of the Bmab intermediate;
  - the Raman claim of an additional low-temperature phase.
- These are potentially important. However, the strongest claims ("unequivocal", "third structural phase", "SC emerges only in the tetragonal phase") currently rest on evidence that is marginal or indirect (see Section 3).

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

**High-quality, technically demanding data set**
- Helium-loaded membrane DACs with ruby pressure calibration before and after each acquisition.
- Measurements at 300, 80 and 20 K.
- Complementary ambient-pressure SXRD at P24 and high-temperature PXRD at BM01.

**Clarifying crystallographic bookkeeping**
- The explicit transformations between standard and non-standard settings are useful to the community.
- The authors point out that the large-looking β ≈ 100° of standard P2₁/c is a setting artefact, and that the physical monoclinicity is only ~0.1°. This helps reconcile conflicting literature reports.

**Multi-probe consistency on the main structural point**
- Reflection broadening and splitting (Fig. 2a), superlattice reflections at (½,½,0) (Fig. 2b), octahedral tilt (Fig. 3c) and lattice-parameter trends (Fig. 4) are mutually consistent.
- DFT provides a qualitatively coherent picture: square-root tilt scaling and a P_c of the right magnitude.

**Timely relevance**
- The phase diagram gives a direct structural reference for the many transport and spectroscopy studies of pressurized La₄Ni₃O₁₀.
- The Raman data (≥51 modes at 26 K) add to the ongoing debate on DW-induced lattice effects [48–50].

## 3. Potential reasons for rejection

### R1. The experimental discrimination between a direct I4/mmm → B2₁/a transition and an intermediate Bmab phase is not demonstrated with the rigour implied by "unequivocally".

**The monoclinic angle is at the resolution limit**
- At 300 K, Fig. 4(b) shows β within ~0.05° of 90° over essentially the entire 2/m range, and β ≈ 90° at the 10.5 GPa onset.
- The DFT section itself states that β ~ 10⁻¹ deg is "close to the experimental accuracy".
- With four partially overlapping twin domains and anisotropic, split reflections (EVAL15 failed and box integration was used), the e.s.d. on β is likely underestimated. The paper gives no systematic-error analysis covering:
  - domain-dependent peak centroids;
  - detector geometry;
  - DAC-induced absorption.
- β ≠ 90° alone therefore cannot carry the "absence of Bmab" claim near the boundary, which is exactly where an intermediate phase would sit.

**Intensity-based model comparison (Table S4) is insufficiently specified**
- B2₁/a has more free parameters than Bmab and is refined on twinned data with refinable twin fractions. A lower R-value is therefore expected regardless of the true symmetry.
- The following are needed:
  - number of parameters and reflections;
  - twin fractions and twin laws for both models;
  - weighted R on *observed* reflections;
  - a Hamilton R-ratio test or equivalent significance test.
- The most decisive discriminator is not used: reflections allowed in B2₁/a but forbidden by the Bmab glide conditions, i.e. violations of the extinction rules of the "b" glide in Bmab not attributable to B-centering. Their observation, intensity versus pressure, and robustness against twin overlap (ideally with simulated twinned extinction patterns) should be shown.
- The authors attribute weak B-centering-violating spots (Fig. 2c) to twinning. This is the same observation Li et al. [22] interpreted differently. No quantitative twin-law simulation is presented to show that the observed positions and intensities are reproduced by the four-domain model and not by the alternative.

**Internal inconsistency on the order of the transition, and no Landau/group-theoretical analysis**
- The text calls the transition both "weak first-order" (jump in β, Fig. 4b) and "continuous bypassing the orthorhombic phase". These are different scenarios with different implications.
- A direct transition from I4/mmm to a monoclinic subgroup, skipping the intermediate isotropy subgroup Bmab, requires one of:
  - an order parameter along a low-symmetry direction of a multi-dimensional irreducible representation;
  - the coupling of two primary order parameters (e.g. two tilt systems), which generically yields a first-order transition or a tetracritical point.
- The relevant irreps (e.g. X-point tilt modes) and the isotropy-subgroup tree are not identified, so the physical mechanism of the "direct" transition remains unexplained.
- Fig. 3c shows a sizeable tilt (~7°) at 80 K near 9–10 GPa while the 80 K boundary is placed near ~13 GPa. This suggests either a discontinuous jump of the primary order parameter or an incomplete data set near the boundary. The authors should reconcile this with the "continuous" description.

### R2. The DFT evidence does not support the strong qualitative conclusions drawn from it.

**Physical limitations of the calculation are not addressed**
- The calculation appears to be non-spin-polarized PBE with no +U and no treatment of the ambient-pressure spin/charge DW.
- It is strictly a T = 0 static-lattice relaxation.
- The experimentally relevant question is whether an intermediate Bmab phase exists at *finite* temperature, e.g. along the ambient-pressure 1030 K transition or at 300 K under pressure. A T = 0 enthalpy minimisation cannot rule out an entropically stabilised intermediate phase.
- The claim that DFT "definitely rules out" intermediate structures therefore overreaches. At most, DFT shows that at T = 0 within PBE the P2₁/c minimum connects directly to I4/mmm.
- Magnetism and the DW can renormalise tilt instabilities in nickelates. At least a test with spin polarisation (and/or a U scan) at selected pressures is needed.

**The energy landscape is extremely flat and the method is relaxation-only**
- The Methods state that a force/stress threshold of 5×10⁻⁶ Ha/Bohr was "absolutely critical". With 5×10⁻⁵ the relaxation could stop in tetragonal or orthorhombic structures, depending on the starting point.
- Total energies are converged only to 1 mHa (~27 meV per cell). This is likely much larger than the relevant enthalpy differences, which raises the question of numerical robustness.
- A convincing demonstration would include:
  - enthalpy differences ΔH(P) between relaxed P2₁/c, Bmab-constrained and I4/mmm-constrained structures, converged well below their magnitude;
  - phonon or frozen-phonon calculations of I4/mmm identifying the unstable modes and their pressure dependence (soft-mode picture);
  - relaxation in other candidate subgroups not contained in the chosen P2₁/c cell (e.g. other tilt patterns such as P4₂/ncm-type).
- The non-monotonic β−90° with a maximum at ~4 GPa, and the breakdown of mean-field scaling, are presented as remarkable. They are left uninterpreted and cannot be tested experimentally, as the authors acknowledge.

**The quantitative agreement with experiment is partly fortuitous**
- The authors' own estimate of the PBE pressure error is ΔP = 1–3 GPa.
- The DFT point is plotted at T = 0, while the experimental boundary at 20 K is 14 GPa. Agreement "within error" therefore does not independently validate the experimental boundary or the absence of an intermediate phase.
- It should be explained why the secondary order parameters (basal strain, β−90°) vanish at the same P_c in terms of Landau coupling, e.g. strain ∝ (tilt)², hence linear in (P_c−P). Simultaneous vanishing is expected by symmetry and is not in itself independent evidence for a single transition.

### R3. The claim of a "third structural phase" with symmetry below 2/m is not supported by the Raman analysis and contradicts the authors' own caveats.

**Selection rules and mode counting are vulnerable to polarization leakage and twinning**
- In P2₁/c with b unique, Ag has xx, yy, zz and xz tensor components, and Bg has xy and yz.
- Table I states that Y and Z lie in the (bc) plane "but not aligned with a particular axis". In that case the parallel channel contains contributions of both Ag (α_yy cos² + α_zz sin²) and Bg (2α_yz sin·cos). The simple assignment "yy → 24 Ag, yz → 24 Bg" is then invalid.
- The four-domain twinning further mixes in-plane orientations within the laser spot.
- The key observation (31 modes in the parallel channel > 24 Ag) is therefore not evidence of symmetry lowering. Only the total count matters, and it is 51 versus the allowed 48. This excess of 3 is marginal given:
  - possible weak second-order features;
  - impurity phases (NiO and La₂NiO₄ are present in the growth);
  - defect-activated IR modes;
  - the difficulty of resolving overlapping lines.
- An angle-resolved polarization study and explicit fit statistics for each "new" mode are required before a symmetry conclusion can be drawn.

**Alternative origins of new modes that preserve 2/m are not excluded**
- Below T_DW, new Raman lines can arise from:
  - amplitude (and phason) modes of the DW;
  - zone-folding of phonons from the DW wavevector (backfolding, which the authors themselves invoke);
  - electronic or magnetic excitations (the SDW is intertwined).
- None of these requires breaking the *point group* 2/m. A (super)space group with point group 2/m and an enlarged or modulated cell produces many more Γ-point modes than 48.
- The specific inference to the polar groups Pc or P2₁ (Table I) assumes the same unit cell as P2₁/c. That assumption is inconsistent with a translational-symmetry-breaking DW.
- The manuscript itself states that "the familiar Raman selection rules [are] no longer applicable" for a modulated state. Yet the Abstract and Conclusion assert a breaking of P2₁/c / 2/m symmetry. The conclusion should be downgraded or tested independently, e.g. by:
  - second-harmonic generation for polar Pc/P2₁;
  - pyroelectric or piezoelectric response;
  - high-resolution diffraction.

**The Raman claims sit uneasily with the XRD results and other Raman reports**
- The paper's own SXRD sees no lattice distortion and no satellites below 130 K, and reports "no discernible structural distortions associated with DW ordering". The Abstract nonetheless proposes a new structural phase.
- Gim et al. [48] see no new modes, and Suthar et al. [49] see 48. The authors attribute these differences to sample quality and composition but do not characterise their own composition beyond the La:Ni ratio (see R4).
- Without a sample-specific explanation, the "third phase" may be extrinsic.

### R4. Phase-diagram construction, sample characterization and some broader claims lack sufficient support.

**The boundary is defined by sparse data and a partly subjective criterion**
- The diagram rests on very few points: one PXRD point at ambient pressure and ~1030 K, then SXRD onsets at 300 K (~10.5 GPa), 80 K (~13 GPa) and 20 K (~14 GPa).
- There are no points between 0 and ~10 GPa above room temperature. The "linear suppression" over 1000 K is essentially a line through two clusters of points.
- T_S under pressure is identified from qualitative broadening or splitting of a single reflection per isotherm (Fig. 2a). This criterion depends on resolution, mosaicity and domain size. The superlattice-reflection intensity at (½,½,0), a direct measure of the primary (tilt) order parameter, would be a more objective criterion and should be plotted versus P with error bars.
- For a transition described as weak first-order, compression/decompression hysteresis is not reported.
- The ambient-pressure point comes from PXRD on a ground, multiphase powder (NiO and La₂NiO₄ present), while the high-pressure points come from single crystals. Combining these on one line requires justification.

**Sample stoichiometry and structural quality are under-characterized**
- Oxygen content (δ) is known to shift transition and SC pressures in RP nickelates (cf. La₄Ni₃O₁₀₋δ in [19, 22]).
- EPMA oxygen quantification is unreliable at the needed precision, and only the La:Ni ratio is confirmed. Thermogravimetry or refined O occupancies are needed.
- Flux-grown crystals can contain K incorporation and RP intergrowths (n = 2 or n = 4 stacking faults). Diffuse scattering along c* and refined occupancies should be examined.
- These factors are directly relevant to:
  - the RT transition pressure (~10.5 GPa here versus ~15 GPa reported elsewhere [19–22]), which is not discussed;
  - the non-observation of DW satellites;
  - the discrepancy with other Raman studies.

**The negative DW-satellite result is not quantified, and the SC statement is not tested**
- The absence of satellites contradicts x-ray and neutron results [25] and the STM observation of a unidirectional CDW [27].
- To be meaningful, the following must be stated:
  - the detection limit (e.g. I_sat/I_main threshold);
  - reciprocal-space maps at the specific wavevectors of Ref. [25];
  - counting statistics.
- The bulk-modulus variation (K₀ = 155 GPa at 300 K versus 182–185 GPa at 80/20 K) is unusually large. It suggests fitting correlations, e.g. K′ fixed or free and a narrow pressure range, that should be clarified.
- The conclusion that this "affirms that superconductivity emerges only once the system is restored to the tetragonal phase" is not tested on the authors' crystals: no transport or susceptibility is reported. The structural boundary at low T (~14 GPa) also lies close to the reported SC onsets, which span 5–40 GPa. This claim should be softened or supported by same-sample SC measurements.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

**S1. Strengthen the B2₁/a-versus-Bmab discrimination with decisive, quantitative crystallography.**
- Identify reflection classes allowed in B2₁/a but forbidden in Bmab, and show their maps and intensities versus P and T.
- Provide full refinement statistics for both models with identical twin models: parameters, reflections, twin fractions, R and wR, and a Hamilton test.
- Simulate the four-domain twinned diffraction pattern to show that the "centering-violating" spots in Fig. 2c arise from twinning.
- Give a realistic systematic error on β and on (b−a)/(b+a).
- Add an isotropy-subgroup/Landau analysis identifying the primary order parameter(s), explaining how a direct transition to P2₁/c is possible, and settling whether it is continuous or first-order.

**S2. Make the DFT a genuine test rather than a corroboration.**
- Report converged enthalpy differences between symmetry-constrained I4/mmm, Fmmm, Bmab and P2₁/c structures versus pressure, with energy convergence well below these differences.
- Compute zone-boundary phonons (or frozen-phonon energy surfaces in the two-tilt order-parameter space) of I4/mmm versus pressure to reveal the soft modes and the shape of the Landau landscape.
- Test spin-polarized and/or DFT+U solutions at a few pressures.
- Explicitly restrict the conclusions to T = 0. If possible, estimate finite-temperature effects, e.g. a quasi-harmonic or effective-Hamiltonian treatment of the tilt modes.

**S3. Rework the Raman analysis and temper the "third phase" claim.**
- Perform angle-resolved polarization measurements with polarizations aligned to b and c (verified by Laue or XRD on the same crystal) to establish clean Ag/Bg channels and quantify leakage.
- Map different twin domains, or use a micro-spot on a single domain.
- Provide fits and confidence criteria for every "new" mode.
- Discuss explicitly the alternatives that preserve point group 2/m: DW amplitude modes, zone-folded phonons, magnetic excitations.
- Test the proposed polar symmetries (Pc/P2₁) with SHG.
- Revise the Abstract and Conclusions to reflect that Raman indicates translational and/or point-symmetry breaking associated with the DW, not a demonstrated Pc/P2₁ phase.

**S4. Densify and objectify the phase diagram, characterize samples, and align claims with the evidence.**
- Add isobars or additional isotherms (e.g. 150–250 K and above RT if feasible) and plot the superlattice intensity and tilt versus P as order parameters.
- Report compression/decompression hysteresis.
- Determine oxygen stoichiometry (TGA or refined occupancies), check for K incorporation and RP intergrowths, and discuss the ~5 GPa discrepancy in the RT transition pressure with Refs. [19–22] in terms of δ.
- Quantify the detection limit of the DW-satellite search at the wavevectors of Ref. [25].
- Clarify the EOS fitting (fixed or free K′, correlations).
- Either measure SC on the same crystals or rephrase the SC statement as consistent with, rather than affirming, prior reports.
- Remove internal inconsistencies:
  - T_DW quoted as 120, 130 and 135 K;
  - "continuous" versus "weak first-order";
  - the description of the octahedral distortion at 15 GPa as "perfect".
- Tighten the language (e.g. "unequivocally", "defies interpretation") to improve clarity for a broad readership.
