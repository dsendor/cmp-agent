# Review outline

## 1. Significance and novelty

**Topic and motivation.**
- The structural ground state of pressurized La₄Ni₃O₁₀ is central to the debate on superconductivity in Ruddlesden–Popper nickelates.
- Competing proposals exist:
  - P2₁/a(c) → I4/mmm (Zhu et al., Zhang et al.).
  - An intermediate Bmab phase (Li et al., Adv. Mater. 2025).
  - Ambiguity over whether the density-wave (DW) phase lowers the lattice symmetry.

**What the manuscript combines.**
- High-temperature synchrotron PXRD at ambient pressure (T_S ≈ 1030 K).
- Helium-medium single-crystal XRD in a diamond anvil cell at 300, 80 and 20 K, up to ~16 GPa.
- Ambient-pressure low-temperature SXRD at DESY down to 20 K.
- DFT-PBE relaxations versus pressure.
- Polarized Raman scattering across T_DW.

**Claimed advances.**
- (i) A direct 4/mmm → 2/m transition, described as a 2-fold superstructure with q = (½,½,0), with no Bmab intermediate.
- (ii) A P–T phase boundary running from 1030 K (0 GPa) to 20 K (14 GPa), with DFT P_c ≈ 16.5 GPa at 0 K.
- (iii) No XRD-detectable DW satellites at ambient pressure.
- (iv) Raman evidence (51 modes > 48 allowed in P2₁/c) for a third, lower-symmetry phase below ~130 K.

**Assessment of novelty.**
- The room-temperature P2₁/a → I4/mmm transition under pressure is already reported. The incremental novelty lies in:
  - extending the boundary to low temperature (where superconductivity is measured);
  - the explicit, consistent setting transformations (B2₁/a ↔ P2₁/c, F4/mmm ↔ I4/mmm);
  - the Raman claim of symmetry breaking below T_DW.
- The low-T phase boundary and a clear framework for setting choices would be valuable to the community.
- However, the two headline "resolutions" rest on evidence that is currently weaker than the language used: the absence of Bmab, and the "third structural phase."

## 2. Potential reasons for acceptance

- **High-quality experimental conditions.**
  - Helium pressure medium with good hydrostaticity.
  - Ruby pressure measured before and after each collection.
  - Synchrotron SXRD at three temperatures over a dense pressure grid.
  - Low-temperature SXRD at an independent beamline on the same crystal batch.
- **First systematic tracking at low temperature.** The structural transition is followed at 80 and 20 K, directly relevant to the pressure range where superconductivity onsets (~14–16 GPa). This gives a practical map for interpreting transport data.
- **Pedagogical clarity on crystallographic settings.** The setting transformations and twin-domain relations (four pseudo-merohedral domains) help reconcile disparate literature values. Examples include β ≈ 100° in P2₁/c versus β − 90° ≈ 0.1–0.4° in B2₁/a.
- **Complementary probes.**
  - DFT reproduces the order of magnitude of the tilt angle and P_c.
  - Raman provides spectroscopic evidence of changes at T_DW, including new modes and anomalous softening and broadening of the 350 and 470 cm⁻¹ A_g modes.
  - The Raman data are novel and potentially important; they contradict Gim et al. and go beyond the mode count of Suthar et al.
- **Careful reporting of the DFT numerics.** The force/stress-threshold sensitivity is disclosed. This is a useful and honest methodological warning for the community.

## 3. Potential reasons for rejection

### 3.1 The central claim ("direct 4/mmm → 2/m, no Bmab") is not demonstrated with the rigor its "unequivocal" wording implies

**Discrimination relies on β and R-factors.**
- Near the transition (10.5 GPa, 300 K), β − 90° is ≈ 0–0.05° according to Fig. 4b. This is at or below the resolution of a twinned DAC experiment with anisotropically broadened, partially overlapping reflections.
- The "much better" B2₁/a fit (Table S4) is not assessed against the extra refinable parameters (lower symmetry, additional twin fractions). An improved R value is expected automatically.
- What is needed instead:
  - a Hamilton R-ratio or equivalent significance test;
  - explicit identification of reflection classes allowed in P2₁/c (B2₁/a) but systematically absent in Bmab, with their observed intensities and σ, separated from twin-overlap positions.
- Without this, the data at the onset cannot exclude a narrow Bmab window. That window is precisely what Li et al. propose.

**The nature of the transition is internally inconsistent and lacks symmetry-mode analysis.**
- The text calls the transition "weak first-order" (jump in β, Fig. 4b) and also "continuous." The DFT section fits mean-field square-root scaling.
- No irreducible-representation (ISOTROPY/AMPLIMODES) analysis is given. Readers therefore cannot tell:
  - whether I4/mmm → P2₁/c is reachable through a single X-point order parameter (and along which direction);
  - or whether it requires two coupled irreps (e.g., distinct tilt systems in inner and outer octahedral layers). In that case a direct transition is generically first-order, and an intermediate phase is symmetry-allowed.
- No pressure up/down hysteresis is reported, so first- versus second-order character cannot be judged.

**The phase diagram is sparse, and its extrapolation is questionable.**
- The "linear" boundary across ~1000 K rests on essentially four points: 1030 K at 0 GPa; ~10.5 GPa at 300 K; ~13 GPa at 80 K; ~14 GPa at 20 K. There are no data between 0–10 GPa above 300 K.
- Thermodynamics (Clausius–Clapeyron with vanishing entropy difference) requires the boundary to become vertical as T → 0. The near-linear drawing to ~15 GPa at 0 K is therefore unphysical at low T.
- The comparison "20 K at 14 GPa versus DFT 16.5 GPa at 0 K" is consequently ambiguous.
- The 300 K onset (~10.5 GPa) also appears lower than several literature values. This discrepancy, and possible sample dependence (oxygen content δ), are not discussed.

**Twin assignment of the centering-violating spots is asserted, not demonstrated.**
- The weak spots that violate B/F centering (Fig. 2c) are attributed to twin domains. Li et al. interpret similar features differently.
- A quantitative twin model is required, ideally an overlay of predicted domain lattices with refined domain fractions. It should show that every extra spot is reproduced and that no residual intensity remains.

### 3.2 The DFT evidence is overstated and methodologically incomplete

**Local relaxation is not a phase-stability proof.**
- The authors themselves show that relaxations stop in I4/mmm or orthorhombic structures with a 10× looser threshold. This reveals an extremely flat landscape.
- Showing that a P2₁/c-unconstrained relaxation lands in P2₁/c does not "rule out" Bmab. What would:
  - enthalpy differences H(P) between separately relaxed I4/mmm, Fmmm, Bmab and P2₁/c structures, resolved below the 1 mHa convergence quoted;
  - zone-boundary phonon (X-point) instabilities.
- Finite-temperature and anharmonic effects, known to stabilize intermediate tilt phases in RP compounds, are absent. So is zero-point motion.

**Electronic and magnetic treatment and comparison with experiment.**
- The calculation is not specified. It is unclear whether it is spin-polarized, whether +U or magnetic order (relevant to the SDW) is included, and how oxygen stoichiometry is handled. Any of these can shift tilt energetics by amounts comparable to the energy scales at play.
- The admitted PBE pressure error (ΔP ≈ 1–3 GPa) is comparable to the agreement being claimed with experiment.
- The fits show a "quick breakdown" of the square-root law for β and a maximum near 4 GPa. This suggests β is a secondary (coupled) order parameter, not a primary one. It undercuts the use of three order parameters "nullifying at the same P_c" as independent evidence.

### 3.3 The Raman-based inference of a "third structural phase below 2/m" is not supported by the analysis presented

**Selection rules and mode counting are fragile.**
- Polarizations Y and Z are stated to lie in the bc plane but "not aligned with a particular axis." Under that geometry:
  - both yy and yz channels contain A_g and B_g components in P2₁/c;
  - the four twin domains (with in-plane axes interchanged) mix channels further.
- The assignments "24 A_g in yy / 24 B_g in yz" (Table I) and the claim that modes "clearly follow" A/B selection rules are therefore not justified.
- The conclusion rests on 51 versus 48 modes, a margin of only 3. It requires demonstrating that no mode is double-counted across yy and yz, and that second-order, overtone or leakage features have been excluded.
- The counting is described only as "conservative" in the SI.

**Alternatives are not excluded.**
- *Impurity and intergrowth phases.* The authors' own PXRD shows NiO and La₂NiO₄ side products, and RP intergrowths (n = 1, 2) are common in flux growth. NiO phonon and magnon features (~400–570 cm⁻¹) and La₂NiO₄ (which has its own low-T structural transitions) could contribute new or temperature-dependent peaks, for example the new 515 cm⁻¹ mode. Reference spectra and spatial mapping are needed.
- *Translational symmetry breaking.* Only the translationengleiche subgroups (P1̄, Pc, P2₁, P1) are considered. Klassengleiche subgroups, i.e., a commensurate DW or zone folding with a q ≠ 0 supercell, would also yield > 48 Raman modes while preserving the 2/m point group and centrosymmetry. The paper itself invokes this backfolding mechanism.
- Hence the claim of a structure "below 2/m" (point-group breaking, non-centrosymmetric Pc/P2₁) does not follow. Γ-point mode counting is also inconsistent with the acknowledged incommensurate character of the DW.

**XRD/Raman tension is left unresolved.**
- A bulk transition to Pc or P2₁ should produce measurable consequences: lattice-parameter anomalies, intensity changes in specific reflections, or newly allowed reflections.
- The ambient-pressure SXRD (refined at 230 and 80 K) is not analyzed for any of these. No temperature dependence of lattice parameters across T_DW is shown, despite the reported negative thermal expansion of b.

### 3.4 Key sample-level and supporting evidence is missing, and the broader conclusions exceed the data

**Sample characterization and the DW itself are not established.**
- No resistivity, magnetization or specific-heat data on the same flux-grown batch confirm that these crystals exhibit the DW at ~130–135 K. (The abstract gives 135 K; the text gives 130 K and ~120 K.)
- Oxygen stoichiometry (δ) is not determined; EPMA confirms only La:Ni = 4:3. Yet δ is known to control both the structural transition and the DW, and is the most likely origin of discrepancies with Zhang et al. and Li et al.
- The null result for incommensurate satellites is reported without a quantitative detection limit. A useful bound would be the upper intensity relative to main reflections at the specific q reported by Zhang et al. (and its harmonics). Without it, "contrary to Zhang et al." is not a meaningful contradiction.

**Over-reach and internal inconsistencies.**
- "This affirms that superconductivity emerges only once the system is restored to the tetragonal phase" is not tested: no superconductivity measurements are presented on these samples or at these pressures.
- The EOS gives K₀ = 155(4) GPa at 300 K versus 182–185 GPa at 80/20 K. This ~18% stiffening is unusually large and suggests fitting across the phase boundary or a fixed-K′ artefact; it needs justification.
- Inconsistent statements appear:
  - the octahedra are "strongly distorted" yet "perfect at 15 GPa" (presumably meaning untilted);
  - the 300 K distortion is called "negligible," while Fig. 4c shows (b − a)/(a + b) ≈ 2–3 × 10⁻³.
- Words such as "unequivocally" and "fully corroborate" overstate the case.
- Data are available only "upon reasonable request," and no CIFs or deposition numbers are given. For a structural paper whose main contribution is a symmetry assignment, this is a significant limitation.

## 4. Suggestions for improvement

**1. Make the symmetry assignment rigorous and falsifiable.**
- Provide a full group–subgroup and irrep (ISOTROPY/AMPLIMODES) analysis of I4/mmm → Fmmm/Bmab/P2₁/c. Specify the order-parameter directions and the Landau-allowed transition order.
- Report observed intensities (with I/σ) of reflection classes that discriminate P2₁/c from Bmab, separated from twin-overlap positions.
- Include a quantitative twin model reproducing all "centering-violating" spots, and apply Hamilton tests to the B2₁/a versus Bmab refinements.
- Add compression/decompression hysteresis and more P–T points, especially 1–10 GPa at elevated T. Redraw the boundary without an unphysical linear extrapolation to T = 0.
- Directly confront the Li et al. data or criteria (e.g., re-index their reported reflections).

**2. Strengthen and temper the DFT.**
- Compute enthalpy-versus-pressure curves for independently relaxed I4/mmm, Fmmm, Bmab and P2₁/c structures.
- Compute X-point phonon spectra to identify soft modes and their coupling. Test sensitivity to spin polarization, DFT+U and functional choice (e.g., PBEsol, which corrects PBE volume errors relevant to P_c).
- Treat β as a secondary order parameter in the scaling analysis.
- Rephrase the conclusion as "within PBE at 0 K, no intermediate phase is found," rather than "unequivocally ruled out."

**3. Rebuild the Raman argument on secure footing.**
- Measure with polarizations aligned to the crystallographic in-plane axes, ideally on single domains identified by polarized microscopy or micro-Raman mapping, so that A_g/B_g selection rules are meaningful.
- Provide the full mode table showing no cross-channel double counting.
- Acquire reference spectra of NiO, La₂NiO₄ and La₃Ni₂O₇, and spatially map to exclude impurities and intergrowths.
- Extend the subgroup analysis to klassengleiche (supercell) subgroups and discuss zone folding by the DW wavevector. State clearly whether the data require point-group lowering or only translational-symmetry breaking.
- Raman under pressure, tracking the disappearance of the new modes, would directly link them to the DW and strongly support the "third phase" proposal.

**4. Close the sample and DW gap and moderate the claims.**
- Provide transport and/or susceptibility data on the same batch, demonstrating T_DW, plus oxygen-content determination (TGA or neutron/X-ray occupancy refinement).
- Quantify the XRD detection limit at the Zhang et al. wavevector.
- Show the temperature dependence of lattice parameters and selected reflection intensities across T_DW from the DESY data, and attempt refinement in Pc/P2₁ to test the Raman proposal.
- Justify the temperature dependence of the EOS (stating K′ treatment and fit ranges per phase).
- Remove or qualify the superconductivity conclusion, which is not tested here.
- Harmonize T_DW values and the continuous/first-order wording, and correct typographical errors ("PsuedoDojo," "propoposal").
- Deposit CIFs, Raman spectra and DFT structures in public repositories.
