**Review outline:**

**1. Significance and novelty**

- **Topic and relevance.** The manuscript addresses a timely and contested question: what structural phases does the pressure-induced trilayer nickelate superconductor La4Ni3O10 pass through? This matters because the superconductivity is widely linked to recovering a tetragonal (I4/mmm) lattice.
- **Claimed advances.** The authors claim four things:
  - a P–T structural phase diagram from flux-grown single crystals, spanning ambient pressure/1030 K to 14 GPa/20 K;
  - a direct I4/mmm → P21/c (B21/a) transition with no intermediate orthorhombic Bmab phase, contrary to Li et al. (Adv. Mater. 2025);
  - DFT-PBE relaxations in which all three order parameters vanish at a common Pc ≈ 16.5 GPa;
  - Raman evidence for a further symmetry lowering below P21/c near TDW, even though no density-wave satellites are seen in XRD.
- **Novelty is incremental-to-moderate.**
  - The ambient-pressure Ts, the room-temperature pressure-induced transition and the twinned superstructure are all already reported.
  - The genuinely new elements are the low-temperature high-pressure points, the "no-Bmab" claim, and the Raman mode count.
  - However, each of these rests on small effects: β − 90° ≈ 0.1–0.2°, and a margin of 3 modes over the 48 allowed. These are close to the resolution or interpretation limits of the methods.
- **Overall judgement.** In its present form, the significance for a top-tier venue depends on whether the central "direct transition" and "third phase" claims can be made robust. Currently they are suggestive rather than conclusive.

**2. Potential reasons for acceptance**

- **Data coverage.** The SXRD data set (He medium, ruby before/after each collection, 20–300 K, 0–16 GPa) is high quality and broad. Low-temperature/high-pressure structural data on this system are scarce and valuable to the community.
- **Consistent crystallographic framework.** The paper explicitly connects P21/c ↔ B21/a and I4/mmm ↔ F4/mmm. This is a useful pedagogical clarification that removes the apparent "large β ≈ 100°" monoclinicity, and can help reconcile conflicting literature.
- **Multi-technique approach.** XRD, DFT and polarized Raman are combined. The DFT result, if confirmed with proper energetics, gives a qualitative argument that the octahedral tilt, basal distortion and monoclinic angle vanish together.
- **Raman phenomenology.** The data are of good quality: new modes emerge, and the ~350 and ~470 cm⁻¹ Ag modes show anomalous softening and broadening near TDW. These are informative about electron–phonon coupling, independent of the symmetry assignment.
- **Experimental documentation.** Ambient-pressure SXRD at P24 tests for DW satellites, and the methods (integration strategy, pressure metrology) are described in reasonable detail.

**3. Potential reasons for rejection**

**3.1 The "direct tetragonal-to-monoclinic, no Bmab" conclusion is not established at the required level of rigor.**

- **β is at the resolution limit.**
  - The monoclinic angle differs from 90° by only ~0.1–0.2°. At the claimed onset (10.5 GPa, 300 K), Fig. 4b shows β ≈ 90° within scatter.
  - This is measured in a DAC on a crystal with four pseudo-merohedral twin domains and anisotropic, split reflections. Integration used EVAL14 box integration precisely because profiles are irregular.
  - No analysis shows that a refined β of 90.05–90.1° is statistically distinguishable from 90° once twin-domain overlap, systematic centroid shifts and DAC geometry are accounted for.
  - "Better fit" in Table S4 is not quantified in the main text. The following are needed:
    - the number of parameters and the twin model used for each refinement;
    - wR values computed on the same reflection sets;
    - a Hamilton R-ratio or similar significance test.
  - A lower-symmetry model with more free parameters and more twin fractions will almost always fit twinned data better.
- **Symmetry discrimination should rest on diagnostic reflections, not lattice metrics.**
  - The decisive distinction between Bmab and B21/a lies in their reflection conditions and tilt patterns: which glide/centring-violating reflections appear, and with what intensity.
  - The weak "centring-violating" reflections in Fig. 2c are attributed to twinning without an explicit twin law. The authors should show the twin law that maps allowed reflections of one domain onto those positions, with refined domain fractions.
  - Otherwise the same spots could equally signal a different space group, or the Bmab-type intermediate reported by Li et al.
  - Similarly, Fig. 2b shows both q1 = (½,½,0) and q2 = (½,−½,0) plus "mixed-order" combinations. These need explanation:
    - Are they a single-domain multi-k effect, or simply superposed domains?
    - Does q1 + q2 (an I-centring-forbidden position) carry intensity?
- **No group-theoretical (Landau) analysis.**
  - The authors do not identify the irreducible representation(s) of I4/mmm (X-point tilt modes) driving I4/mmm → P21/c.
  - A direct transition to P21/c that bypasses the isotropy subgroup Bmab generally requires either two coupled order parameters or a specific order-parameter direction.
  - That situation typically implies a first-order transition, or a narrow intermediate region that may be below experimental resolution.
  - Without an ISODISTORT-type decomposition of the refined structures into symmetry-adapted modes (with amplitudes vs. P and T), "no intermediate phase" is not demonstrated.
  - The P-step sampling near the boundary (~0.5–1.5 GPa) could miss a narrow Bmab window.
- **Internal inconsistency on transition order.**
  - Page 8 invokes "a jump in β … weak first-order" but also states the transition "is continuous".
  - The DFT is fitted with mean-field (Pc − P)^½ scaling, i.e. a continuous transition.
  - These statements are mutually incompatible as written and bear directly on whether an intermediate phase is thermodynamically expected.

**3.2 The DFT evidence is weaker than presented ("unequivocally").**

- **Relaxation-based evidence.**
  - The conclusion comes from relaxing within a single P21/c cell. The authors concede that a force threshold one order of magnitude looser yields tetragonal or orthorhombic outcomes depending on the starting structure. This indicates an extremely flat energy landscape.
  - The physically meaningful quantity is the enthalpy difference H(P21/c) − H(Bmab) − H(I4/mmm) vs. P, computed with each symmetry imposed. This is not reported.
  - If these differences are a few meV/f.u. or less, the PBE ground state says little about which phase appears at finite T.
  - Zero-point/thermal effects and anharmonicity, which can stabilize intermediate phases in tilt systems, are absent.
  - No phonon calculation demonstrates dynamical stability of I4/mmm above Pc, or the soft-mode character below it.
- **Undocumented physics and limited configuration space.**
  - The calculation's treatment of magnetism and correlations is not stated: spin polarization, any DFT+U, or antiferromagnetic/SDW configurations. These are known to affect octahedral tilts and lattice parameters in RP nickelates.
  - The P21/c cell excludes other tilt patterns and larger cells, so "free to span all four structures" is true only for this chosen subgroup chain. Competing structures outside it (e.g., other X/M-point combinations) are not tested.
- **Quantitative agreement is not a validation.**
  - The PBE lattice overestimation (~1%) is acknowledged to shift Pc by 1–3 GPa.
  - The β(P) maximum at ~4 GPa and the α = 0.4 deviation suggest non-mean-field behaviour the authors do not explain.
  - The agreement with experiment (Pc ≈ 16 GPa vs. ~14.5 GPa extrapolated) should therefore not be presented as validation. The DFT is a zero-temperature, single-functional result.

**3.3 The Raman-based claim of a third, lower-symmetry phase is not convincingly supported.**

- **The mode-count margin is thin and vulnerable to selection-rule leakage.**
  - The argument rests on 51 observed modes vs. 48 allowed (24 Ag + 24 Bg). However, in yy alone, 31 modes vs. 24 allowed Ag is the stronger claim and depends critically on polarization purity.
  - The crystal contains four twin domains related by ~90° rotations about the stacking axis. The unique b axis of one domain lies along an in-plane direction of another, so "yy" and "yz" spectra necessarily mix Ag and Bg across domains.
  - Y and Z are explicitly "not aligned with a particular axis". Leakage of Bg modes into yy could therefore explain the excess in the parallel channel.
  - The total of 51 then depends on the "conservative" separation of near-degenerate peaks, which must be demonstrated (fits, residuals, temperature tracking) in the main text.
- **Non-phonon or extrinsic origins of the new peaks are not excluded.**
  - Possible origins include:
    - zone-folded phonons from an incommensurate DW, which do not require a lower point-group symmetry, as the authors themselves note;
    - DW amplitude modes or magnetic (magnon/two-magnon) excitations below the SDW transition;
    - second-order scattering;
    - contributions from impurity phases or intergrowths.
  - The PXRD shows NiO and La2NiO4 in the flux product. La2NiO4 has its own low-temperature structural transition (LTO→LTT-type), and RP stacking intergrowths (e.g., La3Ni2O7-like layers) are common.
  - Spatial mapping, multiple crystals and comparison with reference spectra are needed.
  - Conversely, the conclusion that P-1 is excluded but Pc/P21 are allowed hinges on the same marginal count and on selection rules that are compromised by twinning.
- **Conflict with the XRD data is not resolved.**
  - At ambient pressure, structure refinements at 230 and 80 K (JANA2006) are available. These could test directly for loss of inversion or of the 21/c operations, via:
    - intensities of systematically absent reflections;
    - refinements in Pc/P21 with inversion twinning;
    - Flack-type parameters.
  - These tests are not reported.
  - The authors' own XRD therefore provides no evidence for the proposed third phase. Proposing an "uncharted" phase in the P–T diagram on the basis of Raman alone is an overreach.

**3.4 Sample characterization, key consistency checks and some conclusions are insufficient or overstated.**

- **No evidence that these crystals have the DW.**
  - No resistivity, susceptibility or specific-heat data are shown for the measured crystals.
  - XRD finds no DW satellites, contrary to Zhang et al. (2020). Without proof of TDW in the same batch, it is impossible to tell whether satellites are merely weak or the DW is absent or altered in flux-grown crystals.
  - This undermines the link between the Raman anomalies (120–150 K) and "DW-driven lattice distortions".
  - The detection limit for satellites (e.g., upper bound on I_sat/I_main) is not quantified.
  - Oxygen stoichiometry is not reported. EPMA confirms only La:Ni = 4:3, and no TGA or refined oxygen occupancies are given. Yet Li et al. study La4Ni3O10−δ, and the authors themselves suggest growth conditions matter; oxygen content could plausibly control both the Bmab question and the DW.
- **The phase diagram is sparse and partly at odds with prior reports.**
  - Between 300 and 1030 K there are no data. The "linear suppression" line connects ambient-pressure Ts to only three high-pressure points.
  - Thermodynamically, the boundary should become vertical as T → 0, so linear extrapolation is unjustified.
  - The transition criterion (onset of peak broadening/anisotropy) is qualitative. Fig. 2a shows broadening at 9 GPa at 300 K, whereas the superlattice criterion gives ~10.5 GPa and Fig. 3c gives tilt → 0 near 11 GPa.
  - The introduction cites Pc ≈ 15 GPa at room temperature from prior work, but the present data place it at ~10.5–11 GPa. This large discrepancy is not discussed (hydrostaticity, stoichiometry, criterion).
  - The EOS fits give K0 = 155 GPa (300 K) vs. 182–185 GPa (80/20 K). A ~17% stiffening over 220 K is physically implausible and suggests fitting artefacts:
    - K′ fixed or not;
    - correlation between K0 and K′;
    - fitting a single EOS across the phase transition.
- **Overstated superconductivity conclusion.**
  - "This affirms that superconductivity emerges only once the system is restored to the tetragonal phase" is not supported. No transport or magnetic measurements under pressure are presented, the sample-dependent Pc for SC spans 5–40 GPa, and the 20 K phase-boundary point (14 GPa) is close to the reported SC onset.
- **Presentation problems.**
  - Fig. 5 is partly garbled in the text version.
  - TDW is quoted variously as 120/130/135 K.
  - Statements such as "unequivocally" and "definitely ruled out" are not matched by the margins of the data.
  - Key quantitative evidence (Table S4/S5) is relegated to the SI.

**4. Suggestions for improvement**

1. **Make the symmetry assignment quantitative and diagnostic.**
   - Provide a full symmetry-mode (ISODISTORT) analysis identifying the active irreps for I4/mmm → Bmab and → P21/c, and refine mode amplitudes vs. P and T.
   - Report, in the main text, side-by-side refinements of I4/mmm/F4/mmm, Bmab and B21/a with:
     - identical reflection sets and explicit twin laws;
     - refined domain fractions;
     - parameter counts and wR/GoF;
     - Hamilton tests.
   - Show the specific reflection classes that distinguish Bmab from B21/a, and their intensities vs. pressure near the boundary.
   - Add denser pressure steps (≤0.25 GPa) across 9–12 GPa at 300 K and near 13–15 GPa at low T to exclude a narrow intermediate phase.
   - Resolve the continuous vs. weak-first-order inconsistency, e.g. via hysteresis on compression/decompression, coexistence, or the discontinuity of superlattice intensity.

2. **Strengthen the DFT.**
   - Report enthalpies of I4/mmm, Fmmm, Bmab and P21/c (symmetry-constrained relaxations) vs. pressure, in meV/f.u.
   - Compute phonons, or at least frozen-tilt energy curves, at Γ and X to show soft-mode behaviour and dynamical stability above Pc.
   - State and test the spin-polarized/AFM and DFT+U settings.
   - Assess functional dependence (e.g., PBEsol or SCAN, which reduce lattice overestimation).
   - Temper "unequivocal" wording accordingly, and discuss the physical origin of the β(P) maximum.

3. **Substantiate the Raman "third phase" claim or recast it more cautiously.**
   - Measure detwinned or single-domain regions, or map domains (polarized microscopy, micro-Raman mapping), and give a quantitative leakage analysis with explicit fits and residuals for all counted modes.
   - Rotate polarization relative to crystal axes to separate Ag/Bg cleanly.
   - Discuss and test alternatives: zone folding by the DW, amplitude/magnetic modes, second-order features, impurity/intergrowth contributions (compare with NiO and La2NiO4 spectra; check spatial homogeneity).
   - Use the existing 80 K ambient-pressure SXRD refinements to test Pc/P21 directly (absences, inversion twinning), and consider SHG to probe loss of inversion.
   - If the XRD tests are null, present the third phase as a hypothesis, not a proposed phase-diagram region.

4. **Improve sample characterization, phase-diagram robustness and presentation.**
   - Provide resistivity/susceptibility on the same batch to confirm TDW.
   - Quantify oxygen content (TGA, refined O occupancies).
   - State a quantitative upper bound on DW-satellite intensity, compared with Zhang et al.'s reported satellite strength.
   - Add intermediate-temperature high-pressure points (e.g., 450–800 K, or at least 150–250 K) and use an order-parameter-based criterion (superlattice intensity, tilt angle).
   - Replace the linear guide with a physically motivated boundary.
   - Discuss the discrepancy with the ~15 GPa room-temperature Pc in the literature.
   - Refit the EOS separately for each phase with stated K′ treatment, or explain the K0(T) variation.
   - Remove or soften the superconductivity conclusion unless supported by in-situ transport.
   - Fix Fig. 5, harmonize TDW values, move key refinement tables into the main text, and edit the language for precision ("we state", "horizon", overuse of "unequivocally").
